# What matters to people in South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal), as of October 2026

Scope note: research done 24 Oct 2026, with about 22 web searches and 40 page fetches. **The best survey data is heavily India-weighted.** Pew, Lokniti-CSDS, IHDS and the Global Flourishing Study all cover India only. For Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Nepal this pass found mainly economic, migration and political data, and little attitude-survey data. Each section flags this in its Gaps. "Old" marks data collected before 2022. Some sources are secondary summaries, such as exam-prep sites (Drishti IAS, CivilsDaily) and wire copy; they are labelled where used.

## 1. What people most want for their lives; what surveys show about priorities and life evaluation

### Takeaway
Life evaluation across South Asia sits in the bottom third of the world: in the World Happiness Report 2026, Nepal is the regional best at 99th of 147 and Afghanistan is last. Indian data shows that money worries, not a lack of meaning, drag down flourishing. India ranks 19th of 22 in the Global Flourishing Study when financial security is counted, but 12th of 22 without it. Indian youth name jobs, especially secure government jobs, as their top concern, and that concern grew sharply between 2016 and 2023.

### Cited Findings
**Life evaluation (World Happiness Report 2026, released 20 Mar 2026; Cantril ladder)**
- Ranks out of 147: Nepal 99th, Pakistan 104th, India 116th (up from 118th in 2025), Bangladesh 127th, Sri Lanka 134th, Afghanistan 147th (last). Scores were not given in the summary I could access — [Drishti IAS summary of WHR 2026](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually); Nepal "tops South Asia" despite a "modest decline" — [Nepal Traveller](https://nepaltraveller.com/news/nepal-tops-south-asia-in-world-happiness-index-2026-despite-global-slide)
- Nepal's regional lead is attributed to community support and resilience; the article also says social bonds matter alongside GDP. This is the outlet's interpretation, not the report's — [Nepal Traveller](https://nepaltraveller.com/news/nepal-tops-south-asia-in-world-happiness-index-2026-despite-global-slide)
- WHR 2026 on social media: light use (under 1 hour a day) goes with better well-being than no use. The global average has reached 2.5 hours a day, which falls in the report's "harmful" range. Passive visual platforms (Instagram, TikTok) are linked to social comparison. Communication platforms (WhatsApp, Facebook) are linked to higher life satisfaction in Latin America and the Middle East — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually)

**Global Flourishing Study, wave 1 (published 2025; India n = 12,765)**
- India's flourishing index *with* financial security: mean 6.87 (95% CI 6.82–6.91), **19th of 22** countries. Indonesia is highest at 8.10 and Japan lowest at 5.89 — [VanderWeele et al., GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- India's index *without* the financial-security items: mean 7.43, **12th of 22** — [GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- In India, married respondents report *lower* flourishing than single ones (−0.39; 95% CI −0.50 to −0.28). India is one of only two countries with this reversal; the other is Tanzania — [GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- India's age pattern is U-shaped: the young and the old flourish more than the middle-aged — [GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845); [PTI via The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/30/lst1-research-flourishing-india.html)
- Indian students report higher flourishing than employed people. Self-employment and retirement were also associated with more satisfaction than employment — [GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845); [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/30/lst1-research-flourishing-india.html)
- India has the smallest flourishing gap of any GFS country between weekly religious attenders and non-attenders (0.15; CI −0.01 to 0.30, not significant) — [GFS study profile, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- India's reported strengths: housing, government approvals, political voice, satisfaction with one's city. Weaknesses: education, "taking little interest in life", financial anxieties — [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/30/lst1-research-flourishing-india.html)

**Youth priorities (Lokniti-CSDS, India, ages 15–34, n = 9,316 in 18 states, reported Aug 2023)**
- 36% named unemployment as India's most critical problem, **up 18 points from 2016**. The figure is 40% among graduates and above, 42% among men and 31% among women. Poverty followed at 16% and inflation at 13% — [CivilsDaily summary of Lokniti-CSDS](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)
- 60% favour government jobs; over 25% lean toward entrepreneurship; only 2% are content to stay in their current job. Ideal sectors: health 16%, education 14%, science and technology 10% — [CivilsDaily](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)
- 49% of 15–34-year-olds were doing some form of work, and only 6% of those working held government jobs — [CivilsDaily](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)
- The earlier Lokniti-CSDS/KAS youth study (fieldwork Apr–May 2016, n = 6,122, 19 states; **old**) covered careers, marriage, lifestyle, anxieties and discrimination. I could not retrieve its numbers — [Lokniti](https://www.lokniti.org/content/Attitudes-anxieties-and-aspirations-of-India-s-youth-changing-patterns); [CSDS](https://www.csds.in/attitudes-anxieties-and-aspirations-india-s-youth)

**Work life (Gallup State of the Global Workplace 2025, 2024 data)**
- South Asia has the world's lowest share of "thriving" employees and the highest daily stress and anger among workers. In India, 30% of employees report daily stress and 34% regular anger. Engagement fell from 33% to 30%, and nearly half are looking for new jobs — [Gulf News on Gallup 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/half-of-indian-employees-want-to-quit-jobs-30-face-daily-stress-gallup-report-reveals-1.500114812)
- A 2024 report put India's "thriving" share at 14% — [WION headline](https://www.wionews.com/business-economy/only-14-of-indian-employees-feel-they-are-thriving-86-struggling-or-suffering-report-731486/amp) (headline only, not fetched). Gallup said in 2014 that "South Asia leads world in suffering" — [Gallup 2014, old](https://news.gallup.com/poll/166028/south-asia-leads-world-suffering.aspx) (headline only)

**Faith as a life priority (India; Pew; fieldwork Nov 2019–Mar 2020; old but the largest source)**
- 97% of Indians believe in God, and about 80% in most groups are absolutely certain. 77% of Hindus and 77% of Muslims believe in karma — [Pew key findings, 2021](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)

### Inferences
- The craving pattern for India, which probably extends to the rest of the region, is **security first**: a secure job (ideally government), money that covers the family, and safety from shocks. The GFS gap (19th with finances, 12th without) suggests people do *not* lack meaning, relationships or character. The pain is material precarity. App questions about meaning can assume plenty of it is there. Questions about money worry should be expected to land hard.
- The Indian GFS finding that married people flourish less than single people is unusual. Marriage may bring obligations, in-law dynamics and financial load as well as support. Even if marriage is the "expected" good, the app should not assume it is a source of well-being.
- Weekly worship adds little measurable flourishing in India, perhaps because religion is near-universal and woven into daily life rather than tied to attendance. The app should treat faith as a background presence (prayer, festivals, karma language), not an activity to track.
- Gallup's high daily anger and stress at work suggest that "a hard moment at work today" is a very common daily-question hook.

### Gaps
- I could not get WHR 2026 scores, or the report's own theme or chapter findings on South Asia; only ranks came from secondary summaries.
- I found no GFS results for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka or Nepal. India and Indonesia are the only South/Southeast Asian countries in wave 1, as far as I saw. I did not confirm India's domain-level scores; the GFS Table 9 row for India was truncated.
- I fetched no World Values Survey wave 7 data for South Asia (India, Pakistan and Bangladesh took part). Pew attitude data for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Nepal was not found in this pass.
- Lokniti-CSDS 2023 fieldwork dates and its non-employment findings (marriage, religion, mental health) were not available in the summary I fetched.

## 2. Social life: family, duty to parents, marriage, in-laws, gender roles, "what will people say", religion; handling caste and community without asking

### Takeaway
Family decides most marriages, and religious and caste endogamy is strongly endorsed. Traditional gender norms are held by large majorities of both women and men in India. They are measurably weaker in the South, among the college-educated, and in the North-East. Caste is socially pervasive: 70% of Indians say their close friends are mostly or all from their own caste. So the app must leave room for caste and community without ever naming or asking about them.

### Cited Findings
**Marriage**
- IHDS 2011-12 (**old**): about 5% of Indian women chose their spouse with no family input, and 95% had family involvement. 55% had some say, rising to 77% of college-educated women. Nearly 65% met their husband only at or around the wedding. Self-chosen marriage is about 34% in the North-East, just under 10% in the South, and "hardly seen" in the North — [NCAER NDIC](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/)
- 79% of women with no education had no contact with their husband before marriage, against 30% of college-educated women — [NCAER NDIC](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/)
- Pew (fieldwork 2019–20): 64% of Indians say it is very important to stop women from marrying outside their caste (62% for men). Among Hindus, 67% say the same for marrying outside the religion; among Muslims, 80% — [Pew key findings](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)
- 40% of Indians prefer a marriage where the husband earns and the wife keeps the home, against a 23% median across 34 countries — [Pew, gender roles 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)

**Dowry**
- In rural India, dowry was paid in **95% of marriages, 1960–2008** (REDS, about 40,000 marriages; **old**). The average net dowry was about Rs 27,000 in real 2005 rupees: the bride's family gave about Rs 32,000 and the groom's about Rs 5,000. Dowry often equals several years of household income — [World Bank blog, Anukriti, Prakash & Kwon, 2021](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)
- Dowries are highest among upper castes, then OBCs, SCs and STs. Kerala has the highest recent average and persistent inflation. Dowries rose sharply among Christians and Sikhs; among Muslims they were slightly lower and stable — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)

**Gender roles (Pew, India, n = 29,999, fieldwork 2019–20)**
- About 9 in 10 agree "a wife must always obey her husband", and nearly two-thirds completely agree (61% of women, 67% of men) — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- 80% say men should have more right to jobs when jobs are scarce, against a 17% global median for "completely agree" — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- Changing norms: 62% say both parents should be responsible for childcare and 54% that both should earn. Nearly three-quarters say both should make financial decisions. 72% say equal rights are very important, close to the 70% global median — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- 94% say having at least one son is very important, and 90% say the same of a daughter. 64% support equal inheritance. 58% say sons and daughters should share care of ageing parents, but about 4 in 10 say mainly sons and 2% mainly daughters. 63% say sons should mainly perform last rites — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- Variation: agreement that a wife must obey is 75% in the South and 94% in the Hindi Belt, and 80% among the college-educated against 88% among others. Saying sons should do last rites ranges from 81% in Karnataka to 30% in Kerala — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- NFHS trend: the share of husbands mainly deciding how a wife's earnings are used fell from 31% (1998–99) to 15% (2005–06) (**old**) — [Pew 2022 citing NFHS](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- 75% say violence against women is a "very big problem". In the past 12 months, 16% of women felt discriminated against for being women — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- Female labour force participation was 21% in India at the time, against a 53% global median (Pew citing ILO-type data). Sri Lanka's was 32.4% in 2025, "among the lowest in the region" — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/); [Daily FT on World Bank, Oct 2026](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Poverty-declining-but-still-above-pre-crisis-levels-World-Bank/44-798344)

**Caste, community, religion in daily life (Pew, India)**
- 70% say most or all of their close friends share their caste. 86% of Hindus, 80% of Sikhs and 72% of Jains say their close friends are mainly from their own religion — [Pew key findings](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)
- Most Indians, including most lower-caste Indians, say there is not "a lot" of caste discrimination. Yet in the past 12 months, 14% of women and 15% of men personally felt discriminated against because of caste, and 16–17% because of religion — [Pew key findings](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/); [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- 84% say respecting all religions is very important to being "truly Indian" — [Pew key findings](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)

**"What will people say"**
- The phrase appears in Pakistani public discourse as a recognised source of psychological strain — [APP headline: "The psychological toll of 'Log Kya Kahen Gy'"](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1238965) (headline only, not fetched)
- An older review of developing Asian countries (2007, **old**) found that families with a mentally ill member face social disapproval, "especially regarding marriage". It called stigma from family members "pervasive" — [Lauber & Rössler 2007](https://sonar.ch/global/documents/32237)

### Inferences
- **Marriage is a family project, not a personal milestone.** Expect "proposal", "rishta", "seeing a match", "family meeting" and "bio-data" moments. Choice and family involvement are increasingly blended (55% had some input). Romance-only framing ("your partner", "dating") misses most married adults, especially rural, less-educated and northern ones.
- **Duty to parents is gendered and lifelong.** Sons are expected to provide care and perform last rites. Daughters often care in practice, but that care is less socially recognised (inferred from the 58%-shared versus 2%-mainly-daughters split). An insight card that names invisible care work by daughters and daughters-in-law could ring true.
- **Caste and community handling.** Never ask, never infer from surname, language or location, and never label. But caste-linked experiences are real and common: 70% have mostly same-caste friends, around 15% felt caste discrimination in a year, and a majority oppose inter-caste marriage. Users will raise them in their own words. The app should mirror their words back without classifying ("you felt treated as less because of where your family comes from"). Keep generic, user-defined language such as "your community" or "people like you" available, and never store or derive caste or religion as an attribute. Questions about love across family lines are emotionally loaded and can carry real risk. Never nudge someone to "just tell your family".
- **Gender change is real but uneven**: Pew shows majorities endorsing both shared roles *and* wifely obedience at the same time. The app should not take sides or prescribe. It can reflect the tension ("you're doing what's expected and also want something else").
- The "what will people say" weight is likely central across Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, though I found only weak sourcing. It is a strong candidate for gentle reflection prompts that never mock or dismiss it.

### Gaps
- No recent nationally representative figures on arranged versus love marriage after IHDS 2011-12. IHDS-3 (2022–24) results were not found in this pass.
- No comparable family, marriage or gender attitude data was found for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka or Nepal: consanguineous marriage in Pakistan, child marriage in Bangladesh and Nepal, *biradari* (kinship group) in Pakistan, caste in Nepal and Sri Lanka. These are known topics but unsourced here.
- No peer-reviewed measure of "log kya kahenge" or izzat (honour) was found; the evidence is anecdotal and media-based.
- No recent dowry prevalence data after 2008, and no data on dowry costs for Pakistan or Bangladesh (where the term *jahez* is used).
- Honour-based violence related to inter-caste or inter-faith relationships: not researched in this pass.

## 3. Economic life: exams, graduate unemployment, informal and gig work, Gulf migration, remittances, UPI, debt, dowry costs

### Takeaway
Educated young people are the face of unemployment: in India, two-thirds of unemployed youth have secondary education or more. Nearly 90% of Indian work is informal. Gulf and Malaysian labour migration and record remittances underpin household economies in Pakistan, Bangladesh and especially Nepal. Sri Lanka is still climbing out of its 2022 crash. Digital payments are now everyday infrastructure in India: about 24.5 billion UPI transactions a month by August 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Jobs and education (India)**
- Youth make up 83% of India's unemployed. Unemployed youth with secondary education or more rose from 35.2% (2000) to 65.7% (2022). The youth unemployment rate went from 5.7% (2000) to 17.5% (2019) and 12.4% (2022). Graduates have the highest youth unemployment rates — [People Matters on ILO-IHD India Employment Report 2024](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/over-80-of-indias-unemployed-are-youth-ilo-40747)
- Nearly 90% of the workforce is informal. Regular wages were stagnant or falling from 2012 to 2022, and self-employed earnings fell after 2019. 7–8 million young people join the workforce each year — [People Matters / ILO-IHD 2024](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/over-80-of-indias-unemployed-are-youth-ilo-40747)
- In 2022, 48.4% of young women were not in employment, education or training (NEET), against 9.8% of young men — [People Matters / ILO-IHD 2024](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/over-80-of-indias-unemployed-are-youth-ilo-40747)
- 60% of youth favour government jobs (Lokniti-CSDS 2023) — [CivilsDaily](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)

**Exam pressure and student distress (India)**
- NCRB 2024: **14,488 student suicides**, 8.5% of all suicides, up about 4.3% from 2023 (article dated 11 May 2026) — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/education/rising-student-deaths-by-suicide-what-the-2024-ncrb-report-shows). Exam failure and family problems are reported as top causes over 2014–2024 — [The Wire headline](https://thewire.in/education/over-1-23-lakh-students-died-by-suicide-from-2014-2024-exam-failure-family-problems-top-causes-ncrb) (headline only)
- NCRB 2023: 171,418 suicides (12.3 per 100,000). 10,785 were under 18, 24,048 housewives, 14,234 unemployed people and 10,786 farmers. Daily-wage workers made up 28%, and 66% earned ₹1 lakh or less a year. Student suicides rose 65% over the decade. Nearly half were attributed to "family issues and illness" — [CMHLP policy brief, Mar 2026](https://cmhlp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Suicides-in-2023_Policy-Brief_Mar-2026.pdf)
- Data caveat: NCRB assigns one cause per death, releases late (2023 data appeared in Sept 2025), and varies by state — [CMHLP](https://cmhlp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Suicides-in-2023_Policy-Brief_Mar-2026.pdf)

**Migration and remittances**
- India received about $129bn in 2024 (World Bank estimate), the largest in the world. Pakistan received about $33bn. South Asian remittances were expected to grow 11.8% in 2024 — [Nukta on World Bank, Dec 2024](https://nukta.com/pakistan-india-among-world-s-top-recipient-of-remittances-in-2024)
- Remittances were about 8% of GDP for Pakistan and for Sri Lanka in 2023. GCC (Gulf state) outflows are the main swing factor — [Business Recorder on World Bank Migration & Development Brief 40, Jun 2024](https://www.brecorder.com/feeds/news/40310119)
- **Pakistan** FY25 (Jul 2024–Jun 2025): a record **$38.3bn**, up 27%. Saudi Arabia sent $9.35bn, the UAE $7.87bn, the UK $5.91bn and the US $3.72bn (State Bank of Pakistan) — [Profit / Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=204436)
- **Bangladesh** FY2025-26: **$35.59bn**, up from $30.33bn in FY2024-25. Saudi Arabia sent $5.85bn, the UK $5.07bn, the UAE $4.58bn and Malaysia $3.40bn, while US inflows fell 35.9%. Figures are from a minister's parliamentary reply on 4 Sept 2026 — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=298820)
- **Nepal** FY2025/26: remittances of Rs 2.363 trillion, given as **35.8% of GDP** (citing Nepal Rastra Bank). There were 792,187 labour approvals, down from a record 839,266 in 2024/25 (renewals included). Approvals went to the UAE (183,580), Malaysia (132,646), Qatar (132,051), Saudi Arabia (124,583) and Kuwait (43,360). Remittances pay for school fees, medical bills, rent, food and loans, acting as de facto social protection. Source: opinion column, 23 Sept 2026 — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/67358/). *Uncertainty:* 35.8% is higher than shares usually quoted for Nepal. It may reflect a different GDP base, and I did not cross-check it with NRB or World Bank data.

**Sri Lanka after the 2022 crisis (World Bank Sri Lanka Development Update, Oct 2026)**
- Poverty at $4.20 a day was 11.5% in 2019, peaked at 20.7% in 2023 and stood at 16.9% in 2025; it is projected at 15.8% in 2026. Real labour incomes fell more than 40% from 2019 to 2023, and real wages are still about 12% below 2019. Poverty is 36% in the estate (plantation) sector (2024). Child stunting rose from 7.4% (2021) to 10.1% (2025). Cyclone Ditwah and Middle East-linked energy costs stalled the recovery in H1 2026, and transport costs rose about 21%. The World Bank says the figures are "not official statistics" — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Poverty-declining-but-still-above-pre-crisis-levels-World-Bank/44-798344)

**Digital money (India)**
- UPI reached a record 24.51bn transactions worth ₹29.82 lakh crore in August 2026, up 22% in volume year on year (NPCI via PTI) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/upi-transactions-near-record-level-of-298-lakh-cr-in-aug-volume-at-all-time-high-of-2451-bn). September 2026: 24.07bn, with daily transactions crossing 800m for the first time — [StartupTalky headline](https://startuptalky.com/india-upi-transactions-september-2026/) (headline only). UPI marked 10 years in August 2026 — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/08/upi-completes-10-years-clocks-nearly-13000-fold-rise-transaction-volume) (headline only)

**Dowry costs**: see Section 2. Rural India, 1960–2008: paid in 95% of marriages, often several years of household income — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)

### Inferences
- "Results day", "the competitive exam attempt", "waiting for a government recruitment result" and "another year preparing" are high-salience, high-risk moments for young Indians. Prompts around them need care and crisis routing (see Section 7).
- Graduate unemployment combined with the government-job preference produces a specific pain: being educated and waiting, often at home and financially dependent. "What did you do today?" can sting. Prompts should honour effort and waiting, not output.
- Remittance-receiving households (Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Kerala and other Indian states) live with separation: a spouse, son or father abroad, video calls, and money tied to fees, loans and house-building. Both sides, sender and receiver, are prompt-worthy, and a migrant worker in the Gulf or Malaysia is a likely user in their own right.
- Money talk is family talk. Salaries are often pooled or sent home, and wedding and dowry costs and EMIs (monthly loan instalments) are collective. Prompts that assume a solo personal budget will miss.
- UPI ubiquity in India means "a payment moment" (paying a vendor, splitting with family, sending to parents) is a natural, true-to-life micro-moment there, but not to the same degree in the other four countries.
- Sri Lanka's users are likely to carry crisis memory: the 2022 shortages and queues, incomes still below 2019, and the 2025 cyclone. Their economic anxieties differ from India's growth-economy anxieties.

### Gaps
- Gig and platform worker numbers, and recent PLFS 2025–26 unemployment figures for India, were not fetched.
- No youth unemployment or graduate data was found for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka or Nepal. Bangladesh's 2024 government-job-quota protests and the February 2026 election, and Pakistan's inflation and poverty, were not sourced in this pass.
- I found no data on household debt or microfinance stress (for example Bangladesh MFIs or India's Andhra and Karnataka microfinance crises), or on migration debt such as recruitment fees.
- No data on Gulf worker deaths or kafala-system conditions; the Annapurna piece only mentions that deaths and injuries continue.

## 4. Mindset: stress, stigma, duty versus personal wishes, spirituality and coping, self-improvement

### Takeaway
Indians tend to see interpersonal obligations as role-based duty rather than personal choice. They report satisfaction from meeting expectations as well as from acting autonomously, so "duty versus self" is not experienced as a simple conflict. Stress is high, especially at work. Mental illness carries heavy family-level stigma, and distress is often expressed through bodily complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Americans tend to treat interpersonal morality as personal choice; Indians tend to see it as "role-related duty". Americans feel more satisfied acting autonomously than meeting social expectations, while **Indians report high satisfaction with both** — [Miller, Goyal & Wice 2017, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617706099/)
- Indian, American and Japanese people differ in how much everyday support follows communal norms (helping because of the relationship) rather than reciprocal exchange norms — [Miller et al. 2017](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617706099/)
- Indian moral-development research uses Shweder's "Big Three" ethics of Autonomy, Community and Divinity. This is inferred from the reference list; the chapter text was not accessible — [Kapadia & Bhangaokar 2015, Cambridge UP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/moral-development-in-a-global-world/an-indianmoral-worldview-developmental-patterns-in-adolescents-and-adults/705C4BF74DEFA00872239936A13633AF)
- South Asian workers report the highest daily stress and anger in the world. In India, 30% feel daily stress at work and 34% feel regular anger — [Gulf News on Gallup 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/half-of-indian-employees-want-to-quit-jobs-30-face-daily-stress-gallup-report-reveals-1.500114812)
- Stigma (**old**, 2007 review of developing Asian countries): stigma from family members is "pervasive" and harms marriage prospects. Supernatural, religious and magical explanations of mental illness prevail. Psychological symptoms are socially disadvantageous and bodily symptoms are not, so somatisation is "widespread". People are sceptical of formal services — [Lauber & Rössler 2007](https://sonar.ch/global/documents/32237)
- India's Tele-MANAS evaluation notes some callers were "too stigmatised" to visit a clinic and names "widespread stigma" as a driver of the treatment gap — [IMPRI, Jun 2026](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=72975)
- 77% of Hindus and Muslims in India believe in karma, and about 80% are absolutely certain God exists — [Pew 2021](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)
- GFS India weakness: "taking little interest in life" and financial anxieties. Promoting good declines with age in India — [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/30/lst1-research-flourishing-india.html); [GFS PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)

### Inferences
- **Avoid a "duty versus true self" frame.** Miller's work suggests that meeting expectations can be genuinely satisfying, not just compliance. Better prompts are "What did you do for someone today, and how did it feel?" and "Was there a moment today where what was expected and what you wanted were the same, or different?" Avoid "Are you living for yourself or for others?"
- **Somatic language is a doorway.** Users may describe distress as headaches, tiredness, "tension", "BP", sleep problems or "no mood". Daily check-ins could offer body-based options ("heavy", "tired", "tense") alongside emotion words.
- **Spiritual coping should be welcomed, not ignored or prescribed.** Prayer, puja, namaz, temple and dargah visits, fasting, karma and "God's will" are likely common coping frames across Hindu, Muslim, Buddhist, Sikh and Christian communities. The app can mirror them neutrally ("You found some peace in prayer today") without suggesting any practice or assuming a faith.
- **Self-improvement** is likely framed collectively: becoming capable in order to support family, clear the exam or get the job. Small steps that serve others, or that build steadiness for family, may resonate more than "self-care" framed as individual indulgence. This is unverified, based on the duty literature.
- Stigma is family-level and marriage-linked, so privacy is not a nicety. A mental-health-flavoured app found on a phone shared with family could cause harm. Neutral naming, discreet notifications and lock options matter (design inference).

### Gaps
- No recent peer-reviewed survey of mental-health stigma in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka or Nepal was found. The Lauber & Rössler review is 2007 and pan-Asian.
- Nothing on attitudes to self-help or self-improvement apps in South Asia, or on phone-sharing rates (especially women sharing phones).
- No sourced data on fatalism or locus of control, or on how "karma" thinking affects coping (it can comfort and can also blame).

## 5. Diversity within the region: where a region-wide assumption would go wrong

### Takeaway
"South Asia" covers five countries with different majority religions, economic trajectories and political moments. Within India alone, gender and marriage norms vary as much between regions, education levels and religions as between countries. A single "South Asian user" persona will be wrong for many users.

### Cited Findings
- **Life evaluation varies**: Nepal ranks 99th, Pakistan 104th, India 116th, Bangladesh 127th and Sri Lanka 134th of 147 — [Drishti IAS on WHR 2026](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually)
- **Regional splits within India**: agreement that a wife must always obey is 75% in the South and 94% in the Hindi Belt. Self-chosen marriage is about 34% in the North-East, just under 10% in the South and "hardly seen" in the North. Support for sons doing last rites ranges from 81% (Karnataka) to 30% (Kerala) — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/); [NCAER NDIC](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/)
- **Education splits**: 77% of college-educated women had input into their marriage, against 41% of women with no education (IHDS, **old**). 80% of college-educated Indians agree a wife must obey, against 88% of others — [NCAER NDIC](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/); [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- **Religious splits**: support for sons doing burial or last rites is 74% among Muslims and 29% among Sikhs. Dowry trajectories differ by religion and state — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/); [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)
- **Generational splits are smaller than expected in India**: age and gender gaps on gender-role attitudes are small. For example, 45% of men aged 35+ and 42% of men aged 18–34 say men should mainly earn — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- **Distinct national moments in 2025–26**:
  - Nepal: Gen Z protests in Sept 2025 against a social-media ban, corruption and "nepo kids". 21 were killed on 8 Sept per one source. Then came an interim PM (Sushila Karki), the 5 Mar 2026 election, an RSP landslide (125 of 165 first-past-the-post seats) and Balen Shah becoming PM on 27 Mar 2026, the first Madhesi PM. Gen Z leaders say core demands remain unmet — [Onlinekhabar, Sept 2026](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/gen-z-protest-what-changed-nepal.html); [Riskline](https://riskline.com/election/nepal-general-elections-2026/)
  - Sri Lanka: post-crisis recovery is incomplete. Poverty is 16.9%, two to three times higher outside the Western Province and 36% in the estate sector — [Daily FT/World Bank](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Poverty-declining-but-still-above-pre-crisis-levels-World-Bank/44-798344)
  - Remittance dependence varies widely: Nepal about 35.8% of GDP (uncertain), Pakistan and Sri Lanka about 8% (2023), and India the largest in absolute terms — [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/67358/); [Business Recorder/WB](https://www.brecorder.com/feeds/news/40310119); [Nukta/WB](https://nukta.com/pakistan-india-among-world-s-top-recipient-of-remittances-in-2024)
- **Gender gap in concerns**: 42% of young Indian men and 31% of young women named unemployment as the top issue. Young women's NEET rate is about five times men's — [CivilsDaily/Lokniti](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321); [People Matters/ILO-IHD](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/over-80-of-indias-unemployed-are-youth-ilo-40747)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not fetched in this pass; verify before use: majority religions differ (India and Nepal Hindu-majority, Pakistan and Bangladesh Muslim-majority, Sri Lanka Buddhist-majority, with large minorities everywhere). Languages number in the hundreds (India has 22 scheduled languages, Nepal 100+). Weekends differ (Bangladesh Friday–Saturday, Nepal Saturday-centred, India, Pakistan and Sri Lanka mostly Sunday). Festival calendars differ: Eid, Diwali, Durga Puja, Pohela Boishakh, Sinhala-Tamil New Year, Dashain-Tihar, Vesak.
- **Where region-wide assumptions break:**
  - "Arranged marriage everywhere": wrong for India's North-East and for urban, educated youth.
  - "Hindu idioms": wrong for most Pakistanis and Bangladeshis and for many Indians.
  - "Rising economy optimism": wrong for Sri Lanka and much of Pakistan.
  - "UPI moments": India-specific. Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nepal have their own mobile wallets; names and adoption were not researched here.
  - "Youth = passive": wrong after Nepal's 2025 and (unsourced here) Bangladesh's 2024 youth-led upheavals.
- Language matters more than ethnicity labels. The app can let users pick a language and write prompts that work in transliterated Hindi-Urdu (Hinglish/Roman Urdu), Bangla, Sinhala, Tamil and Nepali without assuming nationality from language. Tamil spans India and Sri Lanka, and Bangla spans India and Bangladesh.
- Urban/rural and class differences likely outweigh country differences for many prompts, for example phone sharing, privacy and literacy, but this was not sourced here.

### Gaps
- Urbanisation rates, literacy, smartphone and internet penetration by country, and language counts were not fetched.
- No attitude data on Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka or Nepal comparable to Pew India.
- Bangladesh's 2024 uprising and 2026 election and Pakistan's political and economic moment were not sourced.
- Bhutan, Afghanistan and the Maldives were out of scope; Afghanistan ranks last in WHR 2026 per the Drishti summary.

## 6. Everyday moments that ring true, and Western defaults to avoid

### Takeaway
The most resonant prompts sit at points where family, money, exams, marriage, faith and migration meet. Each moment below is anchored to the data above. The main Western defaults to drop are the solo, autonomous, romance-led, therapy-first self.

### Cited Findings (evidence anchoring each moment)
- Results days, competitive exams and family disappointment: student suicides were 14,488 in 2024, with exam failure and family problems among top causes — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/education/rising-student-deaths-by-suicide-what-the-2024-ncrb-report-shows); [The Wire headline](https://thewire.in/education/over-1-23-lakh-students-died-by-suicide-from-2014-2024-exam-failure-family-problems-top-causes-ncrb)
- Waiting for a government job: 60% of Indian youth favour them, but 6% of working youth hold one — [CivilsDaily/Lokniti](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)
- Marriage proposal discussions with family involvement: 95% of women's marriages involve family (IHDS 2011-12, old) — [NCAER NDIC](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/)
- Dowry and wedding cost pressure — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)
- Caring for ageing parents and last rites — [Pew 2022](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)
- Sending or receiving money from the Gulf or Malaysia — [Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=204436); [ANN](https://asianews.network/?p=298820); [Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/67358/)
- Daily stress and anger at work — [Gulf News/Gallup](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/half-of-indian-employees-want-to-quit-jobs-30-face-daily-stress-gallup-report-reveals-1.500114812)
- Social comparison through passive social media: WHR 2026 links Instagram and TikTok-style use to comparison — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually)
- Married Indians flourish less than single Indians: in-law and household load — [GFS PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- Rising costs in Sri Lanka (transport up about 21%, savings depleted) — [Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Poverty-declining-but-still-above-pre-crisis-levels-World-Bank/44-798344)

### Inferences
**Candidate daily moments.** These are drafts drawn from the evidence above; none were tested with users.
- "A relative asked about your marks, job or marriage at a function today. What did you feel, and what did you say (or not say)?"
- "Results came out. Who did you think of first?" (Route to support gently if distress is signalled.)
- "Someone at home brought up a proposal or a 'match'. What part of the conversation stayed with you?"
- "You sent money home, or money arrived from someone abroad. What did it mean to you this time?"
- "A video call with someone far away. What did you not get to say?"
- "Something you did today mainly because it was expected. How did it feel afterwards?" (Allow "good" as a valid answer, per Miller.)
- "A small thing you did for your parents, in-laws or elders today."
- "A moment you felt 'what will people think'. Whose voice was it?"
- "Prayer, a fast, a temple, mosque, church or gurdwara visit, or a quiet moment. Did anything shift?" (Optional, never assumed.)
- "Work or study felt heavy today. Where did you feel it in your body?"
- "A payment, a bill, an EMI or a loan instalment that weighed on you."
- "Waiting: for a result, a recruitment list, a visa, a call. How are you holding the wait?"
- "Festival preparations: the joy and the cost."
- Weekly mirror idea: "This week, your family appeared in most of your moments. Here's how they showed up: support, pressure, both." Mirror, never judge.

**Western defaults to avoid**
- "Set boundaries / cut off toxic family / move out". For many users, separation from family is neither possible nor desired. Better steps are small: "one sentence you could say", "one moment just for you inside the day".
- "Follow your passion / quit your job". With 90% informal work and family dependence, this is unrealistic. Frame steps as small and safe.
- "Your partner / dating / anniversary". This misses arranged and family-involved marriages. Use "spouse or someone you're close to" and allow "proposal" and "engagement" contexts.
- "Talk to a therapist" as the first step. Supply is scarce (0.3 psychiatrists per lakh in India) and stigma high. Offer layered help: trusted person, helpline, professional (see Section 7).
- "You deserve me-time / treat yourself". Spending on oneself may feel selfish where money is pooled. Prefer "steadiness for the people you care about, and for you".
- Assuming a private phone, private room or solo income.
- Assuming alcohol or nightlife as default socialising, or a Monday–Friday week.
- Treating religion as optional decor or as pathology. Equally, never assume a specific faith.
- "Compare your week to others / streaks vs friends". This is against app principles and also against WHR's social-comparison finding.

### Gaps
- No user research or diary study validating these moments in South Asian languages was found. All prompts above are inferences.
- I found no evidence on how South Asian users react to AI-generated insight cards or to "weekly mirror" formats.

## 7. Help-seeking and crisis support: who people turn to, helplines, respectful ways to point to help

### Takeaway
Family and faith tend to come first, formal care is scarce and stigmatised, and helplines exist in all five countries with very different coverage. India's Tele-MANAS (14416) is free, 24/7, in about 20 languages, and is the largest. Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Nepal rely on smaller or NGO lines, some with limited hours. Helpline numbers must be re-verified before shipping.

### Cited Findings
**Treatment gap and supply (India)**
- NMHS 2015-16 (**old**): nearly 11 in 100 Indians live with a mental disorder. About 150 million needed active intervention, but fewer than 30 million accessed care. There are about 0.3 psychiatrists per 100,000 people, against a WHO minimum of 3 — [IMPRI, Jun 2026](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=72975)

**Helplines (verify before use)**
- **India: Tele-MANAS 14416 or 1-800-891-4416.** It is free, 24/7, in 20 languages set by each state, and offers video consultations. Calls totalled 29.82 lakh (about 3 million) by Nov 2025, 12.33 lakh of them in 2025 — [IMPRI, Jun 2026](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=72975). There are 53 cells across 36 states and UTs, and 23.8 lakh calls had come in by mid-Mar 2025 (Rajya Sabha reply) — [Punjab Kesari/IANS, Jul 2025](https://english.punjabkesari.com/india/2025/07/27/tele-manas-mental-health-helpline-handles-over-238-lakh-calls-since-2022-prataprao-jadhav)
- India (NGO): 1Life +91 78930 98930 — [Intellect hotline list](https://intellect.co/mental-health-hotlines)
- **Pakistan: Umang 0311-7786264 (0311-77UMANG)**, described as a "24/7 free mental health helpline" — [Intellect hotline list](https://intellect.co/mental-health-hotlines)
- **Bangladesh: Kaan Pete Roi +880 9612-119911**, open daily 3 pm–3 am and staffed by trained volunteers offering "confidential, compassionate, and open-minded listening". The admin line +880 1713-048013 is not for crisis calls — [Kaan Pete Roi](https://kaanpeteroi.org/helpline-service/)
- **Sri Lanka: NIMH national mental health helpline 1926**, toll-free, launched in 2018. Hours and languages are not stated in the source — [Ada Derana, 2018](https://adaderana.lk/news/50744/nimh-introduces-mental-health-helpline-1926). An NIMH and Airtel chatline was also launched — [Airtel Lanka](https://www.airtel.lk/airtel-and-nimh-launches-sri-lankas-first-mental-health-chatline/) (headline only). The Sri Lanka Sumithrayo number shown on Intellect's list ("011 057 222 266 2") looks malformed and should not be used without verification — [Intellect](https://intellect.co/mental-health-hotlines)
- **Nepal: Mental Health Helpline Nepal 1660 0133666**, open daily 8 am–8 pm — [Intellect hotline list](https://intellect.co/mental-health-hotlines)

**Who people turn to**
- Stigma from family is pervasive. Supernatural and religious explanations of mental illness prevail, distress is often somatised, and formal services are viewed with scepticism (2007 review, **old**) — [Lauber & Rössler 2007](https://sonar.ch/global/documents/32237)
- Tele-MANAS reaches people who are "too stigmatised" to visit a clinic, but referral dropout remains a problem — [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/?p=72975)

### Inferences
- **Layered, low-stigma routing:** (1) "Is there someone you trust, a sibling, friend, elder, or a religious figure you respect, you could tell even a little?" (2) A free, confidential phone line, named by function ("a free, confidential listening line") before any clinical word. (3) A doctor or counsellor. Leading with "psychiatrist" or "therapy" may cause disengagement.
- **Don't diagnose; mirror somatic language.** If someone says "tension", "heavy head" or "no sleep for days", respond to the experience and offer help options without a label.
- **Check hours and language.** Bangladesh's Kaan Pete Roi is not 24/7, and Nepal's listed line is daytime only. The app should show hours and offer a fallback, such as emergency services or a trusted person. Emergency numbers were not verified here.
- **Shared-phone safety**: crisis screens should be discreet and dismissible. Avoid persistent notifications naming mental health.
- **Respectful phrasing**: honour the family and faith context ("Many people find strength in family or faith, and it's also okay to talk to someone trained to listen"). Never set professional help against religion or family.
- **Exam and results moments** carry high risk. The app could proactively show a soft support card around known national results periods (India's board results, NEET and JEE). Results calendars were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No primary or recent peer-reviewed source was found on first points of contact (family, faith healers, general physicians) in each of the five countries. The evidence is a 2007 pan-Asian review plus inference.
- NMHS-2 (India's second National Mental Health Survey) status and results were not found.
- Pakistan's, Nepal's and Sri Lanka's helpline details come from a third-party aggregator (Intellect) or a 2018 news item and **must be verified with the operators**. National emergency numbers (India 112, and others) were not verified in this pass.
- No data on helpline use in Pakistan, Nepal or Sri Lanka, or on women's access to them.
