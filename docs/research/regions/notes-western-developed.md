# What matters to people in Western developed countries (Western/Northern/Southern Europe, UK, US, Canada, Australia, NZ), as of 2026

Scope note: compiled 24 Oct 2026 from 23 searches and 37 page fetches. Data years are given for each item. Where only a secondary report of a primary study could be reached, this is said. Each "Cited Findings" bullet is sourced. "Inferences" are the researcher's reasoning and are not facts. "Gaps" lists what could not be verified.

## 1. What do people most want for their lives, and what do the big surveys show (WHR 2025–2026, Global Flourishing Study, Gallup, Pew, WVS, OECD)?

### Takeaway
The region is materially secure but short on meaning and connection. Rich Western countries score high on financial security and life evaluation, and comparatively low on meaning, close relationships and prosocial character (Global Flourishing Study, 2023 data). The sharpest signal for 2025–2026 is young adults. In the English-speaking countries the old "young people are happiest" pattern has reversed: under-25 life evaluations fell almost a full point over a decade while young people elsewhere grew happier. Connection (shared meals, someone to count on, belief that others are kind) predicts wellbeing more strongly than income.

### Cited Findings
**World Happiness Report 2026 (published 19 Mar 2026; Gallup World Poll data)**
- Finland ranked 1st for the 9th straight year (score 7.764/10). Switzerland 10th, New Zealand 11th, Ireland 13th, Australia 15th, US 23rd, Canada 25th, UK 29th. — [Oxford University news, WHR 2026](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness); [WHR 2026 press release (Gallup PDF)](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/703274/WHR_2026_Press%20Release.pdf)
- No English-speaking country is in the top 10, and only half are in the top 20. Oxford calls this the "second year running". The press-release summary I received said "first time since 2012". These readings conflict, though the second may be a summarisation error. WHR 2025's top 10 (below) also had no English-speaking country, which fits "second year running". — [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness); [WHR 2026 press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/703274/WHR_2026_Press%20Release.pdf)
- Under-25 life evaluations in the US, Canada, Australia and New Zealand fell "by almost one point on a 0–10 scale" over the past decade, while the average for young people in the rest of the world rose. Wellbeing also fell among young people in Western Europe, "especially girls" (no figure given). — [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)
- Gallup's CEO: most of the world's young people are happier today than 20 years ago. The largest youth declines are in the English-speaking countries. — [WHR 2026 press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/703274/WHR_2026_Press%20Release.pdf)
- "Social connections and a sense of belonging" are associated with much larger changes in life evaluations than social media use is. — [WHR 2026 press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/703274/WHR_2026_Press%20Release.pdf)

**World Happiness Report 2025 (Mar 2025; data 2022–2024; theme: caring and sharing)**
- The US fell to 24th, its lowest since the report began in 2012 (best was 11th). The US was one of the few countries where "deaths of despair" (suicide, substance abuse) rose. Top four: Finland, Denmark, Iceland, Sweden. — [Khaleej Times/AFP on WHR 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)
- The authors say generosity and belief in others' kindness are "significant predictors of happiness, even more so than earning a higher salary". People "are too pessimistic about the kindness of their communities". Lost wallets are returned at a much higher rate than people expect, and the Nordics rank top for both expected and actual returns. — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)

**Global Flourishing Study, wave 1 (data mainly 2023, published Apr 2025; ~207,000 people in 22 countries plus Hong Kong; Western countries covered: US, UK, Germany, Spain, Sweden, Poland, Australia)**
- Top: Indonesia (8.47/10), then Mexico and the Philippines. Median 7.45. US 7.18, 15th. Japan lowest. — [Yahoo News report on GFS](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)
- The UK is among the bottom three, with Turkey and Japan. Sweden, 4th in the WHR, sits mid-table on flourishing. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f)
- High-income countries score higher on financial security and life evaluation. Middle-income countries score higher on meaning and purpose, prosocial character and close relationships. Composite flourishing fell slightly as GDP per capita rose. — [Harvard T.H. Chan School, "Measuring a life well lived"](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- VanderWeele (Harvard) on rich countries: "they don't report as high on meaning, on relationships, on pro-social character". — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f)
- Pooled age pattern: flourishing is flat from 18 to 49 and rises each decade from 50; people aged 80+ score highest. The US, UK and Australia no longer show the traditional U-shape. — [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- US: ages 18–29 scored 5.68 and ages 60–69 scored 8.06, the widest age gap of any country in the study. Youth mental health lags most in Brazil, Sweden and the US. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)
- Married people score significantly higher than single, separated or divorced people. Flourishing rises with religious service attendance "even in the most secular countries". Men and women score similarly. — [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- In Australia, less-educated people were *more* likely to flourish, reversing the usual education gradient. — [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f)

**Gallup State of the Global Workplace 2026 (fieldwork Jan–Dec 2025)**
- Share "thriving" in life evaluation: Australia/NZ 55%, US/Canada 51%, Europe 49%, global 34%. — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx)
- Daily stress ("a lot of the day yesterday"): US/Canada 50%, the highest of any region; global 40%. Global daily loneliness is 22%. — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx)

### Inferences
- The craving the data points to most consistently is meaning and belonging, not more security. The app's daily questions about connection, purpose and small kindnesses fit what this region lacks relative to its wealth.
- The "too pessimistic about others' kindness" finding suggests one useful insight-card theme: noticing kindness people actually received, which pushes against a felt sense that others don't care.
- Young adults (roughly 18–29) in the US, UK, Canada, Australia and NZ are a distinct audience with lower baseline wellbeing than their parents. The app should not assume youth equals optimism.

### Gaps
- World Values Survey (Inglehart–Welzel map, self-expression/secular values) and the OECD Better Life Index / How's Life were not fetched within budget. No WVS-sourced claims are included.
- GFS overall scores or ranks for Germany, Spain and Poland were not found.
- WHR 2026 full rankings for the other Nordic, Western European and Southern European countries were not retrieved; only Finland, Switzerland and the English-speaking countries are given above.

## 2. Social life: loneliness, living alone, smaller/later families, friendship, dating apps, secularisation, polarisation, care for ageing parents

### Takeaway
People are seeing each other in person less, especially young people and men. Loneliness is concentrated among under-50s (US) and among the poor and unemployed (OECD). Households are shrinking, births are at record lows, and a large and growing share of adults care for an ageing relative while still working or raising children. Religion has declined, but in the US the decline has plateaued and religious practice is still common. Young men and young women are diverging in values, which raises polarisation risk within close relationships, not only between parties.

### Cited Findings
**Loneliness and friendship**
- US (Pew, survey 3–15 Sep 2024, n=6,204, published 16 Jan 2025): 16% feel lonely or isolated "all or most of the time"; 22% of adults under 50 vs 9% of those 50+. 18% have no close friends. Men and women are about equally likely to have at least one close friend. — [Pew Research Center, "Men, Women and Social Connections"](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- US contact with close friends: texting 61% a few times a week or more, social media 39%, phone/video 35%, in person only 29%. Among under-30s, 72% text and 60% use social media with close friends at least a few times a week. — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- US Surgeon General advisory (2023; older data): about half of US adults reported measurable loneliness even before COVID. Prolonged loneliness was likened to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day and linked to a 26% higher risk of premature death. In 2021, 49% of adults reported three or fewer friends, up from ~27% in 1990. In-person time with friends for ages 15–24 fell from ~150 minutes/day (2003) to ~40 minutes/day (2020), a drop of almost 70%. — [Smithsonian Magazine on the advisory](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/an-epidemic-of-loneliness-threatenes-health-of-americans-surgeon-general-says-180982142/)
- OECD *Social Connections and Loneliness in OECD Countries* (Oct 2025): people meet in person less often than in the past. "Men and young people – groups previously considered at lower risk – have seen some of the largest deteriorations." Disconnection overlaps with socio-economic disadvantage, living alone and older age. — [OECD executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/social-connections-and-loneliness-in-oecd-countries_6df2d6a0-en/full-report/executive-summary_7f72ab4a.html)
- OECD (as reported): the share of 16–24-year-olds with daily contact with friends fell from 44% (2015) to 36% (2022), after a 9-point drop between 2006 and 2015. Among people 65+, the share who never meet friends rose 5.5 points to 11.4% (2015–2022). Unemployed and lowest-income people are about twice as likely to feel lonely. Men saw larger falls in loneliness and relationship-quality measures than women (2018–2022). — [Yahoo News report on OECD](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/why-loneliness-rise-among-young-101120350.html)
- Europe (OECD, as reported): 8% across 22 EU countries say they have no close friends, and 3% no close family. France had the highest share lonely "most/all of the time" in 2022 (11%). Greece and Slovenia have the highest daily contact with friends or family. Daily face-to-face contact fell across 2006, 2015 and 2022 in 21 European OECD countries. — [Euronews](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2025/11/10/nearly-10-of-europeans-say-they-have-no-close-friends-where-do-people-feel-loneliest)

**Eating alone (a concrete marker of social life)**
- WHR 2025: in 2023, "roughly one in four Americans reported eating all their meals alone the previous day", up 53% over two decades. The authors call eating alone "one reason for declining well-being in the United States." — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)
- US 18–24-year-olds are 90% more likely to eat every meal alone on a given day than in 2003 (American Time Use Survey). Americans who share at least one meal report higher happiness and lower stress, pain and sadness that day. In North America and Australia/NZ, going from always eating alone to sharing most meals (8+ a week) is associated with a wellbeing gain comparable to doubling income. The link is half as large in Latin America and sub-Saharan Africa. — [The Conversation, WHR 2025 authors](https://theconversation.com/why-sharing-meals-can-make-people-happier-what-evidence-from-142-countries-shows-252352)

**Living alone and leaving home**
- About one in three EU households is single-occupancy. Germany has the largest share of its population living alone. Among people of retirement age, 42% of women live alone vs 24% of men. — [Eurofound press release](https://eurofound.mynewsdesk.com/news/one-in-three-households-in-eu-are-now-single-occupancy-394453)
- OECD: about 31% of people 65+ live alone on average, from 54% in Lithuania to 11% in Slovakia. More than 75% of 20–29-year-olds live with parents in Italy and Spain. Young people are "much less likely" to live with parents in the Nordics. Living with a partner at 20–29 reaches 42% in Finland. (Data 2021–2024 by country.) — [OECD Affordable Housing Database HM1.4](https://webfs.oecd.org/Els-com/Affordable_Housing_Database/HM1-4-Living-arrangements-age-groups.pdf)
- Eurostat 2024: the average age of leaving the parental home is 26.2 in the EU, from 21.4 (Finland), 21.7 (Denmark) and 21.9 (Sweden) to 30.0 (Spain), 30.1 (Italy), 30.7 (Greece), 30.9 (Slovakia) and 31.3 (Croatia). — [Eurostat news, 23 Sep 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250923-1)

**Smaller and later families**
- England & Wales total fertility rate: 1.39 in 2025 (provisional, a record low), down from 1.41 in 2024. Mothers' standardised mean age is 31.1 (26.4 in 1975), fathers' 34. A fertility clinician cites career and financial priorities, not having met a suitable partner, and not feeling ready. — [EMJ Reviews on ONS Births 2025](https://www.emjreviews.com/general-healthcare/news/birth-rate-in-england-and-wales-falls-to-record-low/)

**Dating apps**
- UK (Ofcom data, reported Aug 2025): since 2023, Tinder lost almost 600,000 UK users, Bumble 368,000, Hinge 131,000 and Grindr 11,000. Ofcom says total adult usage held steady, with the fall apparently driven by Gen Z. — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/gen-z-gender-split-is-reshaping-british-politics/)

**Secularisation and meaning**
- US Religious Landscape Study (Jul 2023–Mar 2024): about 62% identify as Christian (78% in 2007, 71% in 2014). 29% are unaffiliated (5% atheist, 6% agnostic, 19% "nothing in particular"). The decline has slowed or plateaued. About 44% pray daily and ~33% attend services at least monthly, both steady. Youngest adults (born 2000–2006): 46% Christian, 27% pray daily, 25% attend monthly, versus 80%, 58% and 49% for the oldest. The youngest cohort is no less religious than people born in the 1990s. "Large majorities of Americans have a spiritual or supernatural outlook." — [Pew Trust magazine, Summer 2025](https://www.pew.org/en/trust/archive/summer-2025/the-decline-of-christianity-has-slowed)
- GFS: nearly a third of respondents across the 22 countries attend services weekly. Attendance is consistently linked to higher wellbeing. — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)

**Polarisation, including the young men / young women divergence**
- Ipsos and KCL Global Institute for Women's Leadership (~24,000 respondents, 30 countries): 57% of Gen Z men vs 36% of Gen Z women say efforts to promote women's equality have gone so far that men are now discriminated against. — [GZERO Media](https://www.gzeromedia.com/news/analysis/the-gen-z-gender-divide)
- UK: 53% of Gen Z women vs 32% of Gen Z men identify as feminists, the largest gap of any age group. Gen Z men are less optimistic about their future. Both sexes are more likely than older cohorts to perceive rising tension between men and women. — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/gen-z-gender-split-is-reshaping-british-politics/)
- US 2024 presidential vote (Catalist): women 18–29 voted 63% Harris; men 18–29 voted 46% Harris, with over half for Trump. This 17-point gap is the largest of any cohort across four elections. Germany 2025: 27% of men 18–24 voted AfD (far right) and 35% of women 18–24 voted Linke (far left). — [GZERO Media](https://www.gzeromedia.com/news/analysis/the-gen-z-gender-divide)
- European Gen Z (FES "EqualiZe", European Social Survey plus 17 focus groups in Germany, Greece, Poland, Spain and Sweden): gender gaps exist but "vary significantly across countries and issues". Voting gaps are smaller than public debate suggests. Young women frame shared pressures (living costs, housing) in terms of safety, autonomy and care; young men frame them in terms of opportunity, fairness, recognition and status. — [FES Youth Studies](https://youthstudies.fes.de/equalize.html)

**Care for ageing parents and relatives**
- US (AARP/NAC, Jul 2025): 63 million family caregivers, "nearly 1 in 4 adults", up 20 million since 2015. Nearly 1 in 4 provide 40+ hours a week. Nearly half have had a major financial impact (debt, unable to afford food), with roughly $7,200 a year out of pocket. 1 in 5 rate their own health fair or poor. Nearly 1 in 3 are also raising children under 18 (47% among caregivers under 50). 6 in 10 are employed, and half of those report work disruptions. Nearly 1 in 4 feel socially isolated. — [AARP press release](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)

### Inferences
- Loneliness in this region often coexists with constant digital contact; texting dominates and in-person contact is the minority mode (Pew). Questions should treat "a group chat going quiet" or "only texting this week" as real social events, not trivial ones.
- Living alone is not the same as being lonely, and it means different things by country. A Finnish 22-year-old in their own flat and a Spanish 29-year-old still at home are both normal in their context. The app should not frame either as a problem.
- Caregiving is a large and under-recognised identity (1 in 4 US adults). Many users will be quietly managing a parent's decline alongside work and children. "Sandwich" moments (a call from a parent's care home during a meeting) are likely to resonate.
- The gender divergence means a prompt about "the news", "politics" or "men and women" can land very differently by user. The app's rule against asking party affiliation is well-founded. It should also avoid prompts that presuppose a side in gender debates.

### Gaps
- No primary OECD figure for the share feeling lonely (OECD average) was retrieved; the full report pages fetched contained no numbers.
- No reliable 2025–2026 figures were found for dating-app use share (US/EU), the "quiet revival" debate on UK church attendance, or religiosity in Nordic/Western Europe.
- Fertility figures for the US, Nordics, Southern Europe, Canada and Australia for 2025 were not retrieved (only England & Wales).
- Caregiving data outside the US (UK, EU, Australia) was not retrieved.
- Affective polarisation (dislike of the other side, family estrangement over politics) beyond the gender lens was not researched within budget.

## 3. Economic life: housing and cost of living, burnout and work identity, job insecurity and AI worries, student debt, the generational gap

### Takeaway
Housing costs are the central economic stressor. In the US half of renters are cost-burdened (record high, 2023 data). Across Southern and parts of Eastern Europe young adults stay with their parents until about 30. Work is a paradox. North Americans are relatively engaged but the most stressed in the world, while Europeans are the least engaged region but report less stress. UK data show near-universal pressure and money worries outside work. About half of US workers are worried about AI's effect on their jobs.

### Cited Findings
**Housing and cost of living**
- US (Harvard JCHS, *State of the Nation's Housing 2025*, released 24 Jun 2025): 22.6 million renter households (50%) were cost-burdened in 2023, a record for the third straight year. 12.1 million (27%) were severely burdened, spending over half their income on housing. 20.3 million homeowners (24%) were cost-burdened. The median existing single-family home price in 2024 was $412,500, about 5x median household income (3x is "traditionally affordable"). Home insurance premiums rose 57% between 2019 and 2024. Homelessness in Jan 2024 was 771,480, up 33% since Jan 2020. — [JCHS press release](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/press-releases/new-report-highlights-unease-housing-market-amid-worsening-affordability-crisis)
- EU leaving-home ages (2024) range from about 21–22 in the Nordics to 30–31 in Spain, Italy, Greece, Slovakia and Croatia (see section 2). — [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250923-1)
- UK: 51% of 18–24-year-old workers took on extra paid hours or a second job because of rising living costs. Money worries outside work were cited by 48% of workers as a stress driver. (YouGov fieldwork 3–5 Nov 2025, n=4,502.) — [Mental Health UK, Burnout Report 2026](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)

**Burnout, stress and work identity**
- Gallup (2025 data): global engagement is 20%. US/Canada 31%, Australia/NZ 21%, Europe 12% (lowest region for the sixth consecutive year). US/Canada daily stress is 50%, the highest region. Only 47% in US/Canada say it's a good time to find a job (global 52%). — [Gallup SOGW 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx); [Romania Insider on SOGW 2026](https://www.romania-insider.com/romania-top-european-countries-employee-engagement-apr-2026)
- Europeans report "less stress" than US and Canadian employees despite lower engagement. — [Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/romania-top-european-countries-employee-engagement-apr-2026)
- UK (Mental Health UK 2026): 91% of adults reported high or extreme pressure or stress in the past year (34% "always/often"). One in five workers needed time off for poor mental health caused by stress (21% in 2025). Among 18–24s, 93% reported high or extreme stress and 39% took time off. Drivers: poor sleep 59%, money worries 48%, heavy workload 42%, regular unpaid overtime 33%, fear of redundancy or job insecurity 32%. — [Mental Health UK](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)

**AI and job insecurity**
- US workers (Pew, fieldwork Oct 2024, published Feb 2025; older data): 52% worried about future AI use in the workplace, 36% hopeful, 33% overwhelmed. 32% expect fewer job opportunities for themselves long term. 63% use AI little or not at all at work, and ~10% use chatbots daily or a few times a week. AI use is higher among young and degree-holding workers. — [InfoDocket summary of Pew report](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/02/25/new-report-from-pew-research-u-s-workers-are-more-worried-than-hopeful-about-future-ai-use-in-the-workplace/)
- Mental Health UK says AI may ease routine work but could also increase job-security anxiety. It gives no figure. — [Mental Health UK](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)

**Generational gap**
- US flourishing: 5.68 at ages 18–29 vs 8.06 at 60–69 (2023 data). — [Yahoo/GFS](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)
- FES: European Gen Z share "common insecurity", including housing pressure, unstable labour markets, climate anxiety and declining trust in institutions. — [FES Youth Studies](https://youthstudies.fes.de/equalize.html)

### Inferences
- A rent rise, a lease renewal, a second job, or the "I'm 29 and still in my childhood bedroom" feeling are likely to be widely recognised moments. Their emotional meaning differs, though: shame or failure in Anglo/Nordic norms versus ordinary practice in Southern Europe.
- The US/Canada combination of higher engagement and the world's highest daily stress suggests that work identity there is strong and also costly. In much of Europe work may matter less to identity. Questions shouldn't assume work is someone's main source of meaning.
- "Sunday dread" before work is plausible given the UK and US stress data, but no survey measuring it was retrieved (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 data retrieved on: student debt (US totals, UK Plan 2 repayments), housing affordability in the UK, Canada, Australia or NZ specifically, inflation or cost-of-living sentiment in Europe, or a "Sunday scaries" survey.
- Gallup country-level engagement for individual European countries (e.g., UK, France, Italy, Nordics) was not available from the fetched pages.
- More recent (2025–2026) AI-worry surveys from Pew, Eurobarometer or other countries were not retrieved; the Pew figures are from Oct 2024.

## 4. Mindset: individualism and its costs, therapy language and self-optimisation culture, perfectionism, phones and social media, mental-health openness versus rising distress

### Takeaway
Pressure to be perfect, mostly felt as coming from others and from parents, has risen measurably among young people in the US, Canada and the UK. Distress among teenagers, especially girls and LGBQ+ youth, is high: four in ten US high-schoolers report persistent sadness (2023). The 2026 WHR finds light social media use (under one hour a day) is associated with the best youth wellbeing, while heavy, algorithm-driven use is associated with worse. Governments have begun to act; Australia's under-16 ban took effect on 10 Dec 2025. Openness to help is real but partial: about 1 in 5 Americans would likely turn to a mental-health professional for support. More than a third of people in a UK study had used AI chatbots for mental-health support.

### Cited Findings
**Perfectionism**
- Curran & Hill (2022, *Psychological Bulletin*; US, Canada, UK college students): across 84 studies (1989–2021, n=23,975), perceived parental expectations, criticism and combined pressure all increased. Perceived expectations were up ~40% since 1989. Expectations and criticism have a large association with socially prescribed perfectionism. Self-oriented perfectionism is higher among US than Canadian or British students. Curran & Hill (2019) found three types of perfectionism rising among young people in the same three countries. Hill: "the pressure to conform to perfect ideals has never been greater". — [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/03/parental-expectations-perfectionism)

**Youth distress**
- US YRBS 2023 (high-school students): persistent sadness or hopelessness 39.7% (female 52.6%, male 27.7%, LGBQ+ 65.7%). Poor mental health 28.5%. Seriously considered suicide 20.4% (female 27.1%, LGBQ+ 41.0%). Persistent sadness was over 42% in 2021, so 2023 is slightly lower. — [CDC MMWR Supplement 2024;73(4)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a9.htm)
- GFS: youth mental health lags most strongly in Brazil, Sweden and the US. — [Yahoo/GFS](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)

**Phones and social media**
- WHR 2026: young people using social media under one hour a day report the highest wellbeing, higher than non-users. Adolescents average ~2.5 hours a day (one estimate). PISA 2022 data for 15-year-olds in nearly 50 countries link heavy use to a significant average drop in wellbeing, varying by platform, type of use, gender and socio-economic status. Algorithmically curated platforms are negatively associated with wellbeing, and platforms designed for social connection positively. The largest youth declines are in English-speaking countries "despite similar levels of social media use elsewhere". The report says experts' readings of the evidence vary. — [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness); [WHR 2026 press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/703274/WHR_2026_Press%20Release.pdf)
- Australia's minimum-age law took effect at midnight on 10 Dec 2025. Platforms including Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Reddit, Snapchat, TikTok, Twitch, X and YouTube must take "reasonable steps" to keep under-16s off, with fines up to A$49.5m. Messaging, gaming and professional apps are exempt. Denmark proposed an under-15 ban in Oct 2025. Critics (Snap, Meta, youth groups) warn of displacement to less safe apps and of isolating teens. — [ABC7 / AP](https://abc7news.com/amp/post/australias-social-media-ban-kids-16-goes-effect/18269742/)

**Help-seeking openness, therapy and AI**
- US (Pew 2024): 19% would be extremely or very likely to turn to a mental-health professional for emotional support (24% of under-50s vs 14% of 50+; women 22%, men 16%). — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- UK: a 2025 Mental Health UK study found more than one in three people had used AI chatbots for mental-health support. — [Mental Health UK Burnout Report 2026](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)

**Individualism and its costs (indirect evidence)**
- Rich countries score lower on meaning, close relationships and prosocial character despite higher financial security (GFS). — [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- Belief in others' kindness predicts happiness more than salary. People underestimate community kindness (WHR 2025). — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)

### Inferences
- The app needs to avoid feeding perfectionism and self-optimisation. Streaks, "growth" framing and "be your best self" language risk reinforcing socially prescribed perfectionism, a well-documented pressure in exactly this population. The "one small optional step" design is well suited, as long as skipping it is framed as fine.
- An AI-driven self-reflection app enters a context where many people already use AI for emotional support (UK >1 in 3). This raises expectations and also the responsibility to signpost human help.
- Social media should not be framed as simply bad. The evidence favours "how and how much" (light, connection-oriented use) over abstinence, so questions about time on phones should be curious, not moralising.
- Users from English-speaking countries may arrive fluent in therapy vocabulary ("boundaries", "trauma", "toxic"). This is an inference; no source on therapy-speak prevalence was retrieved. Plain-language insight cards that don't diagnose fit the app's rules and avoid echoing clinical labels.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source was retrieved on "therapy speak" or self-optimisation culture (wearables, productivity, wellness spending) for 2025–2026.
- Youth mental-health prevalence for the UK (NHS England), EU (e.g., HBSC 2022) and Australia/NZ was not retrieved; only US YRBS figures are cited.
- Hofstede and WVS individualism measures were not retrieved.
- Evidence on outcomes of Australia's ban (2026) was not retrieved.

## 5. Diversity inside the group: US vs Nordic vs Southern Europe, class, rural/urban, declining regions — where a West-wide assumption goes wrong

### Takeaway
"The West" splits along at least four lines the app must respect. (1) English-speaking countries: sharpest youth wellbeing decline, highest stress (US), strongest perfectionism (US). (2) Nordics: highest happiness and trust, earliest independence, many people living alone without that meaning isolation. (3) Southern Europe: family co-residence to about 30, more daily contact with family and friends in places like Greece. (4) Class: loneliness roughly doubles among the poor and unemployed. Religion, the gender divide and education gradients also vary by country.

### Cited Findings
- **Youth decline is specific to English-speaking countries.** Under-25 life evaluations fell almost a point in the US, Canada, Australia and NZ while rising elsewhere. Western Europe also fell (especially girls), but no magnitude is given. — [Oxford/WHR 2026](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)
- **Nordics vs South on independence:** leaving home at ~21–22 (Finland, Denmark, Sweden) vs ~30–31 (Spain, Italy, Greece, Croatia, Slovakia). — [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250923-1). More than 75% of 20–29s live with parents in Italy and Spain; far fewer in the Nordics. — [OECD HM1.4](https://webfs.oecd.org/Els-com/Affordable_Housing_Database/HM1-4-Living-arrangements-age-groups.pdf)
- **Nordic trust:** top for expected and actual lost-wallet return. — [Khaleej Times/AFP on WHR 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)
- **Happiness vs flourishing:** Sweden is 4th in the WHR (2025) but mid-table in the GFS. Rich countries are high on security and low on meaning and relationships. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f); [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- **UK as a low-flourishing outlier:** among the GFS bottom three. 29th in WHR 2026. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f); [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)
- **US vs Europe at work:** US/Canada are more engaged (31% vs 12%) but more stressed (50% daily stress, the world's highest). — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx); [Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/romania-top-european-countries-employee-engagement-apr-2026)
- **Within Europe on contact and loneliness:** Greece and Slovenia have the highest daily contact with friends or family. France has the highest share lonely most/all of the time (11%, 2022). Hungary has the highest share with no close friends, and Belgium with no close family. Germany has the largest share living alone. — [Euronews/OECD](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2025/11/10/nearly-10-of-europeans-say-they-have-no-close-friends-where-do-people-feel-loneliest); [Eurofound](https://eurofound.mynewsdesk.com/news/one-in-three-households-in-eu-are-now-single-occupancy-394453)
- **Class:** unemployed and lowest-income people are about twice as likely to feel lonely. — [Euronews/OECD](https://euronews.com/my-europe/2025/11/10/nearly-10-of-europeans-say-they-have-no-close-friends-where-do-people-feel-loneliest); [Yahoo/OECD](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/why-loneliness-rise-among-young-101120350.html)
- **Gender in later life:** 42% of retirement-age women in the EU live alone vs 24% of men. — [Eurofound](https://eurofound.mynewsdesk.com/news/one-in-three-households-in-eu-are-now-single-occupancy-394453)
- **Education:** in Australia, less-educated people were more likely to flourish, reversing the usual gradient. — [Harvard](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/)
- **Religion varies:** in the US, 62% are Christian and 44% pray daily (2023–24), not a "post-religious" society. — [Pew Trust](https://www.pew.org/en/trust/archive/summer-2025/the-decline-of-christianity-has-slowed)
- **US help-seeking varies by group:** 26% of Black and 25% of Hispanic adults would likely turn to a mental-health professional, vs 16% of White and 17% of Asian adults. — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- **The gender divide varies by country and issue,** and is smaller in voting than the debate suggests (European Gen Z). — [FES](https://youthstudies.fes.de/equalize.html)

### Inferences — where West-wide assumptions would go wrong
- "Moving out = adulthood" is wrong for Southern Europe and much of Eastern Europe. "Living alone = lonely" is wrong for the Nordics and Germany, where it is common and often chosen.
- "Western people are secular" is wrong for much of the US, and religion still predicts flourishing in secular countries. The app should never ask religion (per its rules) but should leave room for prayer, church or mosque community and spiritual practice to appear in free-text answers.
- "Work is identity" fits US/Canada better than Europe. "Young people are the optimistic ones" fails in the English-speaking world.
- Class shapes loneliness and money stress. A prompt that assumes discretionary spending ("treat yourself", "book a trip") excludes low-income users.
- Older women living alone are a large, distinct group in Europe. A daily-reflection app may be among their few regular prompts to reflect, so warmth matters.

### Gaps
- No sourced data was retrieved on rural vs urban wellbeing or "left-behind" declining regions (US Rust Belt, northern England, eastern Germany, rural France). This is a material gap for the "regions in decline" question.
- No Southern-Europe-specific wellbeing, youth unemployment or emigration data was retrieved beyond leaving-home ages.
- Canada, Australia and NZ were covered mainly in aggregate (WHR youth decline, Gallup ANZ). Indigenous, migrant and minority experiences were not researched; the app's no-ethnicity rule means this matters for tone, not for questions.

## 6. Everyday moments the app could ask about that ring true, and default assumptions to check

### Takeaway
The most evidence-backed everyday moments are: eating alone or with someone, texting versus seeing a friend, a quiet group chat, a rent or bills shock, an extra shift or second job, a hard workday or job worry, a call about a parent's care, time on the phone, and noticing someone's kindness. Default assumptions to check include partner, children, a home of one's own, a 9–5 job, religion's absence, phone use as inherently bad, and living alone as sad.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind each moment)
- **Meals:** 1 in 4 Americans ate all meals alone the previous day (2023). Sharing at least one meal relates to higher happiness and lower stress that day. — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/why-sharing-meals-can-make-people-happier-what-evidence-from-142-countries-shows-252352)
- **Texting vs seeing friends:** texting 61% vs in-person 29% weekly-plus with close friends (US). — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- **Young people seeing friends daily:** fell from 44% to 36% (2015–2022, OECD Europe). — [Yahoo/OECD](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/why-loneliness-rise-among-young-101120350.html)
- **Rent and housing costs:** half of US renters are cost-burdened. — [JCHS](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/press-releases/new-report-highlights-unease-housing-market-amid-worsening-affordability-crisis)
- **Extra hours / second job, poor sleep, money worries, unpaid overtime, redundancy fear** (UK workers). — [Mental Health UK](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)
- **Daily stress:** half of US/Canadians experience a lot of stress daily. — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx)
- **Caring for a relative while working or parenting.** — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)
- **Kindness noticed:** people underestimate others' kindness, and belief in kindness predicts happiness. — [Khaleej Times/AFP](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)
- **Phone and social media time:** under one hour a day is linked to the best youth wellbeing. — [Oxford/WHR 2026](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)
- **Dating-app fatigue among Gen Z** (UK user losses since 2023). — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/gen-z-gender-split-is-reshaping-british-politics/)
- **Feeling judged or not measuring up** (socially prescribed perfectionism, parental expectations). — [APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/03/parental-expectations-perfectionism)

### Inferences — candidate question stems (researcher's suggestions, not tested)
- "Did you share a meal with anyone today? What was it like, or what was it like to eat alone?"
- "Who did you actually see this week, rather than message?" / "Is there a chat or a friend that's gone quiet lately?"
- "Was there a money moment today: a bill, a price, a rent email? How did your body react?"
- "When did work feel most like yours today, and when did it feel like too much?" Allow "I don't work" and "I work nights or shifts".
- "Did anyone do something kind for you, even something small?"
- "Is anyone in your family leaning on you right now?" (care load, without assuming parent or child roles)
- "How did your phone time feel today: connecting, numbing, or neither?"
- "Where did you feel you had to be perfect today?"

**Default assumptions to check (inference)**
- Partner or spouse: many users are single, divorced or widowed; marriage predicts flourishing but must not be presumed.
- Children: fertility is at record lows and parenthood is later (England & Wales TFR 1.39). Don't default to "your kids".
- Own home: half of US renters are burdened, and Southern Europeans live with parents to ~30. "Home" may be a parent's house, a flat-share or insecure housing.
- 9–5 office job: many hold second jobs or shift work, or are students, carers, unemployed or retired.
- Secularity: a large share pray or attend services (US). Leave room without asking.
- Living alone = loneliness: wrong in the Nordics and Germany.
- Phone = bad: light, connection-focused use correlates with the best youth wellbeing.
- Weekly rhythm (Sunday dread, Monday start): reasonable for office workers. No survey data on Sunday dread was retrieved, so the app should allow other rhythms.
- Seasonal light (long Nordic or northern winters) may shape mood. This is unsourced in these notes and needs checking before use.

### Gaps
- No user-testing or qualitative study was found on which reflective prompts resonate in Western markets.
- No data was found on "Sunday scaries", commuting, remote-work loneliness rates or holiday-season loneliness in 2025–2026.

## 7. Help-seeking and crisis support: who people turn to, helplines, respectful ways to point to help

### Takeaway
People turn first to a partner (74% in the US), then mothers and friends; women draw on wider networks than men. Professionals are a minority first choice (19%), and AI chatbots are already used for mental-health support by more than a third of people in one UK study. Every country in scope has a free national crisis line. The app should list the line for the user's country, verify numbers before launch, and frame help as normal and optional.

### Cited Findings
- **Who Americans would turn to** for emotional support (extremely or very likely): spouse/partner 74% (no gender gap); mother 48% (women 54%, men 42%); friend 46% (women 54%, men 38%); other family 35% (women 44%, men 26%); father 28%; mental-health professional 19%; online communities 5%. (Pew, Sep 2024 survey.) — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)
- **AI as support:** more than one in three people had used AI chatbots for mental-health support (UK, Mental Health UK 2025 study). — [Mental Health UK Burnout Report 2026](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)
- **Caregivers are at higher risk of isolation:** nearly 1 in 4 feel socially isolated. — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)
- **Crisis lines** (Wikipedia's list; must be verified against each service's official site before shipping):
  - US 988 (call; Veterans press 1 or text 838255; Spanish press 2 or text AYUDA; LGBTQ+ youth under 25 press 3 or text YOUTH)
  - Canada 988
  - UK and Ireland: Samaritans 116 123 (UK also lists 0800 58 58 58, 5pm–midnight; Wikipedia does not name the service here)
  - Australia: Lifeline 13 11 14
  - New Zealand 1737
  - Germany 116 123 (listed)
  - France 3114
  - Netherlands 113
  - Belgium 107
  - Sweden 90101
  - Norway 116 123
  - Denmark 70 201 201
  - Finland 09 2525 0111
  - Spain 024
  - Italy 02 99777
  - Portugal 1411
  - Switzerland 143
  - Austria 142
  - Iceland 1717
  - Luxembourg 45 45 45
  
  Wikipedia does not describe 116 123 as an EU-wide line; it appears under specific countries. — [Wikipedia: List of suicide crisis lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suicide_crisis_lines)
- **Gender gap in support:** men are less likely than women to turn to friends, mothers, other family or professionals. — [Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)

### Inferences — respectful ways to point to help
- Lead with the people users already turn to (a partner, friend or family member) before professionals, mirroring actual US help-seeking order. Offer professional and crisis options as normal, not as a last resort.
- Men and younger men have thinner support networks (Pew; OECD "men and young people" deteriorating). A gentle "is there one person you could tell?" step may matter more for them.
- Because many users already use AI for emotional support, the app should state plainly that it is not a crisis service. When risk language appears, it should surface the country-appropriate line (988 / Samaritans 116 123 / Lifeline 13 11 14 / 1737, etc.) without diagnosing.
- LGBQ+ youth show much higher distress (65.7% persistent sadness, 41% considered suicide; US 2023). Where available, specialised options (e.g., the 988 LGBTQ+ youth option, press 3) may be relevant. The app must never ask about orientation, consistent with its no-identity-questions stance. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- Text and chat options outside the US (e.g., Canada 988 text, UK text lines such as Shout 85258, Australia Lifeline text) were not verified in these notes.
- No 2025–2026 data was retrieved on help-seeking in Europe, the UK, Canada or Australia (who people turn to, therapy waiting times, stigma trends).
- Helpline numbers were not checked against official service pages; Wikipedia is a secondary source.
