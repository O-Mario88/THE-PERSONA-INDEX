## 1. What do people most want for their lives, and what do surveys show about priorities, trust, optimism and life evaluation?

### Takeaway
Across the region, the strongest and most consistent wants are economic dignity and stability: a decent job, an affordable life and the means to marry and start a household. Those wants cut across very different political settings. Life evaluation splits sharply. Gulf citizens score among the world's top quarter, while most Levantine, North African and Egyptian publics sit in the bottom half. Young people in the region, as elsewhere, report lower wellbeing than earlier generations did.

### Cited Findings
**Life evaluation (World Happiness Report 2026, 2023–2025 averages, 0–10 ladder)**
- UAE ranks 21st globally (score 6.821) and Saudi Arabia 22nd (6.817, up from 32nd in 2025). Kuwait fell from 30th to 40th, Bahrain is 55th and Oman 58th. Qatar is missing from the headline table because of data-coverage rules — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/opinion/development/2026/07/the-gulfs-happiness-test-strong-scores-before-a-geopolitical-shock/)
- The AGBI analysis warns that national averages "can hide differences" between citizens and migrant workers, and that the 2023–2025 window does not yet capture the 2026 Iran war. It may include early effects from the Gaza war and Red Sea disruption — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/opinion/development/2026/07/the-gulfs-happiness-test-strong-scores-before-a-geopolitical-shock/)
- Morocco ranks 112th of 147 globally and 14th of 18 in MENA. Algeria and Tunisia rank above Morocco. Jordan, Egypt, Lebanon and Yemen rank below it. Israel ranks 8th, first in the region — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/)
- WHR 2026 themes: younger generations report lower happiness than 15 years ago, across several regions. "Intensive" social media use in MENA ranges from 20% to 40% of adults, with Morocco around 15% and Lebanon near 45%. Passive use (scrolling, following influencers) is linked to higher stress and depression symptoms — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/)

**Global Flourishing Study (2025; 200,000+ people in 22–23 countries, including Egypt, Türkiye and Israel)**
- Many middle-income countries score better than rich ones on meaning, purpose and relationships. Türkiye is named, with Japan and the UK, among countries that "did not" do well on these — [Gallup GFS press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/660158/Global%20Flourishing%20Study%20Press%20Release.pdf); [Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/global-flourishing-study-maps-topography-human-experience)
- In many countries, people aged 18–24 report the lowest flourishing. Religious service attendance is associated with flourishing across almost all countries — [Gallup GFS press release](https://www.gallup.com/file/660158/Global%20Flourishing%20Study%20Press%20Release.pdf)

**Youth priorities and trust (ASDA'A BCW Arab Youth Survey, 15th edition, 2023: 3,600 face-to-face interviews, ages 18–24, 18 Arab states). This is older data.**
- The top concerns are unemployment, government corruption, rising living costs, economic instability and climate change — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)
- More than 60% of youth outside the GCC lack confidence that their government can tackle unemployment, corruption and living costs. In the GCC, about 78% say their voice matters to leadership and 87% say their government has the right policies — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)
- 54% feel their voice does not matter to leadership, which the source calls "a 19-point drop" from 2022. The direction of that change is stated ambiguously in the source; a separate headline reads "Less than half of Arab youth believe their voice matters to leaders" — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2340656) (headline only)
- 42% aim to start a business within five years (GCC 53%, Levant 39%, North Africa 37%). Preference for a government job fell to 30% from nearly half in 2019 — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)

**Arab Youth Priority Survey 2026 (Arab Youth Center with Ipsos)**
- 6,260 face-to-face interviews with people aged 15–35 in 22 Arab countries, covering 20 priority areas: health, education, family and relationships, employment, community safety, personal development and others. No percentages were published on the page reviewed — [Arab Youth Center](https://arabyouthcenter.org/en/article/our-research/arab-youth-priority-survey-2026)

**Migration as a want (Arab Barometer Wave VIII, 2023–2024, 7 countries)**
- Share wanting to emigrate: Tunisia 46% (its highest ever, up from 22% in 2011), Jordan 42%, Lebanon 38%, Morocco 35%, Palestine 25%, Mauritania 22%, Kuwait 16%. Younger and college-educated people are more likely to consider it. Gender matters less — [Arab Barometer press release](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- Most of those considering emigration cite economic reasons: Jordan 90%, Tunisia 89%, Lebanon 72%, Mauritania 69%, Palestine 57%, Morocco 45%, Kuwait 29%. In Kuwait, 56% cite education instead — [Arab Barometer press release](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- North America is the most preferred destination, then Western Europe, then the Gulf — [Arab Barometer press release](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)

### Inferences
- "Stability and dignity through work and family formation" is the safest region-wide frame for cravings. Political freedom matters too, but surveys show people name it far less often than economic reasons. In Lebanon, though, security (27%), corruption (24%) and politics (23%) rank higher as reasons to leave.
- The Gulf–rest split is large enough that one "MENA" voice will misfire. A Gulf citizen's questions might centre on choice, purpose and pace of change. A Jordanian or Tunisian's might centre on making ends meet and whether to leave.
- Because young adults report the lowest flourishing in many countries, and MENA youth unemployment is the world's highest (see section 3), a self-reflection app's core 18–30 audience is likely carrying more strain than older users.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Arab Barometer Wave VIII headline figures on economic evaluation, food insecurity or trust in government. The topic pages had no body text, and the PDFs were not fetched within budget.
- I found no published 2025 or 2026 ASDA'A Arab Youth Survey figures. The latest numbers I could verify are from 2023.
- I could not obtain country-level GFS scores for Egypt or Türkiye; only the qualitative note on Türkiye is sourced.
- World Values Survey Wave 7 values data (for example, the importance of family and religion) was not retrieved.
- WHR 2026 ranks for Iran, Türkiye, Iraq, Palestine and Libya were not found in the sources read.

## 2. Social life: family, honour, marriage and its cost, gender roles, religion, hospitality and reputation, generational tension

### Takeaway
Family is the main unit of decision-making and support. Marriage is both a deep aspiration and a financial ordeal, and its cost, along with housing and joblessness, is pushing marriage later from Syria to Saudi Arabia to Oman. Gender attitudes are **not** steadily liberalising. Arab Barometer's 2023–24 data shows a shift back toward patriarchal norms since 2021–22. Religiosity is polarising rather than simply declining, and patterns differ by country.

### Cited Findings
**Marriage and its cost**
- **Saudi Arabia:** marriage-related costs (dowry, wedding, furnishing, honeymoon) are estimated at SR145,000–850,000 ($38,600–226,600) or more. Typical salaries for young men likely to marry are SR4,000–7,000 a month (2024) — [Arab News opinion, Nov 2025](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)
- **Saudi Arabia:** 65.3% of 15–34-year-olds are single (2023 Saudi Youth Report). Women's average age at first marriage rose from about 18 in 1995 to 27 in 2017. Fertility fell from 6.6 in 1960 to 2.28 in 2023. Riyadh rents rose nearly 17% between February 2024 and January 2025 (GASTAT) — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)
- **Saudi Arabia:** young men are "especially vocal" about dowry and wedding costs. Social factors cited include fear of failure, high expectations and social media's effect on perceptions of marriage — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)
- **Oman (opinion column, Sept 2025):** median marriage age is rising for both sexes. Reasons given are women's education and work, "prohibitive" costs for young men (dowry, wedding, household setup) and greater individual choice. The columnist says Gulf norms have inflated the dowry beyond its religious requirement. Stigma around being unmarried is fading, family-arranged and within-family marriages are declining, and young people increasingly want partners with compatible education and values — [Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1176691/opinion/young-omanis-now-have-different-perspectives-on-marriage)
- **Syria (May 2025):** one young person said, "The costs of marriage are a nightmare that haunts me every day." A 40-year-old earning about 2 million Syrian pounds a month said marriage could cost "amounts I need years to save." A social researcher *estimates* that more than 60% of Syrian youth avoid marriage (an estimate, not a survey). Emigration of young men, partly to escape conscription under the former regime, has led some families to accept only expatriate suitors — [Enab Baladi](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/05/why-do-syrian-youth-shy-away-from-marriage/)

**Gender roles (Arab Barometer Wave VIII Gender Report, 2023–24: Kuwait, Jordan, Lebanon, Mauritania, Morocco, Palestine, Tunisia)**
- Men agreeing the "husband should have final say in family decisions": Kuwait 75%, Jordan 71%, Morocco 70%, Tunisia 68%, Mauritania 66%, Palestine 58%, Lebanon 48%. Women agreeing: Morocco 53%, Jordan 48%, Palestine 40%, Tunisia 39%, Kuwait 35%, Lebanon 27% — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- The report finds "a broad shift toward patriarchal norms in both private and public spheres since 2021-22." The share saying men make better political leaders rose in six of seven countries — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Support for equal work opportunities is high among women (Tunisia and Lebanon 90%, Palestine 71%) and lower among men (Tunisia and Lebanon 77%, Morocco 54%, Jordan 52%) — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Men with a working female relative are about 22–23 points more supportive of equal work in Morocco and Mauritania. Having a working female relative predicts egalitarian views more strongly than the respondent's own employment — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Childcare cost is the top childcare problem for women in Jordan (43%), Tunisia (41%), Lebanon (39%) and Kuwait (38%). Among women who used to work, 65% in Tunisia and 62% in Jordan plan to return — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Across MENA, Afghanistan and Pakistan, "only about one in five women participate in the labor force—the lowest rate in the world" — [World Bank, Oct 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/10/07/signs-of-improvement-in-the-economic-outlook-for-the-middle-east-north-africa-afghanistan-pakistan-region)

**Religion in daily life**
- Arab Barometer 2010–2022, 11 countries (Azedi, *Int'l Journal of Comparative Sociology*, 2025): "somewhat religious" fell from 56.7% to 47.5%, "religious" rose from 35.1% to 42%, and "not religious" rose from 8.2% to 10.6% — [Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/trends-among-arab-muslims-point-beyond-revival-versus-secularization)
- Patterns by country: Libya declined; Algeria, Jordan and Sudan rose; Egypt, Iraq, Morocco and Tunisia polarised (both ends grew); Palestine showed no change. Religiosity surged during Covid in every country with data, then declined again. The mid-2010s decline was concentrated among young men. Personal piety and support for political Islam did not move together — [Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/trends-among-arab-muslims-point-beyond-revival-versus-secularization)
- 2018–19 data (older): the share describing themselves as "not religious" was 13%, and 18% among under-30s (BBC Arabic and Arab Barometer, 25,000+ interviews, 11 countries) — [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/media-news/is-the-arab-world-really-becoming-less-religious/)

**Reputation and privacy**
- Fear of community gossip damaging one's reputation appears as a confidentiality barrier to mental-health help-seeking in 8 studies (2023 systematic review) — [El Khatib et al., BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)

### Inferences
- Wedding cost and readiness to marry are near-universal, emotionally loaded moments, especially for young men, who in most of these settings are expected to fund the dowry and household. Questions should allow for shame or pressure around "not yet" without implying that marriage is either required or outdated.
- The return toward patriarchal attitudes means the app should not assume users are on a liberalising path, or want to be. It should also not assume they are traditional. Women and men in the same household often answer differently, so neutral framings such as "who had a say in this decision?" are safer than either premise.
- Family is the decision unit. Prompts like "what did your family expect?" or "whose opinion mattered most?" are more resonant than "what do *you* want?" used alone, though both can coexist.
- Religion is personal and varies by country and person. Polarisation means a sizeable minority, larger among young people, is non-religious or less observant. Faith language should be offered as an option, never assumed.

### Gaps
- I found no recent quantitative source on honour norms (*sharaf*/*ʿird*), or on hospitality and reputation as everyday pressures. These are described qualitatively in the literature but were not retrieved this session.
- Marriage-cost data for Egypt, Iraq, the Maghreb, Iran and Türkiye was not retrieved. Only Saudi Arabia (estimates in an opinion column), Oman (opinion) and Syria (reportage) are sourced.
- Religiosity data for Türkiye, Iran and the GCC (beyond Kuwait in Wave VIII) was not retrieved.
- Divorce trends and generational-tension survey data were not found.

## 3. Economic life: youth unemployment, public-sector jobs, cost of living, informal work, remittances, the Gulf versus the rest

### Takeaway
The Arab States have the world's highest youth unemployment (26.2% in 2025) and a youth NEET rate of about 32%. Strikingly, university graduates face *more* unemployment than less-educated peers. Outside the Gulf, cost-of-living pressure is acute: Iran's economy is in crisis after the war and new sanctions, Türkiye has chronic inflation, and Syria, Lebanon and Egypt face severe purchasing-power erosion. Remittances from the Gulf and Europe are a lifeline. Gulf citizens live in a different economic world, but the migrant workers who make up much of the Gulf's population do not share it.

### Cited Findings
**Jobs**
- Youth unemployment in the Arab States was 26.2% in 2025, the highest of any region. North Africa's was 22.6%, against 12.4% globally — [ILO via Reuters/Engineering News, Aug 2026](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/global-youth-unemployment-rises-amid-sluggish-job-creation-and-looming-ai-risk-un-labour-agency-says-2026-08-12)
- Arab States youth NEET rate: 32.4% (11.6 million people) in 2025, projected to reach 12.2 million by 2027. About 2.6 million young people in the Arab States are unemployed. Unemployment among university graduates in the Arab region is about 25.6%, against about 8.6% for those with secondary education and about 9% for basic education (Phenix Center analysis of the ILO's *Global Employment Trends for Youth 2026*; the year for the education split is not given) — [Jordan News](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-113/All/Phenix-Center-Youth-Unemployment-in-Arab-States-Is-the-Highest-in-the-World-54316)
- Nearly 9 in 10 young workers in low- and lower-middle-income countries are in informal employment (global figure) — [ILO via Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/global-youth-unemployment-rises-amid-sluggish-job-creation-and-looming-ai-risk-un-labour-agency-says-2026-08-12)
- Among employed Kuwaiti women, 87% work in the public sector — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Arab youth preferring government jobs fell from nearly half in 2019 to 30% in 2023. Business (33%) and self-employment or family business (25%) gained — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)
- Saudi youth unemployment fell from about 30% in 2020 to 12.9% in 2023 — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)
- Regional growth forecast: 2.8% for 2025 and 3.3% for 2026 (October 2025, before the Iran war). Removing barriers to women's work could raise GDP per capita by 20–30% in Egypt and Jordan — [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/10/07/signs-of-improvement-in-the-economic-outlook-for-the-middle-east-north-africa-afghanistan-pakistan-region)

**Cost of living: Iran (2026)**
- The rial passed 2 million to the US dollar on the free market (Aug 2026), following the war that began on 28 February and new US sanctions plus a naval blockade of Iranian ports. The minimum wage is 166 million rials a month ($82), or under $108 with benefits. Since before the war, prices have risen about 71% for tomatoes, 74% for chicken, 177% for cooking oil, 642% for insulin and 95% for baby formula. Households report cutting meat and chicken, and one mother says her "only concern is making it to the end of the month" — [Al Jazeera, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/one-us-dollar-now-costs-2-million-rials-in-iran-and-heres-what-it-can-buy)
- A headline reports that year-on-year inflation hit 88.6% per Iran's statistics agency (June 2026). I could not read the article (blocked), so treat this as unverified — [NST headline](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/business/economy/2026/06/1474746/iran-stats-agency-says-year-year-inflation-hits-886-cent)

**Cost of living: Türkiye (2026)**
- The 2026 net minimum wage is 28,075 lira (about $655), up 27%. Official inflation was about 31% at the time. The Türk-İş union's hunger line for a family of four was 29,828 lira (Nov 2025), which is *above* the minimum wage, and its poverty line was 97,159 lira. Unions estimate roughly half of all workers earn about the minimum wage — [Turkish Minute, Dec 2025](https://turkishminute.com/2025/12/24/turkey-raises-minimum-wage-by-27-percent-drawing-sharp-political-backlash/amp/)
- The lowest pension, 20,000 lira, buys less than 16 days of food for a family of four. Food for a family of four cost 37,655 lira a month in May 2026. A union research unit (KAMU-AR) puts food inflation at 63.6% year on year, which is union-sourced and differs from official statistics — [Turkish Minute, Jun 2026](https://turkishminute.com/2026/06/02/turkeys-lowest-monthly-pension-buys-less-than-16-days-of-food-for-family-of-four-study/amp/)
- The real minimum wage fell by 5,576 lira by July 2026 (DİSK-AR). The bottom 20% of earners spend 29.2% of income on food, against 12.4% for the top 20% — [WageIndicator](https://wageindicator.org/ai/work/collective-bargaining-agreement/updates/2026/turkey-minimum-wage-has-eroded-august-31-2026/)

**Remittances**
- Egypt received a record $47.3 billion in remittances in FY2025/26, up 29.6% from $36.5 billion. More than 10 million Egyptians are estimated to live abroad, most in the Gulf (Central Bank of Egypt via Xinhua) — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)

**Gulf versus the rest**
- Gulf youth are far more confident in government than non-Gulf youth (see section 1). Kuwait has the lowest emigration desire (16%), and education rather than money drives what desire exists — [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- More than 85% of the food consumed in the GCC is imported. Bahrain and Qatar get more than half their water from desalination, which the 2026 war highlighted as a vulnerability — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649022)

### Inferences
- "Educated but waiting" is a defining experience: people with degrees who are unemployed, under-employed or queuing for public-sector posts. The app should treat waiting for a job, a visa or the means to marry as a real, active life stage, not as idleness or failure.
- Money stress outside the Gulf is often *survival* stress about food, medicine and rent, not budgeting optimisation. "Small optional steps" must cost nothing and never imply that spending money, for example on therapy, a gym or a course, is the answer.
- Many families are transnational economic units: a son in Riyadh, a father in Cairo receiving transfers. A remittance-sender's guilt, pride, loneliness or obligation is a likely real moment.

### Gaps
- No 2026 inflation or unemployment figures were retrieved for Egypt, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Tunisia or Morocco.
- No regional informality rate specific to MENA was retrieved; only the global ILO figure is sourced.
- World Bank remittance totals for Lebanon, Jordan and Morocco were not retrieved.
- No data was found on Gulf migrant workers' (largely South Asian and African) wellbeing. AGBI notes the gap but gives no figures.

## 4. Conflict, displacement and insecurity: how they shape daily life and what an app must never assume

### Takeaway
A large share of the region's people live with active or recent war, displacement or the threat of strikes. In 2026 that includes Iran, Lebanon and even Gulf cities, alongside Sudan, Gaza, Yemen and Syria. The app must never assume a safe home, a stable address, a predictable week, intact family, working internet, electricity or access to medicine.

### Cited Findings
- **Sudan** is the world's largest displacement crisis: 14.3 million refugees and IDPs. **Syria** has 13.5 million displaced, with nearly 2 million having returned since Assad fell in December 2024. Figures are as of April 2025; the 2026 report was not retrieved — [UNHCR press release, 12 June 2025](https://www.unhcr.org/md/en/news/number-people-uprooted-war-shocking-decade-high-levels)
- Host countries: Iran hosts 3.5 million refugees, the most in the world, and Türkiye 2.9 million (end-2024) — [The Sun / UNHCR Global Trends](https://thesun.my/world-news/122-million-forcibly-displaced-worldwide-untenably-high-un-AD14249458)
- **2026 Iran war, two weeks in (as of 13 March 2026):** more than 1,200 Iranian civilians killed (Iran Health Ministry), more than 10,000 injured, 25 hospitals damaged, and up to 3.2 million people temporarily displaced in Iran (UNHCR). In **Lebanon**, 773 were killed and 830,000 displaced. In the **Gulf states**, at least 16 people died (UAE, Kuwait, Saudi, Bahrain and Oman state media) — [NPR via WRVO](https://www.wrvo.org/2026-03-14/these-are-the-casualties-and-cost-of-the-war-in-iran-2-weeks-into-the-conflict)
- A strike on a school in Minab, Iran on 28 February killed 155 people, about 120 of them pupils (Iranian state media). There were drone strikes on Manama (March) and a fire near Dubai airport (March). Iranian Kurdish families in camps in Iraqi Kurdistan were displaced by Iranian strikes. Internet blackouts in Iran hampered documentation — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649022)
- WHO EMRO (Oct 2025): 9 of the 22 countries in the Eastern Mediterranean Region are fragile or conflict-affected. Lebanon has seen sporadic violence despite the November 2024 ceasefire. Nearly 50% of primary-care centres in Syria now integrate mental health and psychosocial support — [WHO EMRO](https://www.emro.who.int/media/news/world-mental-health-day-invisible-wounds-across-the-eastern-mediterranean.html)
- In Lebanon, 27% of those considering emigration cite security (2024) — [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- In Syria, displacement and living with extended family are cited as reasons people feel unready to marry — [Enab Baladi](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/05/why-do-syrian-youth-shy-away-from-marriage/)

### Inferences
- Design rules that follow from these findings:
  - No questions that presume "your home", "your room", "your commute" or "your weekend plans".
  - No location-based features or prompts.
  - Prompts must work offline and in short sessions.
  - Never ask about trauma directly or push someone to recount a loss.
  - Allow "skip / not today" without penalty or streak loss.
- Even in the affluent Gulf, 2026 brought sirens and strikes. Safety cannot be assumed by income or country.
- Grief, waiting for news of relatives, fear when phones go silent, and caring for displaced family members are likely common "real moments". They need gentle, non-diagnostic acknowledgement rather than optimisation prompts.

### Gaps
- No 2026 figures were retrieved for Gaza (casualties, displacement, famine status), Sudan, Yemen or post-war Syria. These must be checked by the report writer, or deliberately left out of app copy.
- I found no reliable final toll or end date for the 2026 Iran war. Sources describe a ceasefire by June 2026 with continued incidents.
- No survey data was found on how insecurity affects everyday routines (sleep, school, work) in 2026.

## 5. Mindset: stress and mental-health stigma, faith-based coping, fatalism versus agency, attitudes to self-improvement

### Takeaway
Stigma and low mental-health literacy are the most frequently documented barriers to seeking help. People prefer informal support from family, friends and faith. The "fatalism" stereotype is contested: Islamic theology pairs God's decree with human responsibility and effort, and scholars warn that labelling faith-based acceptance as passivity reflects a secular bias. Interest in self-development is real, shown in strong entrepreneurship aspirations and "personal development" as a youth priority area.

### Cited Findings
- A systematic review of 74 studies (2002–2022) finds that Arabs "are reluctant to seek help" despite distress. Stigma is the most common barrier (34 studies), followed by mental-health literacy (28) and privacy or confidentiality, including community gossip and reputation (8). Cost is a barrier in Arab countries. People prefer informal sources (family, friends, religion, self-help) — [El Khatib, Alyafei & Shaikh, BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)
- Same review: findings on religion are **mixed**. Several studies found religiosity did *not* predict help-seeking attitudes. Faith is described as an important coping strategy. Women generally show more positive help-seeking attitudes (9 studies), and men may feel more stigmatised. Note that most of the studies were of Arab diaspora or Israeli Arab populations, so evidence from inside the region is thin — [BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)
- 94.8% view youth mental health as a "shared responsibility" (Arab Youth Center study, news item, Oct 2026; methodology not shown) — [Arab Youth Center](https://arabyouthcenter.org/en/article/our-research/arab-youth-priority-survey-2026)
- **Fatalism critique:** Ottoman and Sunni theology held that God decrees outcomes while humans "acquire" their acts and keep moral responsibility through "particular will" (*irade-i cüziyye*). Practice varied: jurists permitted fleeing plague, and reformers argued for material preparation alongside faith. Menchinger argues that dismissing such views reflects a "secular, Enlightenment-based assumption that human initiative is the only proper response to adversity", which is itself a form of Orientalism. He also cautions that fatalism is "by no means purely a trope" — [Menchinger, Aca'ib 2 (2021)](https://ghost.ims.forth.gr/wp-content/uploads/B_1_Acaib_Volume_2_Menchinger-Revisiting-Turkish-Fatalism.pdf)
- Religiosity surged during Covid in every Arab country with data, which is consistent with turning to faith in crisis — [Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/trends-among-arab-muslims-point-beyond-revival-versus-secularization)
- Agency in practice: 42% of Arab youth aimed to start a business within five years (2023), and 65% of formerly working Tunisian women plan to return to work — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/); [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Passive social-media use is linked to higher stress and depression symptoms (WHR 2026). Lebanon has about 45% intensive users — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/)

### Inferences
- The app should hold acceptance and effort together, since many users will. Something like "what was in your hands today, and what wasn't?" reflects both trust in God (*tawakkul*) and agency without forcing a choice. It also works for non-religious users.
- Avoid clinical vocabulary such as "depression", "anxiety disorder" or "trauma" in prompts. Everyday words like "heavy", "tired", "pressure" and "peace of mind" lower stigma and fit the app's no-diagnosis rule.
- Privacy is a cultural feature, not just a technical one. Fear of gossip and reputational harm is a documented barrier, so the app's private-by-default stance is a selling point worth stating plainly, for example with a lock screen or discreet notification text.
- Self-improvement framed as serving family or community, or as personal growth with faith, may land better than self-actualisation framed as individual achievement. This is an inference from the family-centred findings, not directly tested.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed in-region prevalence data for 2024–2026 on common mental disorders by country.
- No source was retrieved on somatic expression of distress (e.g., "my chest is tight") in Arab, Turkish or Persian populations, though it is often claimed.
- There is no direct empirical study (beyond the Ottoman-history critique) testing whether faith-based acceptance reduces or supports active coping in present-day MENA populations. The 2014 study of Bangladeshi patients in London on "Islamic fatalism" could not be read (captcha) and is outside the region anyway.
- No survey was found on attitudes to self-help apps or journaling in the region.

## 6. Diversity inside the region: where a region-wide assumption goes wrong

### Takeaway
"MENA" bundles some of the world's richest citizens and some of its most war-affected people. It spans Arabic, Turkish, Persian, Kurdish, Amazigh and other languages, and countries whose religion and gender trends move in opposite directions. Every single assumption tested here breaks somewhere.

### Cited Findings
- **Gulf (GCC):** UAE and Saudi Arabia rank in the WHR top 25, with high confidence in government (87% of GCC youth say their government has the right policies, 2023). Kuwaiti men are the most likely to say the husband has final say (75%). Kuwait has the lowest emigration desire (16%), driven by education. Large migrant-worker populations are invisible in averages — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/opinion/development/2026/07/the-gulfs-happiness-test-strong-scores-before-a-geopolitical-shock/); [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/); [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf); [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- **Levant:** Lebanon has the lowest agreement with "husband has final say" (men 48%, women 27%) and the highest equal-work support (90% of women, 77% of men). Its emigration reasons are the most political and security-driven, and it went through a 2026 war with 830,000 displaced. Jordan's emigration desire is almost entirely economic (90%), and Jordanian men show the lowest equal-work support (52%) — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf); [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339); [NPR via WRVO](https://www.wrvo.org/2026-03-14/these-are-the-casualties-and-cost-of-the-war-in-iran-2-weeks-into-the-conflict)
- **Maghreb:** Tunisia has record emigration desire (46%). Willingness to migrate irregularly is 53% in Morocco, 44% in Mauritania (up from 22% in 2022) and 42% in Tunisia, and is higher among lower-income and less-educated people. Moroccan women's agreement with "husband has final say" rose from 37% (2022) to 53% (2024) — [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339); [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- **Religion diverges by country:** declining in Libya, rising in Algeria, Jordan and Sudan, and polarising in Egypt, Iraq, Morocco and Tunisia — [Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/trends-among-arab-muslims-point-beyond-revival-versus-secularization)
- **Egypt:** ranks below Morocco in WHR 2026. Remittances are a record $47.3 billion, with more than 10 million Egyptians abroad, mostly in the Gulf — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)
- **Türkiye:** non-Arab and officially secular, with chronic inflation (minimum wage below the union hunger line). It hosts 2.9 million refugees and scores relatively low on meaning, purpose and relationships in the GFS — [Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2025/12/24/turkey-raises-minimum-wage-by-27-percent-drawing-sharp-political-backlash/amp/); [The Sun / UNHCR](https://thesun.my/world-news/122-million-forcibly-displaced-worldwide-untenably-high-un-AD14249458); [Gallup GFS](https://www.gallup.com/file/660158/Global%20Flourishing%20Study%20Press%20Release.pdf)
- **Iran:** non-Arab and Persian-majority, about 92 million people. In 2026 it suffered war, a naval blockade, the rial passing 2 million to the dollar and soaring medicine prices. It hosts 3.5 million refugees, the most in the world — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/one-us-dollar-now-costs-2-million-rials-in-iran-and-heres-what-it-can-buy); [The Sun / UNHCR](https://thesun.my/world-news/122-million-forcibly-displaced-worldwide-untenably-high-un-AD14249458)
- **Minorities:** Iranian Kurdish communities in camps in Iraqi Kurdistan were displaced by Iranian strikes in 2026. In Israel, Christian Arab students were more open to formal mental-health services than Druze and Muslim students — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649022); [BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)
- Within Arab youth, desire to start a business varies: GCC 53%, Levant 39%, North Africa 37% — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)

### Inferences
- Region-wide assumptions that would fail:
  - "Everyone is Arab" fails for Türkiye, Iran, Kurds and Amazigh.
  - "Everyone is Muslim and observant" fails for Christians, Druze, Yazidis and Jews, and for the growing non-religious youth minority.
  - "Everyone is poor or in conflict" fails for Gulf citizens.
  - "Everyone is rich" fails for Gulf migrant workers.
  - "Women are liberalising" fails because Arab Barometer shows reversal.
  - "Young people want to leave" fails for Kuwait and most Gulf youth.
- The app's rule against asking for ethnicity, religion or party is especially wise here, because these identities carry legal and security risk in several states. Localisation should come from language choice and optional, user-chosen context (e.g., "I observe Ramadan", "I send money home"), never from inference.
- Urban–rural gaps are likely large (for example, Saudi youth home-ownership is 13.9–24% in major regions versus 1.4–6.3% elsewhere — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)), but evidence beyond housing was not retrieved.

### Gaps
- I retrieved no data on Amazigh, Coptic, Yazidi, Baha'i, Alawite or Shia-minority experiences, nor on Iraq or Libya specifically.
- Systematic urban–rural survey splits were not retrieved.
- No Turkish or Iranian equivalents of the Arab Barometer gender and religion data were retrieved (e.g., World Values Survey or KONDA for Türkiye; GAMAAN for Iran).
- No data on Gulf migrant workers' lives was retrieved.

## 7. Everyday moments that ring true, and Western defaults to avoid

### Takeaway
The truest everyday moments are about family decisions, money for marriage and household, waiting (for jobs, visas or news), sending or receiving remittances, faith routines, and keeping a good reputation. Western defaults built around individual autonomy, stable safety, therapy access and spending money will alienate many users.

### Cited Findings (anchors for prompts)
- Wedding and dowry cost against income — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp); [Enab Baladi](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/05/why-do-syrian-youth-shy-away-from-marriage/); [Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1176691/opinion/young-omanis-now-have-different-perspectives-on-marriage)
- Choosing a partner outside the family circle, and balancing compatibility with parents' preferences — [Oman Observer](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1176691/opinion/young-omanis-now-have-different-perspectives-on-marriage)
- Weighing emigration, including the irregular route — [Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)
- Government job against business or family business — [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)
- Childcare cost and returning to work for women — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Who has the "final say" in a family decision — [AB8 Gender Report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)
- Food and medicine prices, and making it to the end of the month — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/one-us-dollar-now-costs-2-million-rials-in-iran-and-heres-what-it-can-buy); [Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2026/06/02/turkeys-lowest-monthly-pension-buys-less-than-16-days-of-food-for-family-of-four-study/amp/)
- Remittances and family abroad — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)
- Social-media scrolling and comparison — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/)

### Inferences (prompt ideas grounded in the above; not tested with users)
- "Was there a moment today when what your family expected and what you wanted pulled in different directions?"
- "What did you do today that someone at home depends on?" (fits remittance-senders and carers)
- "Is there something you're waiting on right now (a job reply, papers, a decision)? How did the waiting feel today?"
- "Who did you talk to when something weighed on you this week?" (honours informal help without pushing therapy)
- "What was in your hands today, and what was out of your hands?" (agency together with *tawakkul*)
- "Did you host someone, or were you a guest this week? How was it?" (hospitality as both joy and pressure; my inference, not sourced)
- "Was there a cost this week (money or effort) that you carried for someone else's happiness, like a wedding, a gift or an occasion?"
- **Western defaults to avoid:**
  - "Set boundaries with your family" or "it's your life".
  - "Treat yourself" or "book a therapist" as the default step.
  - "Your weekend."
  - Dating-app or alcohol references.
  - Assuming a private room or a stable address.
  - Assuming a Gregorian-only calendar.
  - Celebrating "independence from parents" as the developmental goal.
  - Weekly mirrors that frame a quiet week as low productivity, when it may reflect insecurity, religious observance or caring for others.

### Gaps
- I found no research specifically on Ramadan routines and wellbeing (sleep, family time, spending, charity), nor on Nowruz for Iranians and Kurds. Ramadan and Nowruz prompts rest on general knowledge and must be optional. Ramadan dates follow the lunar calendar and shift each year; confirm the 2027 dates before scheduling content.
- Weekend days differ by country (some Gulf states moved to Saturday–Sunday weekends; others keep Friday–Saturday). This is background knowledge not verified this session.
- Iran uses the Solar Hijri calendar. This is background knowledge not verified this session.
- No user research on self-reflection or journaling apps in the region was found.

## 8. Help-seeking and crisis support: who people turn to, helpline availability, and respectful ways to point to help

### Takeaway
The first stop is family, friends and faith, not professionals. Formal services are scarce, costly and stigmatised, and in many conflict zones they are disrupted. Some national lifelines exist (verified: Lebanon's Embrace 1564; others listed in a secondary, undated directory). The app should point to help gently, privately and with local options, and use a maintained directory rather than hard-coded numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Preference for informal help (family, friends, religion, self-help) is consistent across Arab populations in Arab countries, immigrants and refugees. Facilitators of formal help include family awareness, encouragement, a shared culture with the therapist and a good doctor–patient relationship — [BMC Psychiatry 2023](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)
- Lebanon: Embrace runs the National Lifeline **1564** with the Ministry of Public Health's National Mental Health Programme. It offers emotional support and suicide prevention in Arabic and English, is described as anonymous, and refers callers to local resources — [Find A Helpline (ThroughLine)](https://chat.findahelpline.com/organizations/the-lifeline-1564)
- An undated secondary directory lists: Egypt, Befrienders Cairo 762 1602; Saudi Arabia, National Center for Mental Health 920033360; UAE, Al Amal Mental Health 800 4673; Iran, national helpline 1480; Türkiye, Sevinç Abla 444 7471; Israel, Eran 1201. **It lists none for Jordan, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Iraq or Palestine. All numbers need verification before use** — [Crisis Lines Worldwide PDF](https://torahinmotion.org/sites/default/files/Crisis%20Lines%20Worldwide.pdf)
- WHO EMRO is rolling out a Regional Action Plan for mental health and psychosocial support in emergencies (2024–2030). Nearly half of Syria's primary-care centres now integrate psychosocial services, which makes primary care a realistic entry point in some settings — [WHO EMRO](https://www.emro.who.int/media/news/world-mental-health-day-invisible-wounds-across-the-eastern-mediterranean.html)
- Cost: a GP visit costs at least 1 million tomans (about $5) in Iran against a $82 monthly minimum wage. Insulin is up 642% — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/one-us-dollar-now-costs-2-million-rials-in-iran-and-heres-what-it-can-buy)

### Inferences
- Respectful ways to point to help:
  - Lead with confidentiality ("this stays on your phone").
  - Normalise ("many people feel this way when pressure builds").
  - Offer layered options: a trusted person, a faith leader or counsellor if the user wishes, a doctor or primary-care centre, then a hotline.
  - Do not lead with "seek professional help", which can feel stigmatising and out of reach.
  - Never require the user to name a religion to see faith-based options. Phrase it as "someone you trust, including a religious figure if that's meaningful to you".
- Use a maintained, region-aware directory (e.g., findahelpline.com / ThroughLine, already used for Lebanon) keyed to the user's chosen country. Hard-coded numbers go stale, and several countries appear to have no verified line.
- Where calling is unsafe or impossible (shared phones, surveillance concerns, blackouts), text or chat options and "save for later" matter. The app should not log or surface crisis-screen visits in notifications.
- In wartime contexts (Iran, Lebanon, Gaza, Sudan, Yemen), humanitarian psychosocial services from WHO, UNHCR and NGOs may be more reachable than national hotlines. The app should not imply a hotline will always answer.

### Gaps
- I could not verify that the hotline numbers for Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iran and Türkiye are current. The source is an undated third-party PDF.
- I found no verified crisis lines for Jordan, Iraq, Palestine, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia or the smaller GCC states.
- No data was found on whether people in the region would actually use a helpline, or on how people respond to app-based referral prompts.
- The WHO EMRO mental-health workforce statistics (psychiatrists per 100,000) were not retrieved.
