# What matters to people in East and Southeast Asia (China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong; Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia and neighbours), as of October 2026

Notes on method and currency: research was done on 2026-10-24 with about 24 web searches and 39 page fetches. Each finding gives its data year. Data from before 2025 is marked **[older]**. Where only a secondary or aggregator source was reachable, the finding says so. "Inferences" are my reasoning from the cited findings, not facts. Anything I could not source is under "Gaps", and none of it is stated as fact anywhere else.

## 1. What do people most want for their lives? What do the big surveys show?

### Takeaway
The surveys do not show one "Asian" pattern. Rich East Asia ranks high on financial security and life evaluation but low on flourishing, relationships and daily calm (Japan comes last of 22 countries in the Global Flourishing Study). Middle-income Southeast Asia, led by Indonesia and the Philippines, scores highest on meaning, relationships, character and faith, and reports less daily stress. For an app, the cravings that repeat most are for close relationships and belonging, security (money, jobs), and meaning. "Freedom" and "excitement" matter less than Western defaults assume, and they vary by place and generation.

### Cited Findings
**World Happiness Report 2026** (life evaluation on the Cantril ladder, 3-year average, published around 20 March 2026)
- Ranks and scores: Taiwan 26 (6.714), Singapore 36 (6.585), Vietnam 45 (6.428), Thailand 52 (6.296), Philippines 56 (6.206), Japan 61 (6.130), China 65 (6.074), South Korea 67 (6.040), Malaysia 71 (6.005), Mongolia 75 (5.936), Indonesia 87 (5.617), Hong Kong 90 (5.569), Laos 92 (5.515), Cambodia 121 (4.462), Myanmar 129 (4.287) — [Atlas & Boots, reproducing the WHR 2026 table (secondary)](https://www.atlasandboots.com/?p=12864). Two of these are confirmed elsewhere: Taiwan "first in East Asia and 26th overall" — [TaiwanPlus](https://www.taiwanplus.com/news/taiwan-news/health/260319020/world-happiness-report-ranks-taiwan-first-in-east-asia); China 65th — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually). The Southeast Asian order (Singapore, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, then Malaysia and Indonesia, with Laos, Cambodia and Myanmar last) matches [Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/2026/03/22/which-country-is-happiest-in-southeast-asia-in-2026/), and the Philippines "climbs up one spot" per [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/03/20/the-philippines-climbs-up-one-spot-on-the-world-happiness-report).
- The rankings rest on a 3-year average of self-rated life evaluation, explained by GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity and perceived corruption — [Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/2026/03/22/which-country-is-happiest-in-southeast-asia-in-2026/).
- The WHR 2026 social-media finding: moderate use ("under 1 hour/day") goes with better wellbeing than no use, but the global average is 2.5 hours/day, which the report treats as harmful. Passive visual platforms (Instagram, TikTok) are tied to social comparison; messaging platforms are linked to higher life satisfaction in some regions — [Drishti IAS summary of WHR 2026](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually).
- **[older, WHR 2024, data 2021–23]** Mainland China had a large generational gap: it ranked 79th among young people and 30th among older people, so older Chinese rated their lives relatively higher. Hong Kong ranked 86 overall and 97 for under-30s; Taiwan 31 overall and 25 for under-30s — [The Standard (HK), 21 Mar 2024](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/61425/Happiness-report-comes-with-few-surprises).

**Global Flourishing Study (GFS), wave 1, data 2022–23, published in *Nature Mental Health*, 30 April 2025**
- About 203,000 people in 22 countries plus Hong Kong, surveyed in 40 languages. Flourishing = health, happiness, meaning, character, relationships, financial security and spiritual wellbeing — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything).
- Without financial indicators, **Indonesia ranked 1st and the Philippines 3rd. Japan was last on both lists** (with and without financial indicators). "Indonesia scored higher on relationships and pro-social character traits, while Japan respondents were least likely to say they had an intimate friend" — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything).
- High-income nations scored higher on financial security and life evaluation. Middle-income nations scored higher on meaning and purpose, pro-social character and close relationships. Hong Kong was one of two high-income places (with Israel) in the top half for composite flourishing. Japan kept a "somewhat more traditional" U-shaped age curve, while most countries showed flat flourishing from the late teens through the 20s. Mainland China joined the study later; no results yet — [Harvard T.H. Chan School](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/).
- Weekly or more frequent religious service attendance was "nearly universally associated" with higher flourishing; so were good relationships with parents and good health in childhood — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything).
- The authors' own caveats: surveys were run at different times, in different languages and contexts, which makes cross-country comparison difficult; no low-income countries were included; they recommend countries collect culturally suited data — [Harvard T.H. Chan School](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/).

**Gallup, State of the Global Workplace 2026** (data January–December 2025; regional figures are 3-year aggregates)
- East Asia vs Southeast Asia vs global: engaged at work 18% / 25% / 20%; thriving 32% / 36% / 34%; **daily stress 46% / 25% / 40%**; daily anger 20% / 19% / 22%; daily sadness 17% / 21% / 23%; **daily loneliness 23% (3rd highest of 10 regions) / 19% / 22%**; "good time to find a job" 58% / **64% (highest of any region)** / 52% — [Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).

### Inferences
- Across these sources, the cravings that come up most are **close relationships and belonging** (GFS relationships, Japan's lack of intimate friends, East Asia's high loneliness), **security** (financial security, jobs) and **meaning, purpose and faith** (high in Indonesia and the Philippines). An app that asks only about personal goals and achievement would miss the relational and spiritual centre of many lives here.
- Life evaluation (WHR) and flourishing (GFS) disagree: Indonesia is 87th on WHR life evaluation but 1st on GFS flourishing. Questions about **meaning, relationships and faith** may resonate with people who would score their life as "only average" on a ladder.
- East Asia combines low daily sadness with high daily stress and loneliness (Gallup). This fits a picture of composed but strained everyday life. Questions about pressure and isolation may land better than questions about sadness.
- GFS did not test, and the sources do not explain, why Japan scores low (for example, modest response styles). Treat this as an open question, not as "Japanese are unhappy."

### Gaps
- **World Values Survey (Wave 7) and Asian Barometer (Wave 5/6)** results on values (family importance, obedience vs independence, harmony, trust) were not fetched within budget. Their country figures should be pulled directly from worldvaluessurvey.org and asianbarometer.org before being quoted.
- I could not reach country-level Gallup figures (Appendix 1) for Japan, China, Korea, the Philippines and others, or the GFS country domain scores (table in *Nature Mental Health*, doi 10.1038/s44220-025-00423-5, not fetched).
- No sourced cross-national data was found on "harmony" or "face" as stated life goals.

## 2. Ideal feelings and happiness: low- vs high-arousal ideal affect (Tsai), independent vs interdependent self (Markus & Kitayama), and critiques

### Takeaway
There is robust, recently re-confirmed evidence that people in East Asian contexts on average **value calm, peaceful positive states (low-arousal positive, LAP)** relatively more, and excitement (high-arousal positive, HAP) relatively less, than European Americans. Cultures differ more in how people *want* to feel than in how they actually feel. But the 2025 meta-analysis also finds differences *among* East Asian groups, by class, gender and age, and a rise in Western valuing of calm. The East–West self-construal binary is too simple. Use these ideas as design defaults, not as labels for people.

### Cited Findings
- Ideal affect = "the emotions and feelings that people value and ideally want to feel." European Americans value HAP (excitement, enthusiasm); East Asian contexts (Hong Kong, Taiwan, Japan, China, South Korea) value LAP (calm, peace) relatively more. "How people want to feel... is different from how people actually feel": actual feelings showed few or no differences — [Psi Chi interview with Jeanne Tsai](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai).
- Evidence includes more excited, open smiles in best-selling US children's books than in Taiwanese ones; US leaders "six times more likely to show these open, toothy smiles" than Chinese leaders; and American preschoolers choosing excited faces over calm ones more than Taiwanese preschoolers, even controlling for temperament — [Psi Chi](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai); [Tsai et al. 2016, leaders' smiles (PDF)](https://culture-emotion-lab.stanford.edu/sites/culture_emotion_lab/files/media/file/tsai_et_al_leaders_smiles_2016.pdf).
- People see others who show the emotions they value as warmer and more trustworthy (an "ideal affect match"). Reserved Asian Americans can be misread as disengaged, but "they are just showing their warmth and friendliness in a different way" — [Psi Chi](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai).
- Tsai notes excitement tends to come with rare events and calm with everyday activities. "If you start defining your happiness in only one way, then it might be harder to achieve" (her suggestion, not a tested finding) — [Psi Chi](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai).
- **2025 meta-analysis** (Tsai et al., *Psychological Bulletin* 151(12):1486–1524; 124 datasets, 31,000+ participants, individual-participant data): actual and ideal affect are distinct; "cultural differences in ideal affect are larger in magnitude than cultural differences in actual affect." European Americans' greater valuing of HAP relative to East Asians persisted over time. But European Americans came to value LAP *more* over time, there were differences *among* specific East Asian groups, and SES, gender and age were also linked to ideal affect — [UCLouvain repository abstract](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280).
- **Critique of the independent/interdependent binary:** Vignoles et al. (2016, *J. Exp. Psych.: General* 145(8):966–1000) studied 2,924 students in 16 nations, then 7,279 adults in 55 cultural groups across 33 nations. Global patterns only partly support Markus & Kitayama (1991). "A simple contrast between independence and interdependence does not adequately capture the diverse models of selfhood"; cultures combine 7 dimensions of independence and interdependence differently, linked to socioeconomic development and religious heritage. The paper argues the field leaned too heavily on North America vs East Asia comparisons — [Ateneo de Manila repository](https://archium.ateneo.edu/psychology-faculty-pubs/143).

### Inferences
- Insight cards and "small steps" should treat **calm, contentment, steadiness and peace** as goals in their own right, not only energy, excitement or "crushing it." A useful default would offer both kinds of positive feeling ("What gave you a quiet, settled feeling today?" alongside "What lit you up?") and let the person's answers steer.
- The data was mostly gathered in East Asian contexts (China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Japan, Korea). It should **not be extended to Southeast Asia** without evidence. Nothing found here tests ideal affect in Indonesia, the Philippines or Vietnam.
- Since how people want to feel differs more than how they actually feel, the app should ask how someone *wanted* to feel in a moment, as well as how they did feel.
- Given Vignoles et al., the app should not assume "Asian = collectivist." A person can be strongly self-directed at work, interdependent at home, and religiously grounded all at once.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Markus & Kitayama (1991) itself or later critiques specific to Southeast Asia (for example, Filipino *kapwa* (shared identity) or Indonesian *gotong royong* (mutual help) in psychology papers). The concepts are widely discussed, but they are uncited here.
- Effect sizes from the 2025 meta-analysis are behind an embargo (repository embargo until 2027-01-01).

## 3. Social life: filial piety, face, marriage pressure, gender, work culture, education pressure, loneliness and ageing, religion and ritual

### Takeaway
Family obligation (care for parents, ancestors, marriage and children), exam-driven education, long and hierarchical work, and new forms of isolation in ageing societies are the strongest everyday social pressures I could source. Religion and ritual divide the region sharply. Indonesia and Thailand are highly devout, while Hong Kong, Korea and Japan have many unaffiliated people who still practise ancestor rituals. Younger people are actively renegotiating these expectations, often with humour, rather than rejecting family.

### Cited Findings
**Family, marriage pressure and holidays**
- China: young urban adults face intense marriage pressure at Lunar New Year (*cuī hūn* 催婚, being pressured to marry), linked to "going home anxiety" (回家焦虑症). Resistance tactics include very high stated standards and giving parents spreadsheet updates on the search. The article ties this to one-child families and the traditional expectation of marrying by about 30, which it says is less realistic for people born in the 1990s. No survey data **[older, Feb 2022]** — [The China Project](https://thechinaproject.com/?p=218440).
- Korea, Chuseok 2026: satirical "nagging menus" price relatives' questions (job search 450,000 won, marriage 650,000 won, children 950,000 won); over 67,000 menus generated. In a PMI survey (1,000 adults, 2026), 48.5% chose excessive interference and personal questions from relatives as the holiday custom they most wanted changed. Hankook Research found the share planning to meet family who live separately fell from 70% (before Lunar New Year) to 68% (before last Chuseok) to 63%. Cafés host "shelter" gatherings for people in their late 20s to mid-30s — [Korea JoongAng Daily, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/chuseok-nagging-menus-put-a-price-on-prying-as-younger-koreans-seek-to-celebrate-without-stress/12886656).
- **[older, 2016]** The holiday phrases Koreans most disliked: "When will you have kids? How about a second child?" 23%, "You've gained weight" 20%, "When do you plan to get married?" 14%, "Have you found a job?" 13%. Most wanted: "Seeing you gives me strength" 28% (Yuhan-Kimberly survey, sample size not reported) — [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2024091215204259947).
- Korea's total fertility rate rose to 0.80 in 2025 (up 0.05), with 254,300 births (+6.7%, the most since 2021). Average age at first birth is 33.2 — [Korea Times, 26 Aug 2026](https://koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20260826/koreas-births-rise-67-to-254300-in-2025).

**Filial care and ageing**
- Singapore: the family holds primary responsibility for aged care, backed by Maintenance of Parents legislation (early 1980s). Home caregivers are mostly ageing women, and nearly half relied on foreign domestic workers (ESCAP 2015 **[older]**). Thailand: tax-funded universal health coverage plus a community-based long-term care model (about 2–8 hours of home care a week by family, neighbours and trained volunteers). Costs still burden poorer and rural older people — [LIRNEasia, Aug 2026](https://lirneasia.net/2026/08/the-many-approaches-to-caring-in-ageing-societies-of-south-and-southeast-asia/).
- Research on Thailand describes "the persistence of parent repayment" and the anticipation of filial care obligations in two Thai provinces (title-level evidence only; not read in full) — [UEA repository](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/64795/).

**Loneliness and dying alone (Japan)**
- In 2025, 76,941 people died alone at home in Japan (of 204,562 bodies handled by police). 58,919 (nearly 80%) were aged 65+. 22,222 were found at least 8 days after death and 7,148 after 30+ days. Of these "isolated deaths", 17,620 were men and 4,598 women (National Police Agency; Cabinet Office estimate) — [Nippon.com, May 2026](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02768/).
- GFS: Japanese respondents were the least likely of 22 countries to say they had an intimate friend — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything). East Asia daily loneliness 23% (3rd highest of 10 regions) — [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).

**Work culture**
- Japan's 2025 Karoshi White Paper (FY2024): 1,030 workers' compensation claims for brain or heart disease (241 approved, 67 fatal) and 3,780 for mental disorders (1,055 approved, 88 suicides or attempts). 68.3% of workers reported strong work-related anxiety, worry or stress. Among employees working over 40 hours a week, 8.0% worked over 60 (government target 5%). "Interpersonal relationships" was the largest trigger category for mental disorders, and "problems with supervisors" made up over 60% of those cases; there were 389 power-harassment decisions — [MHLW 2025 White Paper summary (PDF)](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11200000/001684286.pdf).
- East Asia has the third-lowest employee engagement (18%, 8th of 10) and high daily stress (46%) among the 10 world regions — [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).

**Education pressure**
- Korea, 2025: private education (hagwon, cram-school) spending was 27.5 trillion won, down 5.7% from the 2024 peak of 29.2 trillion only because student numbers fell (5.13m to 5.02m). Spending per participating student reached a record 604,000 won a month. Participation was 75.7% — [Korea JoongAng Daily, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/private-education-spending-for-grade-school-students-hits-record-high-in-2025/12560246).
- China: the share of high-school graduates going on to higher education rose from 17% (2003) to 60% (2023). University graduates rose from 7.53m (2018) to 11.58m (2023) — [Nippon.com, Feb 2026](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01203/).

**Religion and ritual**
- Pew survey of East Asia, June–Sept 2023 (10,390 adults; mainland China not surveyed). Buddhist: Japan 46%, Vietnam 38%, Taiwan 28%, Korea 14%, Hong Kong 14%. Unaffiliated: Hong Kong 61%, Korea 52%, Vietnam 48%, Japan 42%, Taiwan 27%. Roughly half or more in every place made food or drink offerings to ancestors in the past year; in Vietnam, 96% burned incense for ancestors. Between 17% (Vietnam) and 53% (Hong Kong, Korea) have left their childhood religion — [Pew Research Center, 17 Jun 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/06/17/6-facts-about-religion-and-spirituality-in-east-asian-societies/).
- 59% of religiously *unaffiliated* Japanese made ancestor offerings in the past year. 85% of Japanese and 84% of Vietnamese say their family has a gravesite. 70% in Taiwan and 73% in Vietnam offered money or goods for the afterlife. 90% of Thais (2022) say someone in their household performs death-anniversary rituals — [Pew Research Center, 1 Jul 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/01/rituals-honoring-deceased-ancestors-vary-widely-in-east-and-southeast-asia/markdown).
- Indonesia: "nearly all adults say religion is very important in their lives." Singapore 36%, Vietnam 26%. No East Asian place surveyed has more than 21% praying daily (Japan 19%, Hong Kong 13%) — [Pew Research Center, Aug 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/08/09/where-is-the-most-religious-place-in-the-world/markdown).

### Inferences
- "No religion" in East Asia does **not** mean secular in the Western sense. Many unaffiliated people still tend graves, burn incense and make offerings. The app can ask about remembering or honouring someone who has died, or a family ritual, without asking anyone's religion, and that will ring true widely.
- Holiday family gatherings (Lunar New Year, Chuseok, Tết, Eid / Lebaran, Christmas in the Philippines) are recurring, foreseeable pressure points that bring both warmth and intrusive questions. Young Koreans' humour (nagging menus) suggests reflection prompts can be light. They should never frame family as the enemy.
- Japan's work-stress data points to **the boss relationship and harassment** more than raw hours as the main trigger. Prompts about a moment with a supervisor may be more relevant than prompts about overtime alone.
- The isolation data falls mostly on **older men** in Japan. A mobile app may under-reach this group, but adult children worrying about a parent living alone is a plausible everyday moment.

### Gaps
- **Face (mianzi / chemyeon / mentsu / hiya (Philippines, roughly shame) / malu (Indonesia and Malaysia, shame))**: I found no 2025–26 empirical source within budget. The concept is well established in the literature but is uncited here.
- **Gender roles:** I found no sourced 2025–26 data on, for example, Korea's gender conflict, Japan's gender gap, women's double burden, or Chinese "leftover women" discourse. The only gendered facts sourced are OFW feminisation (Section 4), Singapore's female caregivers and Japan's male isolated deaths.
- **996 / China work hours, OECD annual hours for Korea and Japan:** not sourced in this session.
- **Gaokao / suneung 2026 candidate numbers:** not sourced.
- **Filial piety** data for China, Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines (co-residence rates, remittances to parents, laws such as China's Elderly Rights Law): not sourced.
- **Japan hikikomori and Korean "just resting" (쉬었음) youth:** not sourced in this session.

## 4. Economic life: youth unemployment and "lying flat", housing, overseas work, informal work, digital payments

### Takeaway
Economic insecurity takes different forms across the region. In China, many educated young people cannot find jobs that match their degrees (youth unemployment 16–19% in 2025–26). In Southeast Asia, most workers hold informal jobs (about 63–90% outside Malaysia and Singapore, by ILO measures), and migration is a household strategy: 2.19 million Filipino overseas workers, most of them women, plus Indonesians "running away first" (#KaburAjaDulu). Lying flat is more often a temporary, sympathetic coping stance than a lifestyle.

### Cited Findings
- **China youth unemployment** (urban, ages 16–24, excluding students): 18.0% in September 2026, down from 18.9% in August 2026 (a 12-month high). The rate for ages 25–29 was 7.4% and for 30–59 it was 3.9% — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/youth-unemployment-rate). Earlier figures: 16.5% in December 2025 after peaking at 18.9% in August 2025. The rate reached 21.3% in June 2023, after which the National Bureau of Statistics suspended the series for five months and changed the method (it resumed at 14.9% in December 2023). The article attributes the gap to graduates wanting white-collar work while industry needs technical and frontline workers — [Nippon.com, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01203/). A 16.1% reading was reported on 19 March 2026 (the article headline; data month not confirmed) — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-03-19/china-youth-jobless-rate-falls-to-161-extending-decline-102424813.html).
- **Lying flat (*tang ping*):** in a 2021 Weibo poll of more than 240,000 people **[older]**, most were sympathetic or admiring and fewer than 10% fully disapproved. Young people increasingly call themselves "rat people" online. In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration launched a campaign against content promoting a laid-back lifestyle — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01203/).
- **Hong Kong** (YWCA, 990 secondary students, Sept–Nov 2024): 12.6% viewed lying flat positively, 34.8% negatively and 52.6% neutrally. 48% would support a friend who needed to lie flat. About 27% said they had adopted it, but most saw it as temporary — [The Standard, Jan 2025](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/editorial/article/69761/Lying-flat-stigma-easing-in-HK-but-not-as-long-term-lifestyle).
- **Philippines, Overseas Filipino Workers (OFWs), 2024:** 2.19 million OFWs (+1.5%), 57.2% of them women (1.25m). 74.5% work in Asia, including Saudi Arabia 21.9% and the UAE 12.4%. 43.6% are in elementary occupations. The largest age group is 45+ (25.8%). Cash remittances for the survey period were ₱262.20 billion (PSA, reported Dec 2025) — [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/pinoyabroad/dispatch/969755/ofws-grew-to-2-19m-in-2024-psa/story/).
- **Informal employment** (ILO / ILOSTAT, % of total employment): Laos 90.5% (2022), Cambodia 89.4% (2019 **[older]**), Indonesia 81.2% (2023), Myanmar 81.0% (2020 **[older]**), Timor-Leste 80.6% (2021), Vietnam 67.7% (2023), Thailand 63.3% (2024), Mongolia 41.9% (2023), South Korea 26.6% (2019 **[older]**) — [Wikipedia list citing ILOSTAT (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_informal_employment_rate). The World Bank warned in October 2025 of worsening job quality and a shift toward informality in East Asia and the Pacific, with Indonesia "most affected" (headline and summary only; report figures not retrieved) — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/world-bank-warns-of-rising-informal-employment-across-east-asia-with-indonesia-most-affected-405218/).
- **Indonesia, #KaburAjaDulu ("just escape first"):** surged on X in February 2025 as people shared overseas jobs and scholarships and vented about toxic workplaces, graduate unemployment, wages lagging living costs, layoffs and 2025 budget cuts. Ministers responded with calls for domestic job creation and for following official migration procedures — [Wikipedia, citing Kompas, Tirto, Liputan6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KaburAjaDulu); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3298701/young-indonesians-yearn-run-away-overseas-work-frustration-grows).
- In contrast, Southeast Asian respondents were the most optimistic of any world region that it is a good time to find a job (64%), vs 58% in East Asia — [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).

### Inferences
- For many Southeast Asian users, "work" means **irregular, informal, multi-job or family-business work**, not a salaried office job with a boss, HR and a weekend. Prompts should not assume a steady employer, paid leave or a commute.
- Remittances and migration make **money a relational act**: sending money home, being the one who left, being the one left behind, a parent working in Riyadh or Hong Kong. These are high-salience moments for Filipino and Indonesian users (and for Vietnamese, Thai and Myanmar migrants, though not sourced here).
- Lying flat, "rat people" and #KaburAjaDulu read best as **coping and protest language about blocked mobility**, not laziness. The app should not moralise about ambition, and it should not glamorise opting out. China's 2025 campaign against "laid-back" content means users there may be cautious about how they write about it.
- Gallup's optimism figure and Indonesia's #KaburAjaDulu frustration point in different directions. Optimism about the job market can sit alongside frustration about job quality.

### Gaps
- **Housing costs** (Hong Kong, Seoul, Shanghai/Beijing, Manila, Jakarta; China's property downturn and household wealth): not sourced in this session.
- **Digital payments** (Alipay/WeChat Pay, QRIS in Indonesia, GCash/Maya in the Philippines, PromptPay in Thailand, DuitNow in Malaysia, VietQR): not sourced in this session. This matters for designing a "small step" that involves money.
- **Household debt** (Thailand, Korea, Malaysia) and online lending and gambling: not sourced.
- **Youth unemployment** in Korea, Indonesia and the Philippines: not sourced.
- The ILO informal-employment figures come via a Wikipedia table. National statistics offices use different definitions and often report different shares (for example, Indonesia's BPS), so these figures should be verified on ILOSTAT before use.
- The ₱262.20bn remittance figure covers a PSA survey reference period that the secondary source does not state precisely (likely part-year). Check the PSA Survey on Overseas Filipinos release.

## 5. Mindset: stress, emotional restraint, mental health stigma, self-improvement culture

### Takeaway
High stress and self-reliance are well documented. The common Western assumption that **stigma** is the main barrier to help for Chinese adults is not supported by a systematic review: self-reliance, preferring other sources of help, not seeing a need, and cost come first. East Asia shows high stress but low reported sadness, which fits emotional restraint and composure as norms. Self-improvement and competitive striving (cram schools, record tutoring spend) coexist with opting out (lying flat).

### Cited Findings
- **Barriers to professional help, Chinese adults** (Shi, Shen, Wang & Hall 2020, *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 11:442; systematic review of 17 studies from 6,933 citations): the most frequently reported barriers were **self-reliance, seeking help from alternative sources, low perceived need, lack of affordability, and negative attitudes or poor past experiences** with help-seeking. Stigma, family opposition, fear of burdening family and unwillingness to disclose were among the *less* frequently reported barriers **[older, 2020]** — [Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00442/text).
- Japan: 68.3% of workers report strong work-related anxiety, worry or stress (2024) — [MHLW (PDF)](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11200000/001684286.pdf).
- East Asia: daily stress 46% but daily sadness only 17% (tied lowest of 10 regions). Southeast Asia: daily stress 25% — [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).
- Valuing calm (LAP) rather than excitement shapes how people define happiness and how they read others' emotional displays — [Psi Chi / Tsai](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai); [Tsai et al. 2025 meta-analysis](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280).
- Malaysia's government helpline calls more than doubled, from 42,990 (2024) to 90,981 (2025). The ministry said "more people are beginning to come forward to obtain tele-counselling services." Teen calls (ages 10–19) rose from 632 (2023) to 2,729 (2025) — [The Star, Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/07/09/malaysias-mental-health-helpline-received-more-than-230000-calls-since-launch-says-health-ministry).
- Korean families keep spending a record amount per tutored child (604,000 won a month in 2025), even as student numbers fall — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/private-education-spending-for-grade-school-students-hits-record-high-in-2025/12560246).
- Korea has the OECD's highest suicide rate: 24.1 per 100,000 in 2020 vs an OECD average of 11.3 **[older]**. The same Sept 2024 article reports 13,770 suicide deaths "last year" (+6.7%; the year is inferred as 2023 from the article date, and the figure may be provisional) — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/nation/2024/09/113_382248.html).

### Inferences
- An app that prizes **self-reliance and privacy** fits the leading barrier (people prefer to handle things themselves). Private reflection is culturally congruent and could be a bridge: "you are already doing the work yourself; here is one more resource if it ever gets heavier."
- Emotional restraint does not mean an absence of feeling. Prompts that ask about **concrete moments and the body** (sleep, tiredness, a tight chest before a meeting) or **relationships** may draw richer answers than "How do you feel?" or "Name your emotion." This is an inference; I found no sourced evidence on somatic reporting in this session.
- Rising helpline use in Malaysia suggests attitudes toward help-seeking are shifting in at least part of Southeast Asia. Avoid assuming any user is "too ashamed" to seek help.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 peer-reviewed source was fetched on mental-health stigma in Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand), on somatisation, or on *hiya*, *malu* or *kreng jai* (Thai deference). The Springer review on help-seeking (BMC Psychiatry 2022, s12888-022-03754-0) failed to load.
- WHO WPRO data (treatment gap, suicide rates by country, Japan and China suicide trends) was not fetched.
- Korean "god-saeng" (갓생, a highly productive "god-tier" life), Chinese *neijuan* (involution) and Japanese self-help culture: not sourced in this session.

## 6. Diversity inside the region: rich East Asia vs Southeast Asia, urban vs rural, generations; where a region-wide assumption would go wrong

### Takeaway
On almost every metric found, the gap *within* this region is as large as the gap between "Asia" and "the West". This applies to life evaluation, flourishing, stress, religiosity, informality and demography. A single "Asian user" persona would be wrong in predictable ways.

### Cited Findings
- **Life evaluation:** Taiwan is 26th in the world and Myanmar 129th. Vietnam (45) and Thailand (52) rank above Japan (61), China (65) and Korea (67) — [WHR 2026 via Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/?p=12864).
- **Flourishing:** Indonesia is 1st and Japan last of 22 countries — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything).
- **Stress and work:** daily stress is 46% in East Asia vs 25% in Southeast Asia; engagement 18% vs 25% — [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).
- **Religiosity:** nearly all Indonesians say religion is very important, vs 26% of Vietnamese. 61% of Hong Kongers and 52% of Koreans are unaffiliated — [Pew Aug 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/08/09/where-is-the-most-religious-place-in-the-world/markdown); [Pew Jun 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/06/17/6-facts-about-religion-and-spirituality-in-east-asian-societies/).
- **Work form:** informal employment is 26.6% in Korea (2019) vs 81–90% in Indonesia, Laos and Cambodia — [ILOSTAT via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_informal_employment_rate).
- **Demography:** Korea has a fertility rate of 0.80 and a first birth at 33.2 — [Korea Times](https://koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/society/20260826/koreas-births-rise-67-to-254300-in-2025). Japan had 58,919 people aged 65+ die alone at home in 2025 — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02768/). Thailand and Singapore are building ageing-care systems, while Thailand's rural elderly face cost and transport burdens — [LIRNEasia](https://lirneasia.net/2026/08/the-many-approaches-to-caring-in-ageing-societies-of-south-and-southeast-asia/).
- **Generations:** in China (WHR 2024 **[older]**), older people's life evaluations rank far higher than young people's (30th vs 79th) — [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/61425/Happiness-report-comes-with-few-surprises). Young Koreans are reshaping holidays to avoid intrusive questions — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/chuseok-nagging-menus-put-a-price-on-prying-as-younger-koreans-seek-to-celebrate-without-stress/12886656). East Asian groups differ from one another in ideal affect, and age, gender and SES matter too — [Tsai et al. 2025](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280).
- **Within China:** the youth jobless rate is 18.0% at ages 16–24 vs 3.9% at 30–59 — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/youth-unemployment-rate).

### Inferences
Where a region-wide assumption would go wrong:
- **"Asians value calm, not excitement."** The evidence comes from East Asia and has internal variation. Applying it to a 24-year-old in Manila or Jakarta is unsupported.
- **"Asians are religious" or "Asians are secular."** Both are wrong depending on place. The safe ground is ritual, remembrance and gratitude, which run across both.
- **"Work = long office hours with a boss."** True for many in Tokyo, Seoul or Shanghai. For most workers in Indonesia, Vietnam, Cambodia or Laos, work is informal or self-run.
- **"Ageing and loneliness" is an East Asian (and Thai, Singaporean) theme.** Southeast Asia's younger countries face migration and family separation instead (Philippines, Indonesia).
- **"Family pressure = oppression."** Young people negotiate rather than cut ties. They use humour and spreadsheet updates, avoid some gatherings, and still sometimes feel it is "a shame to spend Chuseok like just another weekend."
- **Urban–rural:** Thailand's care costs fall hardest on rural elders, and Chinese graduates' expectations are urban and white-collar. Rural and peri-urban users likely face different everyday moments (farm or seasonal income, distance to services). This is only lightly sourced.

### Gaps
- Urban–rural splits in values and wellbeing (for example, Asian Barometer or WVS by settlement type) were not sourced.
- Ethnic and religious minorities within countries (Malaysia's Malay, Chinese and Indian communities; Mindanao; Indonesia's many ethnic groups; China's minorities) were not researched in this session. The app should not ask about these, but tone and examples should not assume a majority identity.
- No sourced data on Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos or Timor-Leste beyond WHR ranks and informality.

## 7. Everyday moments that ring true, and Western defaults to avoid

### Takeaway
The most authentic prompts in this region are about **concrete relational and obligation moments**: holiday questions from relatives, a boss's mood, a child's exam or cram-school schedule, sending money home, caring for or worrying about a parent, a ritual for someone who has died, a job search that does not match a degree. Western defaults to avoid include framing family as something to set boundaries against, treating excitement and passion as the measure of a good day, assuming a salaried nine-to-five, assuming therapy is the obvious next step, and assuming the user is secular or Christian.

### Cited Findings (the evidence each moment rests on)
- Holiday questions about marriage, children, jobs and weight (Korea Chuseok 2026; China Lunar New Year) — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/chuseok-nagging-menus-put-a-price-on-prying-as-younger-koreans-seek-to-celebrate-without-stress/12886656); [The China Project [older, 2022]](https://thechinaproject.com/?p=218440); [Asia Economy [older, 2016 survey]](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2024091215204259947).
- Trouble with a supervisor as the biggest work-stress trigger in Japan — [MHLW 2025 (PDF)](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11200000/001684286.pdf).
- Cram-school spending and participation (Korea) — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/private-education-spending-for-grade-school-students-hits-record-high-in-2025/12560246).
- Overseas work and remittances (Philippines); wanting to leave (Indonesia) — [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/pinoyabroad/dispatch/969755/ofws-grew-to-2-19m-in-2024-psa/story/); [Wikipedia: KaburAjaDulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KaburAjaDulu).
- Ancestor offerings, incense, grave visits and death anniversaries — [Pew Jul 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/01/rituals-honoring-deceased-ancestors-vary-widely-in-east-and-southeast-asia/markdown).
- Lacking an intimate friend (Japan); everyday loneliness (East Asia) — [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything); [Gallup 2026](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx).
- A degree that does not lead to the hoped-for job (China) — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d01203/).
- Social media comparison (heavy, passive, visual use) — [WHR 2026 via Drishti](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually).

### Inferences (draft prompt ideas, for the app writer to test with local users; not validated)
Everyday-moment questions likely to ring true. They are worded to avoid naming ethnicity, religion or party.
- "Was there a moment today when someone in your family asked about your plans (work, marriage, children)? What did you say, and what did you wish you'd said?"
- "Did you stay at work later than you needed to today? What kept you there?" (This can cover waiting for the boss to leave, though the "boss left" norm itself was not sourced here.)
- "Did you send money, food or something else to family this week? How did it feel to send it?"
- "Who did you look after today, a parent, a child, someone else? Who looked after you?"
- "Was there a moment of calm today, even a small one?" (low-arousal positive). Pair it with "Was there a moment that gave you energy?" (high-arousal positive).
- "Did you remember or honour someone today, a person who has passed, or a tradition?"
- "Did you compare yourself with someone online today? What happened next?"
- "Is there a decision you're making for your family as much as for yourself?"
- Weekly mirror themes: obligations met; small moments of peace; who you were close to; what you're carrying for others; what you let go of.

Western defaults to avoid (inference from the findings above):
- Treating **"set boundaries" or "cut off toxic family"** as the main way to handle family pressure. Better: what part of the expectation you share, and what small response feels honest *and* respectful.
- Treating **excitement, passion or "high energy"** as the marker of a good day or a good life.
- Assuming a **salaried job, a weekend, paid leave or a personal "career path"**. Many work informally, for family businesses, or abroad.
- Treating **"see a therapist"** as the default small step. Self-reliance and informal help come first for many. Offer graded options: a trusted person, a doctor, a hotline, a counsellor.
- Assuming **individual choice** in decisions (study, marriage, migration) that are often family-level.
- Assuming the user is **secular** or **Christian**, and Christmas/Easter-centric seasonality. The calendar should include Lunar New Year, Chuseok, Tết, Ramadan and Eid/Lebaran, Songkran, Obon and Christmas in the Philippines, as *optional* timing cues without asking someone's faith.
- **Praise for public self-promotion** or "loud" confidence as the only form of self-worth. Quiet competence and contribution to others count (fits GFS character and relationship domains).

### Gaps
- None of these prompts has been tested with users in the region. Local co-design or cognitive interviewing in the main languages (Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Bahasa Indonesia/Malay, Filipino/Tagalog, Vietnamese, Thai) is needed.
- Workplace norms such as not leaving before the boss (Japan, Korea), *nunchi* (Korean social awareness of others' moods), *kreng jai* and *utang na loob* (Filipino debt of gratitude) were not sourced in this session.
- Translation issues (for example, there is no neutral word for "depression" in some languages, and honorifics) were not researched.

## 8. Help-seeking and crisis support: who people turn to, helpline availability, respectful ways to point to help

### Takeaway
State-run crisis lines now exist or are expanding in China (12356), Korea (109, 24/7), Malaysia (HEAL 15555), Taiwan (1925) and Thailand (1323). Capacity is uneven: Korea's 109 answered only 47% of calls in early 2026. The evidence suggests people turn first to themselves and informal help. The app should therefore offer help as one respectful, private option among several, with numbers checked per country before launch.

### Cited Findings
- **China:** the 12356 national psychological assistance hotline had launched in 18 provincial-level regions (including Beijing, Shanghai and Zhejiang) as of February 2025, with others to follow. The National Health Commission named 2025–2027 "pediatrics and mental health service years" and committed to at least one hospital with psychological and sleep clinics in every city-level region — [National Health Commission, 14 Feb 2025](https://en.nhc.gov.cn/2025-02/14/c_86389.htm).
- **South Korea:** 109 is the Ministry of Health and Welfare's 24-hour suicide prevention line. In Q1 2026 counsellors answered 582 of an average 1,118 daily calls (47%), down from 56.9% in 2024. More than half of calls come at night. There were 103 counsellors, with a plan for 200 by October 2026. Lifeline Korea takes overflow calls at night — [Korea Herald, 31 May 2026](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10759801). English-speaking callers can request a translator — [Spill (summary of Korea Herald)](https://news.spill.org/en/article/koreas-suicide-fix-starts-with-answering-calls-385).
- **Malaysia:** Talian HEAL 15555 (Health Ministry counsellors, 8am to midnight daily, including holidays) has taken 230,151 calls since 21 Oct 2022, including 90,981 in 2025. It follows up for six months with callers who attempted suicide. Other listed services: Talian Kasih 15999 and Befrienders KL — [The Star, Jul 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/07/09/malaysias-mental-health-helpline-received-more-than-230000-calls-since-launch-says-health-ministry).
- **Aggregator list (Intellect, undated; verify before use):** Taiwan MOHW suicide prevention line 1925 (24h) and Lifeline 1995. Thailand Department of Mental Health 1323 and Samaritans of Thailand 02-113-6789. Indonesia government 119 and LISA suicide prevention helpline (Bahasa Indonesia and English). Philippines National Center for Mental Health 0917-899-8727 / (02) 7989-8727 (24h) and In Touch. Singapore Samaritans 1-767 and IMH 6389-2222. Hong Kong Samaritan Befrienders 2389-2222. Japan: only TELL 03-5774-0992 (English) is listed. Vietnam: only general emergency 113 and child line 111 are listed. **The same list still gives Korea's 1393 and 1577-0199 rather than 109, so parts of it are outdated** — [Intellect](https://intellect.co/?p=7395).
- Help-seeking preferences (Chinese adults): self-reliance and alternative or informal sources rank above stigma as barriers — [Shi et al. 2020](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00442/text).

### Inferences
- Respectful pointing-to-help in this region probably means:
  - framing help as **strength and care for the people who depend on you**, not as a diagnosis
  - offering a **ladder** (talk to someone you trust, a doctor, a counsellor, a hotline), not a single "get therapy" push
  - keeping it **private**, with no sharing or social features
  - **localising by country with verified numbers and hours**. Korea's 109 answer rate and Malaysia's non-24h hours mean the app should list a backup (Lifeline Korea, Befrienders) and emergency numbers.
- Faith-based support (mosque, church, temple, religious counsellor) is likely a first port of call for many in Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines, given the religiosity data. The app could list "a religious or community leader you trust" as one option, without asking the user's religion. This is an inference; I found no direct help-seeking data for Southeast Asia.
- Because the app never diagnoses, crisis copy should name concrete states ("if you are thinking about ending your life", "if you can't keep yourself safe tonight") and give a local number. It should not use clinical labels.

### Gaps
- **Japan's** official lines (for example the MHLW unified mental health line and Inochi no Denwa), **Vietnam's** national mental health or suicide line, **Indonesia's** official Ministry of Health line (numbers vary by source), and the **Philippines' NCMH short code (1553)** were not verified from primary sources in this session.
- The 12356 rollout status after February 2025 (whether nationwide, and hours) was not confirmed.
- I found no sourced data on who people in Southeast Asia turn to first (family, friends, religious leaders, doctors, online). WHO WPRO and national mental-health surveys (for example Indonesia's I-NAMHS, the Philippines' YAFS) should be checked.
- Legal and safety considerations (for example, data-protection laws such as China's PIPL, Indonesia's PDP Law and Vietnam's Decree 13, which matter for a private reflection app) were outside this research.
