# Tailor the setting, never the person

People in every world region want much the same things from life: enough money to get through the week without fear, people they can count on, safety, meaning (often through faith) and respect. Where someone lives explains only a small part of what they value as a person (country accounts for roughly **2–12% of the differences in people's values**, and world-region groupings explain about **5–7% of research findings**) ([Hanel et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00849/pdf); [Field et al.](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/369762)), but it strongly shapes the setting of their day: whether money arrives as a payslip or as irregular cash, who has a say in big decisions, whether faith runs through ordinary hours, whether the street or the sky is safe, which festivals mark the year, and what help exists when life gets heavy. So The Persona Index should keep one shared core of questions and one warm voice for everyone, and change only the setting: examples, hidden assumptions, an optional pool of context questions, calendar cues, words and crisis lines, with each person's own answers doing the rest. To work out region, it should ask "Where do you live now?" once, with a guess filled in from the phone's time zone, keep the answer on the phone, and never guess ethnicity, religion or nationality, which European regulators treat as sensitive data even when the guess is wrong. Crisis help is where region matters most: who people turn to, which lines exist, when they are open and even whether attempting suicide is still a crime all differ by country, and many of the numbers gathered for this report come from undated directories that must be checked before launch. Finally, the owner's three-way split of Africa, Asia and the West is too coarse: North Africa is usually grouped with the Middle East, "Asia" holds both Japan and Afghanistan, and Latin America fits none of the three, so the working unit should be the country, with six broad regions used only to organise content.

## Region explains about a tenth of what a person values

The most useful finding for an app that wants to "speak to any ethnicity" is that the differences it worries about lie mostly between individuals, not between places. Fischer and Schwartz analysed three large value surveys covering about 169,000 people. They found **"more consensus than disagreement"** across countries. Values tied to independence, closeness to others and feeling capable were rated highly important nearly everywhere, and only conformity looked like a clear marker of culture ([Fischer & Schwartz](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/whence-differences-in-value-priorities-individual-cultural-or-art/)). Stankov split 38 measures of personality, attitudes, values and norms. Individual differences accounted for **68–96% of the variation**, and country and world region together for about 11%. The cultural share was larger for social attitudes and norms (a conservatism measure reached about 25%) than for personality and values, though his sample was test-takers rather than whole populations ([Stankov](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q4597/individual-country-and-societal-cluster-differences-on-measures-of-personality-attitudes-values-and-social-norms)). Field and colleagues ran 25,296 meta-analyses over more than a million research findings. Every major cultural model, the UN's world regions included, explained only about 5–7% of the variation ([Field et al.](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/369762)). The owner's region-based idea is therefore no worse than the elaborate alternatives, but all of them are weak guides to one person. Region tells you a lot about context (economy, institutions, calendar, language, services) and about social norms around family, gender and religion. It tells you little about what a particular person feels.

The world's largest study of wellbeing also overturns the idea that rich means well. The Global Flourishing Study surveyed more than 200,000 people in 22 countries. **High-income countries scored better on financial security and life rating, while middle-income countries scored better on meaning, close relationships and kindness to others**. Indonesia came top, Japan came bottom, and the UK sat among the bottom three ([Harvard T.H. Chan School](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/measuring-a-life-well-lived/); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/health/2025/04/30/indonesians-are-flourishing-people-in-the-uk-germany-and-spain-not-so-much-global-survey-f)). The same pattern appears when the money questions are taken out of the score. India climbs from 19th to 12th, Mexico from 4th to 2nd, and Nigeria ranks 5th ([GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845); [Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/what_african_countries_can_teach_everyone_about_flourishing)). In poorer places, money insecurity drags down lives that are otherwise rich in relationships and purpose. In wealthier places, security sits alongside thinner connection and meaning. Two cautions apply. The study's partners include Baylor's Institute for Studies of Religion, and its lead author discloses consulting fees from Gloo Inc., so its emphasis on religion deserves care ([Springer Nature](https://www.springernature.com/gp/advancing-discovery/springboard/blog/blogposts-sustainability-inclusion/publishing-the-gfs/27774920); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-01254-1)). And psychology as a whole leans heavily on one country: **just over 60% of authors and samples in leading journals are American, while authors and samples from the majority world make up 4–5%** ([Thalmayer et al.](https://commons.clarku.edu/faculty_psychology/687)).

What does tailoring actually achieve? In face-to-face therapy, versions adapted to a person's culture beat the same treatment unadapted, but only modestly (g ≈ 0.52, from just nine direct comparisons). The benefit was far smaller for prevention than for treatment (0.25 against 0.76), which matters for an everyday reflection app that treats no one ([Hall et al.](https://wou.edu/wp/ibarakia/files/2018/08/Hall-et-al.-2016-BT.pdf)). Two ingredients stand out. One is working with the person's own explanation of their trouble, the only significant factor in one analysis. The other is using their own language, reported as twice as effective as English ([Benuto & O'Donohue](https://www.ijpsy.com/volumen15/num3/425/is-culturally-sensitive-cognitive-behavioral-EN.pdf)). For apps the evidence is thinner. A review of 42 adapted digital programmes found most changes were surface-level, **more adaptation did not bring better results**, and no study had compared an adapted and an unadapted version head to head ([Spanhel et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41746-021-00498-1)). Tailoring can also backfire. When Black American adults believed a health message had been chosen for them because of their race, they felt judged, trusted the source less and were less motivated to act; White adults showed no such effect ([SPSP on Derricks & Earl](https://spsp.org/news/character-and-context-blog/derricks-health-messaging-targeted-by-race)). Tailoring earns relevance and trust when it fits a person's circumstances and words. It loses both when the person feels labelled.

## Six regions share the same hungers but live them differently

Each regional picture below covers what people want most, their social, economic and cultural life, and mindset, then shows where the regional average breaks down. Read them as descriptions of settings, not of people. The table gives the short version.

| Region | What weighs most, day to day | What people draw strength from | The default the app must drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sub-Saharan Africa | Cash income, health care, jobs, prices; relatives' requests for money | Family and give-and-take, faith, pride in character (forgiving, helping) | A salary and a boss; "set boundaries with family" |
| Middle East and North Africa | Jobs, the cost of marriage and housing, prices; war and waiting | Family, faith, plans to start a business | A safe home and a predictable week; "it's your life" |
| South Asia | A secure job, exams and results, marriage and dowry, money shared with family | Family duty (often satisfying in itself), faith woven into daily life | "Duty versus your true self"; romance-led relationships |
| East and Southeast Asia | Work stress and bosses, loneliness and exams (East); informal work and migration (Southeast) | Calm moments, ancestors and ritual, faith (strong in Indonesia and the Philippines) | Excitement as the sign of a good day; a secular or Christian default |
| Latin America and the Caribbean | Everyday expenses, safety when moving around, relatives abroad, informal work | Warm family and friendships, faith, personal hope | Therapy first; a car commute; "family is always safe" |
| Western developed countries | Rent and housing, stress, loneliness, caring for parents, pressure to be perfect | A partner and close friends, kindness noticed, faith for many | Moving out as adulthood; living alone as loneliness; "be your best self" |

### Sub-Saharan Africa: four in five went without cash income, yet relationships run deep

Material insecurity shapes most days. In Afrobarometer's latest survey of 38 countries (2024–25), people named **health (38%), unemployment (33%) and the cost of living (23%)** among their country's top problems. In the past year **79% had gone without a cash income at least once**, 65% without medical care, 58% without enough food and 57% without clean water ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AD1191-PAP13-Africans-experience-economic-hardship-despite-signs-of-recovery-Afrobarometer-6june26.pdf)). About nine in ten workers have informal jobs, meaning no contract, payslip or protections, and close to six in ten live below US$4.20 a day ([ILO via Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/16/sub-saharan-africa-labour-force-grew-by-15-4-million-in-2025-ilo-report/)). A third of adults, and 43% of young people, describe themselves as looking for work ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AD1191-PAP13-Africans-experience-economic-hardship-despite-signs-of-recovery-Afrobarometer-6june26.pdf)). Money moves on phones: the region handled **US$1.4 trillion, two-thirds of the world's mobile-money value**, in 2025, though about 75% of accounts sit unused in a given month ([GSMA via Connecting Africa](https://www.connectingafrica.com/mobile-money/-1-4t-flowed-through-mobile-money-in-sub-saharan-africa-in-2025-gsma)). The cravings follow from this: a steady income, affordable care, a business of one's own (seven in ten young people in a 16-country survey plan one) and, for many, the option to leave. **55% of young adults have considered emigrating**, mostly for work ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-09-16-after-the-bell-what-young-africans-want.md); [Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/News-release-More-than-half-of-African-youth-consider-emigration-Afrobarometer-bh-20aug26.pdf)).

Family is the first safety net, and obligation runs both ways. In the past year **47% asked family for help to make ends meet**, 33% asked friends or neighbours and 12% asked religious or community groups ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AD1191-PAP13-Africans-experience-economic-hardship-despite-signs-of-recovery-Afrobarometer-6june26.pdf)). Being asked carries real strain. In Kenya, 93% believed business success brings demands from relatives, and **about one in six would give up as much as 70% of their earnings to keep that income secret**. Some of the most productive entrepreneurs kept their businesses small on purpose ([The African Mirror on Squires](https://theafricanmirror.africa/news/kinship-tax-puts-the-brakes-on-business-kenyan-study-measures-the-impact-of-helping-family/)). Young Black South African professionals describe "black tax", the expectation of supporting extended family, as pushing financial stability ahead of fulfilling work; "cultural duty often overshadowed personal goals" ([Moleleki](https://repository.up.ac.za/items/260b83b3-c498-40e0-bcaa-f37c203fe503/full)). Bride price remains a mark of respect, but South African couples are putting off lobola talks in the cash-tight January after December spending, when school fees and debts fall due ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601160009.html)). Savings clubs are social as much as financial; more than 11 million South Africans are believed to belong to stokvels ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2025/08/14/more-than-11-million-south-africans-are-members-of-stokvels-nasasa)). Faith is near-universal: in older data, **95% identified with a religion** (56% Christian, 34% Muslim), and 69% trusted religious leaders against 52% who trusted their president ([Afrobarometer 2016–18](https://afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/migrated/files/publications/Policy%20papers/ab_r7_dispatchno339_pap12_religion_in_africa.pdf)). Most people back equal rights for women in principle, yet nearly a quarter say husbands or families often stop women from taking paid work ([Further Africa on Afrobarometer](https://furtherafrica.com/2026/06/22/afrobarometer-survey-shows-strong-support-for-womens-rights-across-africa/)).

The mindset is hope held alongside anger at institutions: **49% expect conditions to improve, while 58% say their country is heading the wrong way** ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AD1191-PAP13-Africans-experience-economic-hardship-despite-signs-of-recovery-Afrobarometer-6june26.pdf)). Youth anger is political and collective. Protests over water and power cuts in Madagascar brought down the government in 2025 ([Vision of Humanity](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/the-rise-and-spread-of-gen-z-protests/)), so an app must never recast it as a personal mood problem. Strengths are real. Nigeria's flourishing rests on close relationships, forgiveness and helping others ([Greater Good](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/what_african_countries_can_teach_everyone_about_flourishing)). Help is scarce. Fewer than one in ten people who need mental-health care receive care that has been shown to work, and many families read distress through spiritual and medical explanations held together. Religious leaders in Lagos act as "de facto mental health gatekeepers" ([Adewuya et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4257506383356AA5F8FDAD588F631102)), and Ethiopian holy-water priests mostly combine prayer with clinic care ([Baheretibeb et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10943614)). In South Africa, **men account for nearly 80% of recorded suicides** ([TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-06-08-our-phone-bill-alone-is-r150000-a-month-how-sadag-keeps-sas-only-suicide-crisis-helpline-running/)), so praising "strength" can deepen silence. The regional picture breaks quickly. Going without cash ranges from 96% in Madagascar to 27% in the Seychelles. Nigerians are the gloomiest about their country's direction (93% say it is going the wrong way), while 87% of Gabonese say theirs is on the right track. Outlook differs little between town and countryside (59% against 58%) but sharply by deprivation (67% against 44%) ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/AD1191-PAP13-Africans-experience-economic-hardship-despite-signs-of-recovery-Afrobarometer-6june26.pdf)). Ubuntu, "a person is a person through other persons", comes mainly from Bantu-speaking traditions ([Hofmeyr](https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/aa/v57n1/03.pdf)). Using the word in the Sahel would itself be a stereotype, just as "load-shedding" is a South African word for power cuts.

### Middle East and North Africa: jobs, marriage costs and waiting, now under a 2026 war

Any picture of this region in 2026 starts with the war that began on **28 February 2026**. Iranian strikes hit Gulf states, up to 3.2 million people were temporarily displaced inside Iran, and **830,000 were displaced in Lebanon** by mid-March ([NPR via WRVO](https://www.wrvo.org/2026-03-14/these-are-the-casualties-and-cost-of-the-war-in-iran-2-weeks-into-the-conflict); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649022)). Sudan is the world's largest displacement crisis, with 14.3 million uprooted as of April 2025 ([UNHCR](https://www.unhcr.org/md/en/news/number-people-uprooted-war-shocking-decade-high-levels)). Almost all the survey data below was gathered before this war, so it understates today's strain. The strongest, most consistent want is economic dignity: a decent job, an affordable life and the means to marry. The Arab States have **the world's highest youth unemployment, 26.2% in 2025** (North Africa 22.6%, world 12.4%) ([ILO via Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/global-youth-unemployment-rises-amid-sluggish-job-creation-and-looming-ai-risk-un-labour-agency-says-2026-08-12)). About 32% of young people are not in work, education or training, and **graduates face more unemployment (about 25.6%) than people with only secondary schooling (about 8.6%)** ([Jordan News on the Phenix Center](https://www.jordannews.jo/Section-113/All/Phenix-Center-Youth-Unemployment-in-Arab-States-Is-the-Highest-in-the-World-54316)). Being educated and waiting is a defining life stage. Outside the Gulf, money stress is about survival. In Iran the rial passed two million to the dollar, the minimum wage is about US$82 a month, insulin prices rose 642%, and one mother said her "only concern is making it to the end of the month" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/one-us-dollar-now-costs-2-million-rials-in-iran-and-heres-what-it-can-buy)). Türkiye's 2026 minimum wage sits below the main union's hunger line for a family of four ([Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2025/12/24/turkey-raises-minimum-wage-by-27-percent-drawing-sharp-political-backlash/amp/)). Many families live across borders: Egypt received a record **US$47.3 billion** in remittances, mostly from more than 10 million Egyptians in the Gulf ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/21/egyptian-expat-remittances-hit-record-473-bln-usd-in-fy-2025-2026)). Leaving is a common wish. In 2023–24, **46% of Tunisians wanted to emigrate**, as did 42% of Jordanians, 38% of Lebanese and 35% of Moroccans, but only 16% of Kuwaitis ([Arab Barometer](https://www.arabbarometer.org/?p=14339)).

Family is the unit that makes decisions, and marriage is both a deep wish and a financial ordeal. In Saudi Arabia, marriage costs are estimated at **SR145,000–850,000 against typical monthly salaries of SR4,000–7,000** for young men, and 65.3% of 15- to 34-year-olds are single ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2622218/amp)). A young Syrian called the cost "a nightmare that haunts me every day" ([Enab Baladi](https://english.enabbaladi.net/archives/2025/05/why-do-syrian-youth-shy-away-from-marriage/)). Gender attitudes are **not** steadily liberalising. Arab Barometer finds "a broad shift toward patriarchal norms" since 2021–22. The share of men agreeing that a husband should have the final say ranges from 75% in Kuwait to 48% in Lebanon, and childcare cost is the top childcare problem for working women in Jordan, Tunisia, Lebanon and Kuwait ([Arab Barometer gender report](https://arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/AB8-Gender-Report-EN.pdf)). Religion is polarising rather than simply rising or falling. Between 2010 and 2022 the share calling themselves "religious" rose from 35% to 42%, and the "not religious" share from 8% to 11%, with each country moving its own way ([Baylor Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/trends-among-arab-muslims-point-beyond-revival-versus-secularization)). In mindset, a review of 74 studies found **stigma the most common barrier to seeking help (34 studies)**, followed by low awareness of mental health (28) and fear of gossip and damage to reputation (8), with family, friends and faith preferred ([El Khatib et al.](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)). The "fatalism" label is contested. Islamic and Ottoman thought pairs God's decree with human effort and responsibility, and one historian warns that dismissing faith-based acceptance reflects a secular assumption ([Menchinger](https://ghost.ims.forth.gr/wp-content/uploads/B_1_Acaib_Volume_2_Menchinger-Revisiting-Turkish-Fatalism.pdf)). Agency is plain to see: 42% of young Arabs aimed to start a business within five years ([Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/asda-bcw-unveils-results-for-its-15th-annual-youth-survey/)).

The regional label hides huge gaps. In the 2026 World Happiness Report, the UAE ranks 21st and Saudi Arabia 22nd in the world, but national averages hide migrant workers, who make up much of the Gulf's population ([AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/opinion/development/2026/07/the-gulfs-happiness-test-strong-scores-before-a-geopolitical-shock/)). Morocco ranks 112th, with Egypt, Jordan and Lebanon below it ([Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/03/283596/morocco-ranks-112th-in-2026-world-happiness-report/)). Every region-wide assumption fails somewhere. Türkiye, Iran, Kurds and Amazigh are not Arab. Christians, Druze, Yazidis, Jews and a growing non-religious youth minority are not Muslim or observant. Gulf citizens are not poor, and Gulf migrant workers are not rich.

### South Asia: security first, with family deciding and exams as flashpoints

Most of the best data for this region covers India only, and the picture for Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Nepal is thinner. Life ratings sit low: in the 2026 World Happiness Report **Nepal ranks 99th, Pakistan 104th, India 116th, Bangladesh 127th and Sri Lanka 134th**, with Afghanistan last ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/world-happiness-report-2026/print_manually)). The Indian flourishing data show the pain is material, not a lack of meaning. India ranks 19th of 22 with financial security counted and 12th without it ([GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)). The central craving is security. **36% of young Indians named unemployment as the country's most critical problem, up 18 points since 2016**, and 60% favour government jobs, yet only 6% of working young people hold one ([CivilsDaily on Lokniti-CSDS](https://civilsdaily.com/?p=736321)). Young people make up 83% of India's unemployed, two-thirds of them have secondary education or more, nearly 90% of all work is informal, and **48.4% of young women are not in work, education or training, against 9.8% of young men** ([People Matters on ILO-IHD](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/over-80-of-indias-unemployed-are-youth-ilo-40747)). South Asian workers report the world's highest daily stress and anger at work ([Gulf News on Gallup](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/half-of-indian-employees-want-to-quit-jobs-30-face-daily-stress-gallup-report-reveals-1.500114812)). Migration holds households up. Pakistan received a record **US$38.3 billion** in remittances in 2024–25 ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=204436)), Bangladesh US$35.6 billion in 2025–26 ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=298820)), and Nepal's are reported at 35.8% of its economy, a figure higher than usually quoted and not cross-checked ([Annapurna Express](https://theannapurnaexpress.com/story/67358/)). Sri Lanka still carries its 2022 crash: poverty is 16.9% against 11.5% in 2019, and real wages remain about 12% below 2019 levels ([Daily FT on the World Bank](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Poverty-declining-but-still-above-pre-crisis-levels-World-Bank/44-798344)).

Marriage is a family project. In older national data, **95% of Indian women's marriages involved their family**, though 55% of women had some say ([NCAER](https://ndic.ncaer.org/how-much-agency-do-indian-women-have-in-marriage-choices/)). Dowry was paid in 95% of rural marriages between 1960 and 2008, often worth several years of household income ([World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/developmenttalk/evolution-dowry-rural-india-1960-2008)). Indians hold old and new norms at the same time. About nine in ten agree "a wife must always obey her husband", yet 62% say both parents should share childcare, and 63% say sons should mainly perform last rites ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)). Caste shapes social life without being named: **70% say most or all of their close friends share their caste**, and 64% say it is very important to stop women marrying outside it ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)). In a striking reversal, married Indians report *lower* flourishing than single ones, one of only two countries where this happens ([GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)).

The key mindset finding is that duty is not simply a cage. **Indians report high satisfaction both from meeting expectations and from acting on their own choices**, whereas Americans mostly from the latter ([Miller et al.](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617706099/)). Faith is part of the background of life: 97% believe in God and 77% of both Hindus and Muslims believe in karma ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/06/29/key-findings-about-religion-in-india/)). Stigma attaches to the whole family and to its marriage prospects, and distress is often voiced through the body (headaches, "tension", no sleep), according to an older review ([Lauber & Rössler](https://sonar.ch/global/documents/32237)). Exams are flashpoints: **14,488 Indian students died by suicide in 2024** ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/education/rising-student-deaths-by-suicide-what-the-2024-ncrb-report-shows)). The regional picture breaks along many lines. Agreement that a wife must obey is 75% in India's south and 94% in the Hindi-speaking north ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2022/03/02/how-indians-view-gender-roles-in-families-and-society/)). Nepal's young people forced a change of government after protests in September 2025 ([Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/gen-z-protest-what-changed-nepal.html)). Digital payments are everyday life in India, at 24.5 billion UPI transactions in August 2026 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/upi-transactions-near-record-level-of-298-lakh-cr-in-aug-volume-at-all-time-high-of-2451-bn)), but that does not carry over to the rest of the region. Tamil and Bangla each span two countries, so language never reveals nationality.

### East and Southeast Asia: stress and loneliness peak in wealthy East Asia, meaning and faith in the Southeast

This region contains the extremes of the flourishing study. **Indonesia ranked 1st and the Philippines 3rd once money was set aside, while Japan came last on both measures**, and Japanese respondents were the least likely to say they had a close friend ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/04/more-proof-that-money-isnt-everything)). Life ratings run from Taiwan at 26th in the world to Myanmar at 129th. Vietnam (45th) and Thailand (52nd) rank above Japan (61st), China (65th) and South Korea (67th), and Indonesia sits 87th on life rating despite topping the flourishing study ([Atlas & Boots, reproducing WHR 2026](https://www.atlasandboots.com/?p=12864)). Daily strain splits the region: **46% of East Asians report a lot of daily stress against 25% in Southeast Asia**, East Asia has the third-highest daily loneliness of ten world regions (23%), and Southeast Asians are the world's most optimistic about finding a job (64%) ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx)). The cravings that recur are belonging, security and meaning, more than excitement or freedom.

Family obligation is renegotiated rather than rejected. During Chuseok 2026, young Koreans made more than 67,000 joke "nagging menus" that put a price on relatives' questions (marriage 650,000 won, children 950,000 won). **48.5% named intrusive questions as the holiday custom they most wanted to change**, and the share planning to see relatives fell from 70% to 63% ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/chuseok-nagging-menus-put-a-price-on-prying-as-younger-koreans-seek-to-celebrate-without-stress/12886656)). Work and study press hard. **68.3% of Japanese workers report strong work-related stress**, and problems with supervisors make up over 60% of the relationship-related mental-health compensation cases ([Japan's labour ministry](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11200000/001684286.pdf)). Korean families spend a record 604,000 won a month on private tutoring for each child who has it ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/private-education-spending-for-grade-school-students-hits-record-high-in-2025/12560246)). Ageing brings isolation: **76,941 people died alone at home in Japan in 2025**, nearly 80% of them aged 65 or over, most of them men ([Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02768/)). "No religion" does not mean secular. Majorities in Hong Kong (61%) and Korea (52%) have no religious affiliation, yet roughly half or more in every place surveyed made offerings to ancestors, and 96% of Vietnamese burned incense for them ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/06/17/6-facts-about-religion-and-spirituality-in-east-asian-societies/)). Meanwhile nearly all Indonesians say religion is very important ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/08/09/where-is-the-most-religious-place-in-the-world/markdown)). Economic insecurity takes different forms. China's youth unemployment was **18.0% for 16- to 24-year-olds in September 2026, against 3.9% for 30- to 59-year-olds** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/china/youth-unemployment-rate)), and "lying flat" works more as a temporary way of coping than a lifestyle ([The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/editorial/article/69761/Lying-flat-stigma-easing-in-HK-but-not-as-long-term-lifestyle)). In Southeast Asia, informal work dominates (Indonesia 81%, Vietnam 68%, Thailand 63%; via a secondary table of ILO data) ([Wikipedia, citing ILOSTAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_informal_employment_rate)). Migration is a household strategy: there are 2.19 million Filipino overseas workers, 57% of them women ([GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/pinoyabroad/dispatch/969755/ofws-grew-to-2-19m-in-2024-psa/story/)).

The best-known mindset finding needs careful handling. People in East Asian settings tend to **value calm, peaceful good feelings more, and excitement less**, than European Americans, and cultures differ more in how people want to feel than in how they actually feel ([Psi Chi interview with Tsai](https://www.psichi.org/page/242EyeWinter19Tsai)). A 2025 analysis of 124 datasets confirms this, but it also finds differences among East Asian groups and by class, gender and age, and finds Americans valuing calm more over time ([Tsai et al.](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280)). None of it was tested in Southeast Asia. A 55-culture study likewise found the simple split between "independent West" and "interdependent East" does not hold ([Vignoles et al.](https://archium.ateneo.edu/psychology-faculty-pubs/143)). On seeking help, a review of Chinese adults found that **self-reliance, preferring other sources of help, not feeling a need and cost** came before stigma ([Shi et al.](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00442/text)). In Malaysia, calls to the government helpline more than doubled in a year, from 42,990 to 90,981 ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/07/09/malaysias-mental-health-helpline-received-more-than-230000-calls-since-launch-says-health-ministry)).

### Latin America and the Caribbean: warm lives, unsafe streets and half of work off the books

This region reports far more good feeling than its income, crime and governance would predict. **Costa Rica ranks 4th in the world** in the 2026 World Happiness Report and Mexico 12th, though Venezuela sits 80th. Young people's life ratings here rose by 0.26 points over the past two decades while falling 0.81 points in North America, Australia and New Zealand ([Happier Lives Institute](https://www.happierlivesinstitute.org/2026/03/19/world-happiness-report-2026-full-country-rankings-our-favourite-finding/)). In 2023, six of the world's ten highest scorers on Gallup's daily positive-experience measure were Latin American ([BusinessWire on Gallup](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625405488/en/Latin-American-Countries-Continue-to-Lead-the-World-in-Positive-Emotions)). The best explanation is close, person-to-person relationships. In surveys in Mexico, Costa Rica and Colombia, **86% said "in my family we help and support each other a lot", against 67% in the US** ([Rojas](https://upaep.mx/ciie/images/2018/presentaciones/Mariano_Rojas_High_Happiness_in_Latin_America.pdf)). Money is the weak spot. Mexico rises from 4th to 2nd in the flourishing study once financial security is removed ([GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)), and Argentines score far worse than the global average on worry about everyday expenses (3.96 against 5.59) ([Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)). People rate their own prospects above their country's. Personal economic optimism hit a record 52% in 2024, even as **33% said they or a relative had been a victim of crime** (51% in Ecuador) ([CNN Chile on Latinobarómetro](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/latinobarometro-america-latina-muestra-signos-de-recuperacion-democratica_20241220/)).

Daily economic life is irregular and unequal. Poverty fell to a record low of 25.5% in 2024, but the richest tenth take 34.2% of income and the poorest tenth 1.7% ([Cadena 3 on ECLAC](https://www.cadena3.com/noticia/mundo/menos-pobreza-en-america-latina-pero-crece-la-desigualdad-y-baja-movilidad-social_491686)). **47% of workers are informal**, ranging from 82% in Bolivia to 22% in Uruguay ([BioBioChile on the ILO](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/economia/actualidad-economica/2025/12/12/la-informalidad-laboral-afecta-a-casi-una-de-dos-personas-en-america-latina-y-el-caribe-segun-la-oit.shtml); [ABC Color](https://www.abc.com.py/internacionales/2026/07/30/latinoamerica-experimenta-reduccion-de-desempleo-y-de-informalidad-en-2025-senala-la-oit/)). Insecurity is felt on the move. Gangs extort bus routes in Guatemala, extortion has caused driver strikes in Lima, and Ecuador's murder rate hit a record 50.9 per 100,000 in 2025. In Chile, 77% fear being assaulted although its rate is 5.4 ([InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/news/insight-crime-2025-homicide-round-up/); [CNN Chile](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/latinobarometro-america-latina-muestra-signos-de-recuperacion-democratica_20241220/)). Families stretch across borders. A **1% US tax on cash-funded remittances** began on 1 January 2026 ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/us-tax-remittances-goes-effect-first-day-2026-3794166)). Venezuelans are weighing whether to return after Maduro's capture on 3 January 2026 ([Vanguardia](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/02/23/migrantes-venezolanos-en-colombia-dudan-regresar-a-su-pais-tras-captura-de-maduro/)). Cuba lost 245,264 people to emigration in 2025 amid blackouts ([Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/mundo/america-latina/2026/07/21/perdida-de-poblacion-en-cuba-por-migracion-masiva/3606462)). Family is central but not always safe: **at least 3,828 women were killed in femicides in 2024**, about 11 a day, mostly by current or former partners ([El Heraldo on ECLAC](https://www.elheraldo.hn/mundo/mujeres-victimas-femicidio-2024-latinoamerica-segun-cepal-NI28314900)). Religion is trusted but diversifying. In Brazil, Catholics fell to 56.7% and evangelicals rose to 26.9%, and 14.3% of 20- to 24-year-olds have no religion ([DGABC on the IBGE census](https://www.dgabc.com.br/Noticia/4238463/ibge-catolicos-caem-evangelicos-e-sem-religiao-crescem-no-brasil)).

Warmth can hide distress. The Americas are the **only world region where suicide rates are still rising, up 17% since 2000** (a figure that includes the US and Canada) ([PAHO](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas)), with a 38% rise among 10- to 24-year-olds ([PAHO](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/20-5-2026-suicidio-entre-adolescentes-adultos-jovenes-aumenta-americas-ops-alerta-sobre)). Before the pandemic, about three in four people with moderate-to-severe disorders received no treatment ([Kohn et al.](https://scielosp.org/pdf/rpsp/2018.v42/e165/en)). The regional picture breaks in many ways. On older census-based estimates, about one in five Latin Americans is of African descent and about one in twelve Indigenous, with Bolivia at 62% Indigenous ([UNFPA](https://www.unfpa.org/es/updates/poner-fin-al-racismo-es-tarea-de-todos-y-constituye-un-aspecto-central-en-la-construcci%C3%B3n); [Teletica on ECLAC](https://www.teletica.com/internacional/pueblos-indigenas-crecen-y-ya-son-83-de-poblacion-de-america-latina_66243)). Brazil speaks Portuguese, and much of the Caribbean English, French or Kreyòl. Jamaica was still recovering from Hurricane Melissa six months on ([Jamaica Gleaner](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20260428/2000-jamaicans-still-without-power-six-months-after-hurricane-melissa)). Intentions to emigrate have fallen back to pre-pandemic levels ([Vanderbilt](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/cgd/resource/2026-americasbarometer/)).

### Western developed countries: secure on paper, short on company and meaning

The West is materially secure but short on connection, and its young people are the clearest warning. Finland tops the 2026 World Happiness Report for the ninth year, but the US ranks 23rd, Canada 25th and the UK 29th, and no English-speaking country makes the top ten. **Life ratings of under-25s in the US, Canada, Australia and New Zealand fell by almost a full point out of ten in a decade**, while young people elsewhere grew happier ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)). In the US, 18- to 29-year-olds score 5.68 on flourishing against 8.06 for people in their sixties, the widest age gap of any country studied ([Yahoo News on GFS](https://www.yahoo.com/news/kids-aren-t-flourishing-global-150210328.html)). The craving the data point to is belonging and meaning more than further security. **Belief that others are kind predicts happiness more than a higher salary does**, and people underestimate how kind their communities are ([Khaleej Times on WHR 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)).

Social life has thinned. 16% of Americans feel lonely all or most of the time (22% of under-50s), and only 29% see close friends in person weekly, while 61% text them ([Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)). Across the OECD, daily contact with friends among 16- to 24-year-olds fell from 44% to 36% between 2015 and 2022, and **men and young people saw some of the largest falls** ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/social-connections-and-loneliness-in-oecd-countries_6df2d6a0-en/full-report/executive-summary_7f72ab4a.html); [Yahoo News on OECD](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/why-loneliness-rise-among-young-101120350.html)). Roughly one in four Americans ate every meal alone on a given day in 2023 ([Khaleej Times on WHR 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/americas/us-happiness-sinks-as-more-americans-eat-alone-reveals-survey)). In North America and Australia and New Zealand, moving from always eating alone to sharing most meals goes with a wellbeing gain comparable to doubling income ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/why-sharing-meals-can-make-people-happier-what-evidence-from-142-countries-shows-252352)). Caring is a huge hidden role: **63 million Americans, nearly one in four adults, care for a relative**, and nearly a third are also raising children ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)). Housing is the central economic worry. Half of US renters are cost-burdened ([Harvard JCHS](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/press-releases/new-report-highlights-unease-housing-market-amid-worsening-affordability-crisis)), and in the UK 91% of adults reported high or extreme pressure last year, with **51% of 18- to 24-year-old workers taking extra hours or a second job** because of living costs ([Mental Health UK](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)). US and Canadian workers report the world's highest daily stress (50%), while Europeans are the least engaged but less stressed ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/697904/state-of-the-global-workplace-2026.aspx)).

In mindset, the West's particular burden is pressure to be perfect. **Young people's sense of what their parents expect of them rose about 40% between 1989 and 2021** in the US, Canada and the UK ([APA on Curran & Hill](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/03/parental-expectations-perfectionism)), and 39.7% of US high-schoolers reported persistent sadness in 2023, including 52.6% of girls ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/su/su7304a9.htm)). Heavy, feed-driven social media is linked to lower youth wellbeing, while light use, under an hour a day, goes with the best ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2026-03-19-world-happiness-report-2026-shows-complex-global-picture-social-media-and-happiness)). Only 19% of Americans would turn to a mental-health professional for emotional support ([Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)), and **more than one in three people in a UK study had already used AI chatbots for mental-health support** ([Mental Health UK](https://changemh.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Mental-Health-UK_The-Burnout-Report-2026_FAW.pdf)). The Western picture breaks too. Young adults leave home at about 21 in Finland and after 30 in Spain, Italy and Greece ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250923-1)). Americans are not post-religious: 62% are Christian and 44% pray daily ([Pew Trust](https://www.pew.org/en/trust/archive/summer-2025/the-decline-of-christianity-has-slowed)). Across 30 countries, 57% of Gen Z men but 36% of Gen Z women say equality efforts have gone so far that men now face discrimination ([GZERO](https://www.gzeromedia.com/news/analysis/the-gen-z-gender-divide)). And many Western residents live in another region's story. **45% of people of African descent in 13 EU countries faced racial discrimination in five years, and only 9% reported it** ([FRA](https://fra.europa.eu/pl/news/2023/black-people-eu-face-ever-more-racism)), while US$656 billion flowed home to lower-income countries in remittances in 2023 ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/06/26/remittances-slowed-in-2023-expected-to-grow-faster-in-2024)).

## Speaking to any ethnicity works by removing defaults, not adding labels

Across all six regions, what makes a question feel foreign is usually a hidden default, not a missing cultural reference. The defaults that fail most often all come from one narrow kind of life. They assume a salary and a boss, which fails where about nine in ten workers in Sub-Saharan Africa and India, and nearly half in Latin America, work informally. They assume a partner chosen through dating, where 95% of Indian women's marriages involved family. They assume a private room, steady power and data, a weekend, and secular or Christian framing. They assume therapy as the obvious next step, even with 1.4 mental-health workers per 100,000 people in Africa, a figure reported second-hand ([Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/shortage-of-mental-health-specialists-puts-pressure-on-african-economies/)). They assume "set boundaries with family" is the cure, though one Kenyan study's model found that the insurance value of sharing with relatives outweighs its costs ([VoxDev](https://voxdev.org/node/62811)). And they assume excitement is the mark of a good day and that treating yourself is self-care. Removing these defaults does more for a user in Lagos, Lahore or Lima than any regional persona could, because it stops the app speaking to them as someone they are not.

What replaces those defaults is a set of open, universal slots that every person fills with their own life. "Whatever brings money in" works for a salaried nurse and a market trader. "Who did you help today, and who helped you?" honours give-and-take in both directions, which matters where 47% asked family for help in a year. "What was in your hands today, and what wasn't?" fits a Muslim user's trust in God, a Buddhist's acceptance and a secular user's sense of control, without asking which they are. "Something you did because it was expected: how did it feel afterwards?" must allow "good" as an answer, because duty can be satisfying ([Miller et al.](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617706099/)). A calm moment and a moment that gave you energy should both count as a good day. Waiting, for a job, papers, a result or news of family, should be treated as an active part of life rather than idleness. Money should be treated as something shared, sent and received, and family should be allowed to be both love and weight, since femicide and family shame sit alongside the warmth. Youth anger at governments should never be recast as a mood to manage, and the app should never ask about protests or parties.

The single best-supported deep adaptation is to **let the person's own explanation lead** ([Benuto & O'Donohue](https://www.ijpsy.com/volumen15/num3/425/is-culturally-sensitive-cognitive-behavioral-EN.pdf)). If someone writes "my spirit is low", "tension", "nervios" or "heavy", the insight card mirrors those words and never translates them into "depression" or "anxiety". Body words (tired, tight chest, no sleep) are a gentle way in where distress is often voiced physically. The app's existing "That's me / Partly / Not me" taps are the right tool for regional tailoring: country sets a starting mix, the person's taps take over within days, and a small line ("because you said you live in Kenya") plus a "this doesn't fit me" option shows the person why a question appeared. This matches the evidence on labelling: content that fits a chosen context helps, and content that feels assigned to a group hurts.

Diaspora and minority users need particular care, because where they live is not always their cultural reference point. Second-generation Hispanic and Asian Americans are twice as likely as immigrants to feel "typical American", yet many still describe themselves first by their family's country, and only 41% of second-generation Asian Americans speak their family's language well ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2013/02/07/chapter-3-identity/)). Because discrimination is common and rarely reported, prompts such as "Did anyone treat you unfairly today?" or "Was there a moment you felt you had to prove you belonged?" should be open to everyone, without naming any group. Insight cards should not celebrate "the best of both worlds" by default. Holding two cultures goes with better adjustment on average, but a meta-analysis found no benefit in African-heritage samples and a negative link in Indigenous ones ([Nguyen & Benet-Martínez](https://psychology.fullerton.edu/amnguyen/pdf/jccp2013.pdf)). Weekly mirrors should never read a quiet week as low effort, since it can reflect fasting, caring, insecurity or a power cut. Skipped days must cost nothing, and tone must never grade the person. In one tested parenting app, a corrective tone made users feel they were "parenting incorrectly" ([Naderbagi et al.](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e55130)).

## Ask once, keep it on the phone, and never guess identity

There are three ways to learn where someone lives. The phone's time zone and language settings can be read on the phone with no network call, which makes them the most private, but they are unreliable. Many phones ship with US English settings, one time zone covers several countries, and travellers and diaspora users keep home settings. These are practical observations rather than measured findings. Looking up the person's internet address is more accurate at country level: one vendor claims **over 99% for countries but only 20–75% for cities**, and it fails for anyone using a VPN ([MaxMind](https://www.maxmind.com/en/geoip/ip-geolocation-accuracy)). Using a third-party lookup also sends the person's address to that vendor, which goes against the app's promise that internet addresses are never stored. Asking is the most accurate and most respectful option. The recommended pattern is a single question, "Where do you live now?", with a guess filled in from the time zone, a one-line reason ("so your questions and local helplines fit where you are"), a "Prefer not to say" choice that keeps universal content and a global helpline directory, and a setting to change it at any time.

The law points the same way. Article 9 of Europe's data protection law (GDPR) prohibits processing data that reveals racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs or political opinions, unless the person gives explicit consent ([GDPR Article 9](https://gdpr-text.com/en/read/article-9/)). European regulators say that sorting users into such categories from observed behaviour counts as processing this sensitive data **whether or not the sorting is correct, and whatever the category is called** ([EDPB guidance, quoted in the same commentary](https://gdpr-text.com/en/read/article-9/)). The EU's top court has extended this to data "liable indirectly to reveal" sensitive traits ([Covington on CJEU C-184/20](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/)). For the app, that means never inferring ethnicity, religion or nationality from a name, a language, an answer pattern or a time zone. It also means treating any optional "places that feel like home" field, or a choice to see fasting-month prompts, as potentially revealing origin or faith. Both should be optional, explained, deletable and kept only on the phone, subject to a lawyer's reading. Country of residence is ordinary personal data, but it should still be kept coarse (country, never city). Laws outside the EU, such as India's, Brazil's, China's and South Africa's, were not reviewed for this report.

The app's existing design makes private regional tailoring straightforward. According to the app's own data map, each day's edition is public content downloaded by every phone, and the phone records which questions it showed. Regional wording, the context pool and the helpline list can therefore travel inside that public content, with the phone choosing locally, so the server learns nothing from what is downloaded. The country setting belongs in phone storage beside the existing settings: sealed inside the encrypted backup if the person turns it on, never sent otherwise, and shown on the "What I keep" screen. One detail matters. Votes and mirror rows record question IDs, so regional versions of a question should share the same question and option IDs, with only the words changing on the phone. Any question someone sees only because of a choice that hints at faith, origin or country should reach the server the way the app's intimate questions already do, as a sealed marker with no question or answer, or not at all. Language should be a separate choice from country, because Tamil, Bangla, Arabic, Spanish and English each cross many borders.

## Crisis help must follow the country, and most numbers still need checking

Who people turn to first differs by place, so the app should offer a ladder rather than a single door. In Sub-Saharan Africa, family comes first for practical help, and religious leaders are often the first stop for distress: about 26% of Nigerians with mental illness are reported to see one before any formal service ([Adewuya et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4257506383356AA5F8FDAD588F631102)). In Arab countries family, friends and faith come first, and fear of gossip keeps people away from formal help ([El Khatib et al.](https://bmcpsychiatry.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12888-023-04827-4)). Chinese adults lean on self-reliance ([Shi et al.](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00442/text)). **Americans turn first to a partner (74%), a mother (48%) or a friend (46%)** ([Pew](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/social-trends/2025/01/16/emotional-well-being/print)). The universal ladder runs from someone you trust (if they feel safe to you), to a community, faith or health worker you already know, to a free confidential line for your country, to emergency services. Today the app shows one quiet line ("If things feel heavy, talking to someone you trust, or to a helpline, can help.") with a link to Find A Helpline's front page. That is the right tone. What needs to change is the destination, which should become the person's own country's line.

What varies by country is concrete. Opening hours differ: Bangladesh's Kaan Pete Roi runs **3pm to 3am** ([Kaan Pete Roi](https://kaanpeteroi.org/helpline-service/)) and Malaysia's HEAL line 8am to midnight ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/07/09/malaysias-mental-health-helpline-received-more-than-230000-calls-since-launch-says-health-ministry)). Capacity differs: **Korea's 24-hour 109 line answered only 47% of calls** in early 2026 and uses Lifeline Korea for overflow at night ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10759801)). Channels differ: Brazil and Panama have WhatsApp services ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/lac/Ayuda-SaludMental)). Some lines are local only, such as Colombia's 106, which is free only from Bogotá ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/lac/Ayuda-SaludMental)). Funding differs: South Africa's SADAG runs what it calls the country's only suicide crisis line with no government money ([TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-06-08-our-phone-bill-alone-is-r150000-a-month-how-sadag-keeps-sas-only-suicide-crisis-helpline-running/)). The law differs too. **Kenya's High Court struck down the offence of attempted suicide in January 2025, but repeal was still pending in parliament** as of late 2025 ([IASP](https://www.iasp.info/?p=72569)). Ghana is reported to have repealed its equivalent law ([The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/latest-kenya-times-news/attempted-suicide-is-no-longer-a-crime-in-kenya-court-rules/)). Other countries are unconfirmed. Crisis wording should therefore stress that lines are confidential, and the app must never share anything automatically. In war zones (nine of the 22 countries in WHO's Eastern Mediterranean region are fragile or conflict-affected), primary care and humanitarian services can be more reachable than a hotline, so the app must not promise that a line will answer ([WHO EMRO](https://www.emro.who.int/media/news/world-mental-health-day-invisible-wounds-across-the-eastern-mediterranean.html)). Where phones are shared, the help screen must be discreet, with no notification that names mental health.

Find A Helpline, run by ThroughLine, is the natural source. The International Association for Suicide Prevention points to it as its main directory ([IASP](https://www.iasp.info/?p=10328)). It says helplines verify their own details and that its data is maintained daily, and it lists Google, OpenAI and Anthropic (the company behind the assistant writing this report) among its partners ([Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/about)). Its own pages variously claim 100+, 170+ and 178 countries, and no independent audit was found ([ThroughLine](https://www.throughlinecare.com/)). The safest design routes by the person's confirmed country, sending only a country code. It keeps a refreshed copy of the list on the phone, so the help screen works offline and opening it reveals nothing. It shows each number with its hours, channels and the date it was last checked, and always gives the local emergency number as a fallback. Crisis wording should name concrete states ("if you are thinking about ending your life", "if you can't keep yourself safe tonight") rather than diagnoses. It should say plainly that the app is not a crisis service, which matters where many people already lean on AI for support. ## What changes by region, what never does, and what to check before launch

The work falls into a clear order. First, rewrite the universal core to remove Western defaults, which helps every user at once. Second, add the country setting, kept on the phone. Third, connect verified crisis lines. Fourth, build the optional regional context pool. Fifth, test it with local people. The three tables below are meant to be used as working checklists. The app itself carries no citations; keep this report as the team's source sheet.

**What changes by region**

| What changes | How it changes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Examples inside insight cards | Names, foods, places, family shapes and kinds of work rotate to fit the confirmed country. No card ever says "people like you" | A card in Lagos might mention a customer who didn't pay; in Seoul, a relative's question at Chuseok; in Madrid, still living at home at 29 without calling it failure |
| Assumptions switched off | Default words change wherever the evidence shows the default fails | "Whatever brings money in" instead of "your job"; "people at home" instead of "your partner"; no weekend, car commute or private room assumed |
| Which optional questions are offered | Country sets the starting mix of an optional context pool. The person's "That's me / Not me" taps then take over, and anyone can switch any pool on. The core questions stay the same for everyone | Sub-Saharan Africa: a relative asking for money, a slow or good day for income, a savings-group turn, a power or water cut. Middle East and North Africa: waiting for a job reply or papers, a cost carried for someone's wedding. South Asia: a relative asking about marks, job or marriage, "what will people think", money sent home. East Asia: a calm moment, holiday questions, a moment with the boss, honouring someone who has died. Latin America: changing route to feel safer, someone far away, what gave you strength. West: a meal shared or eaten alone, a friend you saw rather than messaged, where you felt you had to be perfect, someone leaning on you |
| Calendar cues | Festivals and seasons the person chooses to see, used only to time gentle prompts, never to guess faith | Ramadan and Eid, Lunar New Year, Chuseok, Tết, Diwali, Nowruz, Christmas; South Africa's January squeeze; India's exam results season; reversed seasons south of the equator |
| Words and language | Language is chosen separately from country; local words appear only where they are truly local | "Load-shedding" in South Africa only; Tamil serves India and Sri Lanka; Hinglish and Roman Urdu as options |
| Crisis help | The country's checked line, its hours and channels, a backup, the emergency number, and wording on confidentiality where the law is harsh | Tele-MANAS 14416 in India; *4141 in Chile; 109 plus Lifeline Korea in Korea; WhatsApp lines in Brazil and Panama; humanitarian services in war zones |

**What never changes**

| Rule | Why it holds everywhere |
|---|---|
| Never ask or guess ethnicity, religion, nationality, caste or politics | Guessing counts as processing sensitive data even when wrong; these identities carry legal and safety risk in several countries; being labelled lowers trust |
| Never compare a person with anyone, including "people in your country" | It is the app's promise, and comparison through passive social media is linked to lower wellbeing |
| Never hand out solutions; reflect, ask, and let any step be the person's own and free | Money for therapy, gyms or treats is scarce for most of the world; a corrective tone backfired in a tested app |
| Never use clinical labels | Stigma is the top barrier in Arab studies and affects whole families in South Asia; mirroring the person's own words is what helps |
| The same core questions for everyone | Independence, closeness to others and feeling capable matter nearly everywhere |
| Private by default, with region kept on the phone | People pay to hide income from relatives in Kenya, fear of gossip blocks help in Arab countries, and stigma harms marriage prospects in South Asia |
| Skipping costs nothing | Power and water cuts, war, fasting, caring and shared phones all break streaks |
| Family is allowed to be both love and weight | Support from relatives is insurance as well as strain, and family is also where much violence happens |
| Calm and energy both count as a good day | People differ in which good feelings they seek, within regions as much as between them |
| Help is always one tap away, offline and discreet | Crises don't wait for a signal, and a visible "mental health" notification can do harm on a shared phone |

**Open checks before launch**

| Check | Why it matters | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| Verify every helpline with its operator: number, hours, languages, cost, and text or WhatsApp options | Most numbers came from undated directories. One still lists Korea's old numbers, another gave a malformed Sri Lankan number, and the US 988 sub-options listed on Wikipedia should be checked as some have reportedly changed | Only Bangladesh, Chile, India (Tele-MANAS), Lebanon and Ghana come from an operator, a ministry, a recent report or a verified directory entry |
| Fill the gaps | No line was found for Jordan, Iraq, Palestine, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, the smaller Gulf states, Argentina, Venezuela, Haiti, Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Guatemala, Japan (official), Vietnam or Indonesia (official), and South Africa's SADAG number was not captured | Until filled, show the emergency number and a calm note |
| Collect the emergency number for every country | It is the fallback everywhere | Only Ghana's 193 was confirmed |
| Find where attempting suicide is still a crime | It shapes confidentiality wording, and whether suggesting emergency services is safe | Kenya's offence was struck down in January 2025 but repeal is pending; Ghana is reported repealed; other countries are unconfirmed |
| Get a legal reading on the country setting, any "places that feel like home" field and calendar choices linked to faith | EU guidance treats inferences and indirect hints about origin or religion as sensitive data | Keep them on the phone for now. Ask counsel about India's DPDP Act, Brazil's LGPD, China's PIPL, South Africa's POPIA, Indonesia's PDP Law and Vietnam's Decree 13, none of which were reviewed |
| Check California's SB 243 if insight cards are written by AI | It is believed to require crisis-referral steps for companion chatbots | Not verified |
| Agree terms and coverage with Find A Helpline / ThroughLine | Coverage claims vary from 100+ to 178 countries, with no independent audit | Anthropic is listed as a partner; worth noting in any decision |
| Test the draft questions with local people in the main languages | None of the example prompts has been tried with real users | Start with the largest markets and languages |
| Confirm calendar and weekend dates | Lunar festival dates move each year and weekends differ by country | Ramadan 2027 and weekend days were not verified |
| Update the data map, storage registry and "What I keep" screen | The app promises to show everything it keeps | The country setting needs a new phone-only key |

## Conclusion

The owner set out to identify regions and the issues that affect them, and the research supports doing that, though for a narrower purpose than expected. Region tells the app what kind of morning a person wakes up to: informal cash or a payslip, a full household or a flat alone, a fasting month or a Lunar New Year, a free 24-hour line or none at all. That knowledge is valuable precisely because the app can use it without saying anything about who the person is. The moments that make people feel seen turn up in every region: a family that is both love and weight, money that belongs to more than one person, waiting that is not idleness, calm that counts as happiness, strength drawn from faith or from somewhere else. Writing those into the shared core is how the app will speak to any ethnicity. Separate regional versions of the app would not do that.

Two further points follow. Privacy is more than a legal duty here; it is what makes honest reflection possible. In Kenya, people give up income to hide it from relatives. In Arab countries, fear of gossip keeps people from help. In South Asia, stigma follows a whole family into marriage talks. An app that keeps everything on the phone offers exactly what these settings lack, and it should say so plainly. And because region explains so little about any one person, its role should shrink as the person answers. After a week of "That's me" and "Not me", the person's own pattern should outweigh their country's, leaving region to do what it does best: choose the right calendar, the right words and the right number to call.
