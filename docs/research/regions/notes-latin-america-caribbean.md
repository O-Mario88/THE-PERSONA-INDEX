# What matters to people in Latin America and the Caribbean (as of October 2026)

Scope note: these notes are for a private self-reflection app. It asks daily questions about real moments, shows short insight cards, offers one small optional step, and gives weekly mirrors. It never compares people or diagnoses, and it never asks about ethnicity, religion or party. Dates of the underlying data are marked; anything before 2024 is flagged **[older data]**. Each section keeps sourced facts, my own inferences and the gaps apart.

---

## 1. What do people most want for their lives, what do the surveys show, and why does the region report high positive emotion despite hardship?

### Takeaway
On positive emotion and life evaluation, Latin America scores well above what its income, governance and crime levels would predict. The best-supported explanation is dense, warm, person-to-person relationships, especially in the family. At the same time, people report heavy money worry, fear of crime and low trust in institutions. The pattern is "my life and my people are OK; the country is not". People are personally optimistic and collectively sceptical.

### Cited Findings
**Life evaluation and emotion rankings (2026)**
- In the World Happiness Report 2026 (released March 2026), Costa Rica ranks 4th (7.439), the highest a Latin American country has ever ranked. Other placings: Mexico 12th (6.972), Belize 27th, Uruguay 31st, Brazil 32nd, El Salvador 37th, Panama 39th, Guatemala 42nd, Argentina 44th, Jamaica 49th, Chile 50th, Nicaragua 51st, Paraguay 57th, Ecuador 59th, Honduras 63rd, Dominican Republic 64th, Colombia 68th, Peru 72nd, Trinidad and Tobago 76th, Bolivia 78th, Venezuela 80th. Haiti and Cuba do not appear in the table — [Happier Lives Institute summary of WHR 2026](https://www.happierlivesinstitute.org/2026/03/19/world-happiness-report-2026-full-country-rankings-our-favourite-finding/); [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/03/19/2026/costa-rica-ranked-fourth-happiest-country-a-first-for-latam)
- Costa Rica rose from 23rd in 2023 to 4th in 2026 — [Oxford Wellbeing Research Centre, 19 Mar 2026](https://wellbeing.hmc.ox.ac.uk/news/the-worlds-happiest-countries-for-2026-and-what-they-get-right/)
- Among under-25s, Latin America's Cantril Ladder scores rose 0.26 points between 2006–2011 and 2020–2025. Over the same period they fell 0.81 points in North America, Australia and New Zealand, and 0.42 points in the UK and Ireland — [Happier Lives Institute on WHR 2026](https://www.happierlivesinstitute.org/2026/03/19/world-happiness-report-2026-full-country-rankings-our-favourite-finding/)
- **[older data: 2023 fieldwork]** Gallup Global Emotions 2024: six of the world's top 10 countries on the Positive Experience Index were Latin American. Paraguay and Panama scored 86, Guatemala 85, Mexico 84, El Salvador 83 and Costa Rica 83. Within the region, Peru had the highest Negative Experience Index (40) and Paraguay the lowest (27). The share reporting "a lot of stress yesterday" ranged from 35% in Paraguay to 45% in Costa Rica, against 37% globally. Between 69% and 75% of people in seven Latin American countries said they learned or did something interesting the previous day, against 54% globally — [Gallup via BusinessWire, 25 Jun 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625405488/en/Latin-American-Countries-Continue-to-Lead-the-World-in-Positive-Emotions)

**Global Flourishing Study, wave 1 (published 2025)**
- Flourishing including financial security: Mexico 7.64 (4th of 22 countries), Argentina 7.14 (10th), Brazil 7.02 (15th). Without the financial-security items, Mexico rises to 2nd (8.19), Argentina to 6th (7.79) and Brazil to 9th (7.63). This is the clearest data point showing that the region's money insecurity drags down otherwise high relational and meaning scores — [GFS study profile and initial results, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- Argentina's relative strengths are "promoting good", mastery, social support and hope. Its relative weaknesses are financial worry, material worry, anxiety and subjective financial well-being. Flourishing increases with age in Argentina and Brazil — [GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- Across all countries, flourishing rose from 6.86 for people who never attend religious services to 7.67 for those attending more than weekly. Good childhood relationships with mother (+0.18) and father (+0.11), being married and being employed were also associated with higher flourishing — [GFS study profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845)
- In a Universidad Austral, Harvard and Baylor analysis of Argentina (n=6,724, published March 2026), Argentina is above the global mean on 10 of 12 indicators. Examples: meaning 7.92 vs 7.39, relationship satisfaction 8.04 vs 7.71, promoting good 8.63 vs 8.01. It is far below on financial worry about everyday expenses (3.96 vs 5.59) and on material worry about safety, food and housing (3.79 vs 5.89). The authors call this a "paradox" and propose "cultural and social compensation" through family and community networks, free public education and health care, and church-run social services. They note that the sample over-represents less-educated people — [Infobae, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/); [Austral PDF](https://www.austral.edu.ar/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/IF-Florecimiento-humano-articulo-IJW.pdf)

**Why positive emotion is high: research explanations**
- **[older data: 2018]** Mariano Rojas, the main academic voice on this question, argues that what Latin America has in abundance "is not social capital… it is person-based interpersonal relations". He means relationships where people know each other's history and needs, as opposed to functional or instrumental ties. Survey comparisons with the US:
  - 76.5% vs 61.1% agree "we spend a lot of time together" as a family.
  - 86% vs 67% agree "in my family we help and support each other a lot".
  - 64.5% vs 37.8% say their relationships are warm and close.

  His caveats: the surveys covered only Mexico, Costa Rica and Colombia (plus US samples), and "corruption, crime and economic difficulties reduce happiness in Latin America" — [Rojas, UPAEP presentation 2018](https://upaep.mx/ciie/images/2018/presentaciones/Mariano_Rojas_High_Happiness_in_Latin_America.pdf)
- The secondary literature summarises the "well-being paradox" as follows: 10 of the 15 countries with the highest positive affect are Latin American (Rojas 2018; Gallup 2023); relationships are "a source of positive affect and purpose"; and there is some evidence of a cultural tendency to rate life positively (Diener et al. 2009, 41-country student sample). The evidence on optimism is thinner — [MAPP Magazine, Jul 2024](https://mappmagazine.com/articles/the-well-being-paradox)
- **[older data: 2023]** Latinobarómetro director Marta Lagos says happiness is driven by personal factors rather than by a realistic reading of national conditions. People value being alive despite disasters, and they consistently rate their own prospects above the country's: "La gente va a estar mejor que el país" (people will be better off than the country) — [El Observador CR, 2 Nov 2023](https://observador.cr/america-latina-nunca-habia-sido-mas-feliz-que-ahora-como-explica-el-latinobarometro-la-dicotomia-de-la-region￼/)

**What people say they want and fear (security, economy, democracy, leaving)**
- Latinobarómetro 2024 ("La Democracia Resiliente"):
  - Personal optimism about one's economic future hit a record 52%. The Dominican Republic and Panama are the most hopeful, in contrast with low confidence in national economies.
  - 33% say they or a close relative were a crime victim, rising since 2017. In Ecuador the figure is 51%.
  - In Chile, 77% fear being assaulted.
  - Support for democracy is 52%, a decade high, and 75% in Argentina.
  - 42% question whether political parties are needed.

  — [CNN Chile, Dec 2024](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/latinobarometro-america-latina-muestra-signos-de-recuperacion-democratica_20241220/)
- **[older data: 2023]** Firefighters, churches and national companies were the most-trusted institutions. Parties, congress, the judiciary and unions were near the bottom — [El Observador CR](https://observador.cr/america-latina-nunca-habia-sido-mas-feliz-que-ahora-como-explica-el-latinobarometro-la-dicotomia-de-la-region￼/)
- AmericasBarometer 2026 (LAPOP, released 9 Sep 2026; 25,282 interviews in 21 countries, with Cuba included for the first time):
  - Household economic conditions, food insecurity and perceived neighbourhood safety improved in many countries, but unevenly.
  - Support for democracy reached its highest regional level in over a decade.
  - Emigration intentions fell back to pre-pandemic levels, with US immigration-policy changes a "likely" factor.

  The page publishes no country figures — [Vanderbilt CGD](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/cgd/resource/2026-americasbarometer/); [Notre Dame Kellogg](https://kellogg.nd.edu/pulse-democracy-americas)
- **[older data: 2023]** AmericasBarometer: the regional crime-victimisation rate was 23% in 2023, against 19–25% since 2010. Emigration intentions peaked in 2021. Trust in elections ran from 67% in Uruguay to 21% in Colombia — [LAPOP AB2023 presentation](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/lapop/AB2023-Dialogue-Presentation-Final.pdf). In Nicaragua, 50% intended to emigrate in 2023, up from 30% in 2019 — [Divergentes, Mar 2024](https://www.divergentes.com/americas-barometer-nicaragua-a-country-prepared-to-migrate)

### Inferences
- What people want, ranked by what the evidence supports: (1) the wellbeing and togetherness of family and close others; (2) economic security for everyday expenses, not wealth; (3) physical safety; (4) meaning, faith and hope; (5) dignity and fair treatment by institutions; (6) for a large minority, the option to leave or to support someone who left. The GFS Mexico jump from 4th to 2nd once financial security is removed is the clearest numeric sign of a "rich in relationships, poor in security" profile.
- The "people will be better off than the country" pattern suggests insight cards should honour personal agency and hope without blaming individuals for structural problems such as crime, inflation or informality. They should also avoid toxic positivity: stress is real, and in Costa Rica 45% reported a lot of stress yesterday.
- High positive emotion should not be read as "people are fine". GFS shows money and material worry near the bottom globally in Argentina, and youth suicide is rising (see section 4).

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the WHR 2026 regional chapter itself, so I cannot confirm the report's 2026 theme or whether it includes a Latin America-specific analysis. Haiti and Cuba scores were not in the summary table.
- I did not fetch the World Values Survey wave 7 or 8 cultural-map positions for Latin American countries.
- I found no regional Latinobarómetro 2025 report with "main problem" percentages. Country news coverage existed but was not verified, so the region-wide ranking of crime vs economy vs corruption for 2025 is unconfirmed.
- I found no full Gallup Global Emotions 2025 data for the region (2024 fieldwork). The cited figures are from 2023 fieldwork.

## 2. Social life: family closeness, compadrazgo and community, gender roles and machismo debates, religion, Indigenous and Afro-descendant experiences

### Takeaway
Family time and mutual support are central and measurably higher than in the US. That same closeness coexists with high levels of partner violence against women and with shame around problems seen as private. Religion remains a trusted institution, but it is diversifying fast: Brazil's 2022 census shows Catholics falling to 57% and evangelicals rising to 27%. About one in five Latin Americans is Afro-descendant and roughly one in twelve Indigenous, with large country-by-country variation. Both groups face measurable poverty and discrimination gaps.

### Cited Findings
**Family and community**
- **[older data: 2018]** Latin American respondents are more likely than US respondents to say their family spends a lot of time together (76.5% vs 61.1%) and helps and supports each other a lot (86% vs 67%). They also report knowing more neighbours by name and taking part in more community activities, though the absolute numbers are small — [Rojas 2018](https://upaep.mx/ciie/images/2018/presentaciones/Mariano_Rojas_High_Happiness_in_Latin_America.pdf)
- Authors of the Argentina study attribute high flourishing to "strong family and community networks" and to shared time with family and friends during hardship — [Infobae, Mar 2026](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)
- In a US college sample, so not Latin America residents, familism (a felt duty to support and respect family) can make mental-health problems feel like a family shame. Prior work links Latinx familism to informal and religious help-seeking rather than specialty mental-health care — [Zhou et al. 2021, J Racial Ethn Health Disparities](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9249685)

**Gender**
- At least 3,828 women were victims of femicide in Latin America in 2024, about 11 a day, and 19,254 over five years. Honduras has the highest rate (4.3 per 100,000 women) and Chile the lowest (0.4). Most are killed by a current or former partner. There were 587 "indirect victims", mostly children, in the 10 countries reporting this — [ECLAC Gender Equality Observatory via El Heraldo/EFE, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.elheraldo.hn/mundo/mujeres-victimas-femicidio-2024-latinoamerica-segun-cepal-NI28314900)
- **[older data: 2023]** Latinobarómetro's director says support for gender equality falls during economic crises, and that on women's roles the region is "the same as in '97" — [El Observador CR](https://observador.cr/america-latina-nunca-habia-sido-mas-feliz-que-ahora-como-explica-el-latinobarometro-la-dicotomia-de-la-region￼/)
- Unpaid care overload limits young women's participation in education, work and public life — [ECLAC Social Panorama 2025 via Cadena 3](https://www.cadena3.com/noticia/mundo/menos-pobreza-en-america-latina-pero-crece-la-desigualdad-y-baja-movilidad-social_491686)
- Suicide in the Americas is about 3.7 times higher in men (14.7 per 100,000) than women (4), but attempts are almost five times more frequent in women. Rates among women rose faster after 2000 (+23% vs +14.4% for men). For men, suicide is linked to alcohol and drug use, unemployment and living in high-homicide areas — [PAHO, 10 Sep 2025](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas). Note that the "Americas" here includes the US and Canada.

**Religion**
- Brazil, 2022 census (published June 2025), population aged 10+:
  - Catholic 56.7%, down from 65.1% in 2010.
  - Evangelical 26.9%, up from 21.6%.
  - No religion 9.3%, up from 7.9%.
  - Umbanda and Candomblé 1.0%, up from 0.3%.
  - Spiritist 1.8%.

  Regional variation is large: 36.8% of people in the North are evangelical, compared with 77.4% Catholic in Piauí. Acre is 44.4% evangelical, and Roraima is 16.9% "no religion". "No religion" peaks among 20–24-year-olds (14.3%), while 72% of people aged 80+ are Catholic — [Diário do Grande ABC / IBGE, 7 Jun 2025](https://www.dgabc.com.br/Noticia/4238463/ibge-catolicos-caem-evangelicos-e-sem-religiao-crescem-no-brasil)
- **[older data: 2023]** Churches are among the most-trusted institutions region-wide — [El Observador CR](https://observador.cr/america-latina-nunca-habia-sido-mas-feliz-que-ahora-como-explica-el-latinobarometro-la-dicotomia-de-la-region￼/)
- GFS (all countries) links frequent religious attendance with higher flourishing. In Argentina specifically, Christians report higher flourishing than comparable non-religious people, though the authors say religion "is not necessarily the main factor" — [GFS profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845); [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)

**Afro-descendant and Indigenous peoples**
- **[older data: 2020]** About 134 million Afro-descendants make up 21% of Latin America's population. Compared with others, they face:
  - higher poverty and extreme poverty, even in low-poverty countries, with poverty concentrated among women in Afro-descendant households;
  - higher infant and maternal mortality and adolescent pregnancy;
  - higher unemployment even with more schooling.

  Only 13 constitutions condemn racial discrimination, and 16 countries criminalise it — [UNFPA/ECLAC, 29 Oct 2020](https://www.unfpa.org/es/updates/poner-fin-al-racismo-es-tarea-de-todos-y-constituye-un-aspecto-central-en-la-construcci%C3%B3n)
- **[older data: 2010 census round, published 2014]** About 45 million Indigenous people make up 8.3% of Latin America. They belong to 826 peoples, plus an estimated ~200 in voluntary isolation. Shares by country: Bolivia 62.2%, Guatemala 41%, Peru 24% (7 million), Mexico 15.1% (17 million) — [ECLAC via Teletica/AFP, 22 Sep 2014](https://www.teletica.com/internacional/pueblos-indigenas-crecen-y-ya-son-83-de-poblacion-de-america-latina_66243). An older ECLAC presentation (c. 2010) cites "more than 670" state-recognised peoples, a count that conflicts with this one — [ECLAC presentation](https://cepal.org/sites/default/files/presentations/cepd_fabianadelpopolo.pdf)
- Afro-Brazilian religions (Umbanda, Candomblé) tripled in self-declaration between 2010 and 2022 but remain small at 1.0% in Brazil, per the census figures above — [DGABC/IBGE](https://www.dgabc.com.br/Noticia/4238463/ibge-catolicos-caem-evangelicos-e-sem-religiao-crescem-no-brasil)

### Inferences
- Family is the default frame of meaning, but the app must not treat family as only positive. Partner violence, family shame around mental health and care overload for women are common enough that family questions need an opt-out path. They also need wording that allows ambivalence, for example "someone at home made today harder" alongside "someone made you feel cared for".
- Religious change is generational and regional, and Brazil shows this most clearly. Prompts about faith, gratitude or "what gives you strength" should be phrased so they work for Catholics, evangelicals, followers of Afro-descendant traditions, Indigenous spiritualities and the growing non-religious young. The app should never assume Catholicism. This fits the app's rule of not asking which religion someone belongs to.
- Because census counts of ethnicity vary so much by country (Bolivia 62% Indigenous vs single digits elsewhere), the app should never infer ethnicity from country. It should let lived experience (for example "being treated unfairly because of how you look or speak") come up only if the user raises it.

### Gaps
- **Compadrazgo** (godparent and co-parent ties), **simpatía**, **personalismo** and **respeto**: I fetched no peer-reviewed source based on Latin America residents. The only familism source I fetched used a US college sample. These concepts are widely discussed, but their prevalence and current relevance in 2026 are unverified here.
- I found no 2024–2026 survey data on machismo or gender-role attitudes (for example WVS items on women working or men's housework), and nothing on the reach of "Ni Una Menos" type movements.
- I found no region-wide religious-affiliation figure for 2024–2026. Latinobarómetro and Pew data were not fetched; Pew's 2014 survey is old.
- I found no 2020-round census totals for Indigenous and Afro-descendant populations; the ECLAC 2020-round publication page failed to load.
- I found no data on Afro-Caribbean religions (Vodou, Santería or Regla de Ocha, Rastafari) or on the Caribbean's Indo-Caribbean populations (for example in Trinidad and Guyana).

## 3. Economic life: informal work, inequality, inflation, crime and insecurity, migration and remittances

### Takeaway
Poverty fell to a record low in 2024 (25.5%), but about 47% of workers are informal, inequality is among the world's highest, and money and material worry dominate wellbeing gaps. Violence fell regionally in 2025 (median homicide rate down about 5%) but hit records in Ecuador and Haiti. Extortion of bus routes, transport workers and small businesses is an everyday tax in several countries. Migration and remittances shape family life. 2026 changes include a US 1% tax on cash-funded remittances, US deportation flights, Venezuela's post-Maduro transition and Cuba's population haemorrhage.

### Cited Findings
**Poverty and inequality**
- ECLAC Social Panorama 2025 (Nov 2025):
  - Income poverty was 25.5% in 2024 (about 162 million people), the lowest since comparable records began and more than 7 percentage points below 2020.
  - Extreme poverty was 9.8% (62 million), still 2.1 points above its 2014 low.
  - The richest 10% receive 34.2% of income and the poorest 10% receive 1.7%.
  - The region's Gini is 14 points above the OECD average and second only to sub-Saharan Africa.

  — [Cadena 3 / ECLAC](https://www.cadena3.com/noticia/mundo/menos-pobreza-en-america-latina-pero-crece-la-desigualdad-y-baja-movilidad-social_491686)

**Informal work**
- In 2025, 47% of employed people in Latin America and the Caribbean were informal. The rate is higher among women and young people — [ILO Panorama Laboral 2025 via BioBioChile, 12 Dec 2025](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/economia/actualidad-economica/2025/12/12/la-informalidad-laboral-afecta-a-casi-una-de-dos-personas-en-america-latina-y-el-caribe-segun-la-oit.shtml)
- Country spread: Bolivia 82.2%, Ecuador 70.1%, Peru 69.8%, Paraguay 64.3%, Colombia 54.3%, Chile 25.1%, Uruguay 22.4%. Regional unemployment was 5.3% in 2025, among the lowest in decades, and informality was at its lowest since the pandemic began — [ILO via EFE/ABC Color, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.abc.com.py/internacionales/2026/07/30/latinoamerica-experimenta-reduccion-de-desempleo-y-de-informalidad-en-2025-senala-la-oit/)

**Crime and insecurity**
- InSight Crime 2025 Homicide Round-Up (Mar 2026):
  - At least 108,838 murders, with a median rate of about 17.6 per 100,000, down more than 5% from 2024.
  - Highest rates: Haiti 68.0 (likely an undercount), Ecuador 50.9 (a record, up 31%), Trinidad and Tobago 27.0, Colombia 25.8, Jamaica 23.7, Honduras 23.2, Brazil 19.2, Guatemala 17.4 (up 8%), Costa Rica 16.8, Mexico 15.4 (methodology concerns).
  - Lowest rates: Peru 10.7, Uruguay 10.3, Dominican Republic 8.7, Paraguay 6.1, Chile 5.4, Argentina 3.7, El Salvador 1.3 (narrow official definition that excludes police and prison killings).
  - No reliable data for Venezuela, Nicaragua or Bolivia.

  — [InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/news/insight-crime-2025-homicide-round-up/)
- Extortion: in Peru it targets transport above all, and extortion networks caused driver strikes in Lima in 2024 and 2025. "Drop-by-drop" loan-sharking (gota a gota) is rising. In Guatemala, MS13 extorts bus routes. In Venezuela, extortion is among the main drivers of violence — [InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/news/insight-crime-2025-homicide-round-up/)
- Perception vs rate: in Chile 77% fear being assaulted and 96% perceive rising crime, despite a homicide rate of 5.4 — [CNN Chile on Latinobarómetro 2024](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/latinobarometro-america-latina-muestra-signos-de-recuperacion-democratica_20241220/); [InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/news/insight-crime-2025-homicide-round-up/)
- **[older data: 2017 report]** In Central America, nearly half of the poorest respondents avoided public transport for fear of crime, limiting access to jobs and education. About a third of Northern Triangle adults had considered emigrating because of insecurity. Honduras ranked highest on crime-avoidance behaviour — [InSight Crime on LAPOP/Inter-American Dialogue "Beneath the Violence"](https://insightcrime.org/news/analysis/how-fear-crime-central-america-impacts-daily-life-contributes-migration-report/)

**Migration and remittances**
- Remittances to Mexico were US$61.79 billion in 2025, down 4.6% from the 2024 record of US$64.75 billion. Fear of US enforcement reduced formal transfers. Remittances recovered by 3.1% year-on-year in January–July 2026, with an average transfer of US$408 — [Rio Times citing Banxico, Sep 2026](https://www.riotimesonline.com/latin-america-remittances-2026/). Caution: the article has internal inconsistencies, which it notes.
- A US 1% federal excise tax on remittances funded with cash, money orders or cashier's checks took effect on 1 January 2026. Bank-account and card-funded transfers are exempt — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/latin-america-remittances-2026/); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/us-tax-remittances-goes-effect-first-day-2026-3794166)
- Venezuela: Nicolás Maduro was captured by the United States on 3 January 2026, and Delcy Rodríguez became acting president. More than 5,000 Venezuelans returned in her first 100 days, according to official figures — [Gestión, 18 Apr 2026](https://gestion.pe/peru/mas-de-5000-venezolanos-regresan-en-primeros-100-dias-del-gobierno-de-delcy-rodriguez-noticia/). More than 7.7 million Venezuelans have left over the decade, and about 2.8 million live in Colombia. Many hesitate to return because of children in school, rebuilt lives, a parent's medical treatment, fear of detention and the lack of an amnesty — [Vanguardia/EFE, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/02/23/migrantes-venezolanos-en-colombia-dudan-regresar-a-su-pais-tras-captura-de-maduro/)
- Cuba lost 245,264 people to migration in 2025, falling to 9.43 million. That is a 16% loss since 2017 officially and 24% by independent estimates, with nine straight years of decline. The government says more than 3 million Cubans live abroad. As US routes closed, emigrants shifted to Brazil, Mexico, Guyana, Uruguay and Spain. The drivers are prolonged blackouts, shortages and high inflation — [Diario Libre/EFE citing ONEI, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.diariolibre.com/mundo/america-latina/2026/07/21/perdida-de-poblacion-en-cuba-por-migracion-masiva/3606462)
- **[older data: 2022]** At that time R4V counted 6.81 million Venezuelan migrants and refugees, 5.75 million of them in Latin America and the Caribbean — [Sitio Andino](https://www.sitioandino.com.ar/mundo/la-onu-eleva-68-millones-los-migrantes-venezolanos-el-mundo-n5634554)

### Inferences
- With nearly half of workers informal, and over 80% in Bolivia, the app should not assume a salary, a boss, an HR department, paid leave or a fixed schedule. Money questions should allow for irregular income: day sales, gig delivery, a family shop, several small jobs.
- Insecurity is felt in movement and routine (routes, buses, phones, night travel, extortion fees), and the fear can be high even where homicide is low, as in Chile. Safety questions should ask about feelings and small protective choices, not crime details. The app should not infer real risk from country statistics.
- Many users live in transnational families: senders, receivers, returnees, deportees, people who stayed behind and people waiting to leave. In 2026 that includes Venezuelans deciding whether to return, Cubans watching their communities empty, and Mexican and Central American families affected by US enforcement and the remittance tax.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025 regional remittance total, or 2025 figures for Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Haiti or the Dominican Republic. Shares of GDP for Central America were not verified.
- No 2025–2026 inflation data. Argentina's disinflation and Venezuela's and Cuba's inflation are mentioned in sources only qualitatively.
- No R4V figure for Venezuelans abroad as of 2026. The 7.7 million figure comes from a news report citing "international organisations".
- The full AmericasBarometer 2026 PDF was not read, so there are no 2026 country figures for crime victimisation, food insecurity or emigration intent.

## 4. Mindset: stress, mental-health stigma, warmth and expressiveness, faith-based coping, attitudes to self-improvement

### Takeaway
High everyday warmth and positive emotion coexist with a large unmet mental-health need. Before COVID, about three in four people with a moderate-to-severe disorder in Latin America got no treatment. The Americas are the only world region where suicide rates are still rising (+17% since 2000), with steep increases among adolescents. Stigma and a preference for informal, family and religious support shape whether people seek help.

### Cited Findings
- **Suicide trend.** More than 100,000 people in the Americas died by suicide in 2021, a rise of 17% since 2000, and it is the only world region with an increase. North America is a main driver. The Southern Cone shows significant growth, and Caribbean countries have the region's highest suicide mortality — [PAHO, 10 Sep 2025](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas)
- **Youth.** Among 10–24-year-olds, suicide rose from 5.7 to 7.84 per 100,000 between 2000 and 2021 (+38%), with 18,157 deaths in 2021. It is the third leading cause of death in this age group. The steepest rise is among 10–14-year-olds, and rates among females are rising faster. Named risks are depression and anxiety at younger ages, substance use, excessive digital exposure, cyberbullying, social pressure and access to lethal means — [PAHO, 20 May 2026, Lancet Regional Health – Americas study](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/20-5-2026-suicidio-entre-adolescentes-adultos-jovenes-aumenta-americas-ops-alerta-sobre)
- **Treatment gap [older data: 2018].** Latin America's treatment gap is 79% for any disorder and 74.7% for moderate-to-severe disorders. It is worse in Mesoamerica (81.8% and 78.7%) than in South America (77.8% and 73.1%). The gap is 73.7% for affective disorders, 76.7% for anxiety and 83.7% for substance use. There were no data for the Caribbean. 12-month prevalence of any disorder is about 20.7% (weighted). Stigma, low mental-health literacy and cost are named barriers, and Latin American countries invest less in mental health than peers of similar income — [Kohn et al. 2018, Rev Panam Salud Publica](https://scielosp.org/pdf/rpsp/2018.v42/e165/en)
- PAHO's 2025 Regional Suicide Prevention Initiative names stigma as a barrier and includes breaking the silence through media and campaigns — [PAHO](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas)
- **Stress.** **[2023 fieldwork]** Stress is not low despite high positive emotion: 35–45% reported a lot of stress the previous day in five Latin American countries, around the global 37% — [Gallup via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625405488/en/Latin-American-Countries-Continue-to-Lead-the-World-in-Positive-Emotions)
- **Warmth.** **[older data: 2018]** 64.5% of Latin American respondents describe their relationships as warm and close, against 37.8% of US respondents — [Rojas](https://upaep.mx/ciie/images/2018/presentaciones/Mariano_Rojas_High_Happiness_in_Latin_America.pdf)
- **Faith-based coping.** In a US Latinx context, familism is linked to informal and religious help-seeking rather than specialty mental-health services, and mental illness may be framed as emotional weakness — [Zhou et al. 2021 (US sample)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9249685). In Argentina, the Catholic Church acting as an intermediary for social services is cited as a cushion against poverty — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)
- **Self-improvement.** GFS Argentina scores high on "deferred gratification" (8.12 vs 7.45 global), "mastery" and "hope" — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/); [GFS profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12165845). **[2023 fieldwork]** 69–75% in several Central American countries learned or did something interesting yesterday, against 54% globally — [Gallup via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625405488/en/Latin-American-Countries-Continue-to-Lead-the-World-in-Positive-Emotions)

### Inferences
- Expressiveness and warmth make relational prompts natural, but high positive emotion can hide distress, especially in young people and men. Men's suicide is linked to alcohol, unemployment and violent neighbourhoods. Weekly mirrors should leave room for "I'm tired of holding it together" without labelling it.
- Language that frames reflection as care for the people you love ("so you can be there for your family") may fit better than individualistic "self-optimisation". GFS Argentina's high scores on mastery, hope and deferred gratification suggest growth framing still works when it is tied to effort and others. This is my inference and was not tested.
- Clinical vocabulary ("disorder", "therapy") may meet stigma. Plain words ("heavy", "overwhelmed", "can't sleep", "nervios") and faith-compatible options are likely safer. "Nervios" as an idiom of distress was not verified in this research (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No Latin America-resident, peer-reviewed sources were fetched on simpatía, emotional expressiveness, "nervios" or "ataque de nervios", fatalism, or the attitudes behind "si Dios quiere" (God willing). These are well known in the literature but unverified here.
- No post-2020 treatment-gap update. The Kohn 2018 figures predate COVID-19.
- No country-level suicide rates for Latin America and the Caribbean. PAHO says only that the Caribbean and the Southern Cone are high.
- No data on attitudes to self-help, coaching or wellness apps in the region, or on smartphone and data-cost barriers.

- Wrong region-wide assumptions to avoid:

  - "Crime fear tracks homicide rates".
  - "Family is always close and safe".
  - "Latinos are happy, so they don't need support".
  - "Everyone wants to emigrate to the US". Emigration intent is back to pre-pandemic levels, and flows now go to Brazil, Spain, Mexico and Uruguay.
- Urban vs rural (inference): informality and Indigenous presence are higher in rural and Andean settings, while extortion of transport and small businesses is concentrated in cities. I did not fetch an urban/rural breakdown.

## 6. Everyday moments that ring true, and Western or US defaults to avoid

### Takeaway
The most authentic prompts sit where the evidence is strongest: time together with family and friends, help given and received, money stretched across irregular income, choices about moving around safely, relatives abroad, faith and hope as everyday resources, and small moments of learning or enjoyment. Defaults to avoid include the nuclear family living alone, salaried work, therapy as the first step, individual achievement as the goal, and the assumption that one's country is stable.

### Cited Findings (evidence behind candidate moments)
- **Family time and mutual help are normal and frequent.** **[2018]** 76.5% agree "we spend a lot of time together" and 86% agree "we help and support each other a lot" — [Rojas](https://upaep.mx/ciie/images/2018/presentaciones/Mariano_Rojas_High_Happiness_in_Latin_America.pdf)
- **Shared time with family and friends during hardship is a named source of comfort.** — [Infobae on GFS Argentina](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)
- **Everyday money worry is the main weak spot.** Argentina scores 3.96 vs 5.59 globally on everyday-expense worry and 3.79 vs 5.89 on safety, food and housing worry — [Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/salud/2026/03/17/un-estudio-revelo-que-los-argentinos-superan-el-promedio-global-de-bienestar-psicologico/)
- **Irregular work is the norm for nearly half of workers.** — [ILO](https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/economia/actualidad-economica/2025/12/12/la-informalidad-laboral-afecta-a-casi-una-de-dos-personas-en-america-latina-y-el-caribe-segun-la-oit.shtml)
- **Transport is a site of fear and extortion.** These are bus routes and drivers in Guatemala and Peru, and avoidance of public transport by the poorest **[2017]** — [InSight Crime 2025 round-up](https://insightcrime.org/news/insight-crime-2025-homicide-round-up/); [InSight Crime 2017](https://insightcrime.org/news/analysis/how-fear-crime-central-america-impacts-daily-life-contributes-migration-report/)
- **Relatives abroad and money sent home.** The average Mexican remittance is about US$408. In 2026 the 1% cash-remittance tax applies, deportation flights continue, and Venezuelans are weighing return — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/latin-america-remittances-2026/); [Gestión](https://gestion.pe/peru/mas-de-5000-venezolanos-regresan-en-primeros-100-dias-del-gobierno-de-delcy-rodriguez-noticia/); [Vanguardia](https://www.vanguardia.com/colombia/2026/02/23/migrantes-venezolanos-en-colombia-dudan-regresar-a-su-pais-tras-captura-de-maduro/)
- **Disruptions to basic services.** Blackouts in Cuba, and power and water outages in Jamaica after Melissa — [Diario Libre](https://www.diariolibre.com/mundo/america-latina/2026/07/21/perdida-de-poblacion-en-cuba-por-migracion-masiva/3606462); [Jamaica Gleaner](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20260428/2000-jamaicans-still-without-power-six-months-after-hurricane-melissa)
- **Learning or doing something interesting is common daily.** **[2023]** 69–75% in several countries — [Gallup](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240625405488/en/Latin-American-Countries-Continue-to-Lead-the-World-in-Positive-Emotions)
- **Personal optimism exceeds national optimism.** Personal economic optimism hit a record 52% in 2024 — [CNN Chile](https://www.cnnchile.com/pais/latinobarometro-america-latina-muestra-signos-de-recuperacion-democratica_20241220/); [El Observador CR](https://observador.cr/america-latina-nunca-habia-sido-mas-feliz-que-ahora-como-explica-el-latinobarometro-la-dicotomia-de-la-region￼/)

### Inferences (candidate prompts, my own drafting, grounded in the findings above)
- "Who did you share a meal or a long chat with this week, and what did it give you?" (family or friend time, without assuming a Sunday meal or a nuclear family).
- "Someone asked you for a favour, or you asked someone. How did it feel to give or receive?" (mutual help).
- "Was there a moment today when you changed your route, hid your phone or waited for someone so you'd feel safer? What helped?" (protective routines, without probing crime details or assuming victimisation).
- "Did you talk with, send to or worry about someone who lives far away?" (transnational family, framed neutrally so it fits senders, receivers, returnees and people left behind).
- "This week, did money feel a bit more manageable or a bit tighter? What's one thing you did to stretch it?" (irregular income, no salary assumed).
- "When the power, water or bus didn't come through, how did you make the day work?" (resourcefulness under service disruption: Cuba, Jamaica, Venezuela).
- "What gave you strength today? (People, prayer or faith, nature, music, rest, something else)" (faith-inclusive without asking affiliation).
- "What did you learn or try today, even something small?"
- Optional steps should be small, low-cost and relational: call or voice-note someone, thank someone who helped, plan a shared moment, put aside a small amount, take a different route with company.
- **Western and US defaults to avoid:**
  1. A salaried job with a boss, HR, paid time off, a 401k or a "career ladder".
  2. A nuclear family living alone, or "moving out" as a milestone.
  3. Therapy or a counsellor as the first or only suggestion.
  4. Individual self-optimisation as the main value.
  5. Stable institutions you can "report to" (police, courts). Trust in parties and the judiciary is low.
  6. A car commute.
  7. Seasons and holidays on the US calendar. Thanksgiving, for example, does not apply. Southern Cone seasons are reversed, which is general knowledge and was not sourced here.
  8. Assuming English or Spanish.
  9. Treating emigration as only a loss, or only an aspiration.
  10. Imagery or wording that assumes Catholicism, or that equates "faith" with church attendance.
  11. Assuming weekday and weekend rhythms that informal workers do not have.

### Gaps
- No ethnographic or diary-study data on daily routines (for example time-use surveys on family meals, commuting times or WhatsApp use) was fetched. The candidate prompts are inferred, not tested with users from the region.
- No sourced evidence on festivals and calendar moments (Día de Muertos, Carnival, Semana Santa, Christmas posadas, Independence days) or on how people mark them in 2026.
- No data on how people in the region respond to reflective or journaling apps.

## 7. Help-seeking and crisis support: who people turn to, helpline availability, respectful ways to point to help

### Takeaway
Most people with mental-health needs get no formal care (a 70–80% treatment gap, 2018 data). Help-seeking tends to run through family, friends, primary care and faith communities before specialists. Free national lines exist in many countries, some 24/7 with psychologists, such as Chile's *4141. Coverage and hours vary, and English-speaking Caribbean listings were not found in this research.

### Cited Findings
- **Treatment gap [2018].** In Latin America, 74.7% of people with moderate-to-severe disorders receive no treatment, and 78.7% in Mesoamerica. Barriers are stigma, low mental-health literacy and cost — [Kohn et al. 2018](https://scielosp.org/pdf/rpsp/2018.v42/e165/en)
- **Informal help-seeking (US-sample evidence).** Familism is associated with informal (family and social) and religious help-seeking, not specialty services. With somatic symptoms, people often go to medical doctors or alternative healers rather than psychologists — [Zhou et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9249685)
- PAHO recommends early identification and follow-up in schools and communities, restricting access to lethal means, responsible media coverage, reducing stigma and expanding community services — [PAHO 2026](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/20-5-2026-suicidio-entre-adolescentes-adultos-jovenes-aumenta-americas-ops-alerta-sobre); [PAHO 2025](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas)
- **Chile.** \*4141 is a free, confidential 24/7 suicide-prevention line answered by psychologists and dialled from any mobile. It launched in December 2022, and its message is "no estás solo, no estás sola" (you are not alone). The page was last updated 25 Nov 2025 — [MINSAL](https://www.minsal.cl/linea-de-atencion-4141-no-estas-solo-no-estas-sola/)
- **Country helplines listed by UNICEF LAC.** The page is undated and shows no hours; verify each line before use — [UNICEF LAC](https://www.unicef.org/lac/Ayuda-SaludMental)
  - Mexico: Línea de la Vida 800 911 2000; SAPTEL 55 5259 8121; UNAM remote psychological care 55 5025 0855.
  - Brazil: CVV 188; Pode Falar (WhatsApp).
  - Peru: 113, option 5.
  - Ecuador: 171, option 6.
  - El Salvador: 131, option 4.
  - Colombia: Línea 106 (free from Bogotá only); Línea SalvaVidas 311 766 8666; Línea 141 (ICBF, children).
  - Uruguay: Línea Vida 0800 7667; Línea de Apoyo Emocional 0800 1920.
  - Panama: 147; Tía Elaine (WhatsApp).
  - Costa Rica: Aquí estoy.
  - Dominican Republic: family line 809-636-3507.
  - Cuba: adolescent counselling line (53) 78697395.
  - Bolivia: Familia Segura 800113040.
  - Honduras: children's line 111.
  - Chile: LíneaLibre 1515 (children and teens).
- The Americas' suicide burden is concentrated in specific groups. 71% of male and 65% of female suicides are in people over 50, while youth rates are rising fastest. This means both older men and adolescents need visible support pathways — [PAHO 2025](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/10-9-2025-ops-lanza-iniciativa-para-frenar-aumento-tasas-suicidio-americas); [PAHO 2026](https://www.paho.org/es/noticias/20-5-2026-suicidio-entre-adolescentes-adultos-jovenes-aumenta-americas-ops-alerta-sobre)

### Inferences
- Offer help as a gradient that matches how people actually seek it:
  1. "Is there someone you trust you could tell?"
  2. A community or faith figure, or a health worker you already see.
  3. A free national line, chosen by the user's country setting and verified.
  4. Emergency services.

  Each step should be framed as care, not as diagnosis. Phrases like "no estás solo/sola" or "não está sozinho/a" echo official Chilean messaging. The Portuguese wording is my own adaptation.
- Keep crisis copy stigma-free and plain-language. Avoid implying weakness or "disorder". For men, framing around strength and responsibility to family may lower barriers. That is an inference, not tested.
- WhatsApp-based lines (Brazil's Pode Falar, Panama's Tía Elaine) suggest messaging channels are a familiar route. The app could show both phone and messaging options where they exist.
- Because helpline coverage is patchy and some lines are local (Colombia's 106 is free from Bogotá only), the app needs a maintained, country-verified directory with a fallback to an international directory. I did not verify one in this research.

### Gaps
- No verified helplines for Argentina, Guatemala, Honduras (adults), Nicaragua, Paraguay (adult crisis), Venezuela, Haiti, Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, the Bahamas, Guyana or Belize. The UNICEF list is youth-focused, undated and has no hours.
- No Latin America-resident survey on whom people turn to first (family vs priest or pastor vs doctor vs curandero or traditional healer). Evidence is from US Latinx samples.
- No evidence on helpline answer rates, wait times or quality, or on safety concerns about calling, such as fear of police involvement.
- Nothing fetched on traditional or Indigenous healing systems as help-seeking routes.
