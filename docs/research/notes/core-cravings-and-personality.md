## 1. What do the major evidence-based models say humans fundamentally need or want?

### Takeaway
The best-tested models agree on a short core: to feel **free and self-endorsed** (autonomy), **capable** (competence or mastery), **connected and cared for** (relatedness, belonging, love) and **respected** (status, esteem). **Safety and basic material security** come first when they are missing. Large cross-national data show these needs predict well-being in every world region. Maslow's strict ladder is not supported: the order is a "soft" tendency, and needs add up rather than unlock one another. Self-actualization and meaning are the weakest-evidenced as distinct needs.

### Cited Findings

**Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness)**
- **Chen et al. 2015 [NEW, moderate].** Late adolescents (Belgium n=359, China n=324) and university students (USA 298, China 309, Belgium 200, Peru 244). In Study 2, autonomy satisfaction (b=.42) and relatedness satisfaction (b=.23) uniquely predicted life satisfaction, and all three needs predicted vitality (bs .19–.42). The structural paths **did not differ significantly across the four countries** and held after controlling for gender, age, family income and health and financial satisfaction. Limitations: convenience samples of students, cross-sectional data, and poor reliability of autonomy satisfaction in the Chinese Study 1 sample (α=.47). — [Chen et al. 2015 PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf)
- **Chen et al. 2015, need strength [NEW, moderate].** How much people said they valued or desired each need **did not moderate** the link between satisfaction and well-being (Study 1 interaction bs .01, .05, −.02; Study 2 bs between −.08 and .07). The authors conclude the needs are "essential nutrients for optimal human functioning across individual and cultural differences." — [Chen et al. 2015 PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf)
- **Vansteenkiste, Ryan & Soenens 2020 [NEW, strong].** Wörtler et al. 2020 found need strength moderated effects "only to a very limited degree," with "no evidence for cross-over interactions": even people low in need strength did not benefit when the need was frustrated. The review cites meta-analyses (Slemp et al. 2018; Yu et al. 2018) reporting that autonomy has similar benefits across cultures, and frames the claim as "universality without uniformity": the need is the same, but the ways of meeting it differ. — [Vansteenkiste, Ryan & Soenens 2020 PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf)
- **Same review, criteria for a basic need.** It lists nine criteria (psychological, essential, inherent, distinct, universal, plus four associated ones). **Self-esteem** is excluded as a basic need because it does not operate independently of the three core needs. **Security** is treated as a compensatory need-substitute that appears when the core needs are frustrated. Candidate extra needs are discussed: novelty-variety separates in factor analysis, and Prentice et al. propose morality. Beneficence frustration added no prediction beyond the three core needs. — [Vansteenkiste, Ryan & Soenens 2020 PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf)
- **Chirkov et al. 2003 [NEW, moderate].** In South Korea, Russia, Turkey and the US (N=559), the relative autonomy of people's motivation for cultural practices predicted well-being "across cultures and gender." Autonomy here means acting from genuine endorsement, which is not the same as individualism. Horizontal (egalitarian) practices were internalized more readily than vertical (hierarchical) ones in all samples. — [Chirkov et al. 2003, Miami repository](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Differentiating-Autonomy-From-Individualism-and-Independence/991031598656802976)

**Which needs feel most satisfying in real events**
- **Sheldon et al. 2001 [NEW, moderate, partly ⚑].** In three studies with US and South Korean samples, people described their "most satisfying events" and rated 10 candidate needs. **Autonomy, competence and relatedness** were consistently in the top four, alongside self-esteem, for both salience and link to event-related affect. **Self-actualization/meaning, physical thriving, popularity/influence and money/luxury** were rated as less important. The abstract I opened does not mention security. — [Sheldon et al. 2001, Miami repository](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/What-Is-Satisfying-About-Satisfying-Events/991031600454702976)

**Maslow, tested worldwide**
- **Tay & Diener 2011 [NEW, moderate; worldwide].** Gallup World Poll 2005–2010, 123 countries, 60,865 adults. Six needs were coded as met or unmet:
  - basic (enough money for food and shelter, not going hungry)
  - safety (feeling safe walking alone, no theft or assault)
  - social support and love (felt love yesterday, someone to count on)
  - respect and pride (treated with respect, proud of something)
  - mastery (learned something, did what one does best)
  - autonomy (chose how to spend time, felt free in life)

  Results:
  - "Need fulfillment was consistently associated with SWB across world regions."
  - **Life evaluation** was driven mostly by basic needs (63% of explained variance; needs explained 13% of total variance). Income absorbed much of this effect.
  - **Positive feelings** were driven by **respect (36%) and social needs (24%)**; basic needs barely mattered.
  - **Negative feelings** were driven by respect (25%), basic needs (23%) and autonomy (22%).
  - — [Tay & Diener 2011, APA PDF](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- **Tay & Diener 2011, on the order of needs.** People tended to meet basic and safety needs first, but the sequence was "soft," with crossovers. Interactions between needs added only about 1% of variance, so the needs act **additively**: "Motivational prepotency does not mean that fulfilling needs 'out of order' is necessarily less fulfilling." Basic and safety needs depended heavily on which country people lived in; psychosocial needs depended more on individual circumstances. The data are cross-sectional and the items brief. — [Tay & Diener 2011, APA PDF](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- **Kenrick et al. 2010 [NEW, early/theory].** The authors renovate Maslow using evolutionary biology. Their levels are physiological, self-protection, affiliation (split into friendship, romantic love and kin), status/esteem (dominance and prestige plus mastery), and then mate acquisition, mate retention and parenting. They **remove self-actualization** as a distinct need, folding it into status and mating motives, and argue that motives "build upon earlier motivations rather than replace them," as overlapping rather than stacked systems. They concede "much of what we have suggested remains in the realm of speculation," and their critique of Maslow is mostly theoretical. — [Kenrick et al. 2010 PDF (UBC)](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)

**Values: Schwartz's theory**
- **Schwartz & Bardi 2001 [NEW, strong; 63 nations].** The data were 13 representative national samples, 77 teacher samples from 56 nations and 81 student samples from 54 nations. The pan-cultural order of importance, as average rank across the three sample types, was:
  - top: benevolence (1st everywhere), then self-direction and universalism (2nd–3rd)
  - middle: security (4th–5th), conformity (5th–6th), achievement (4th–6th), hedonism (7th)
  - low: stimulation (8th–9th) and tradition (8th–9th)
  - last: power (10th in every sample type)

  National hierarchies agreed strongly with this pan-cultural order: mean r=.92 for representative samples, ranging from .80 (Chile) to .97; 83% of all samples correlated at least .80. Black African samples, Fiji and the Philippines diverged, ranking conformity higher and self-direction lower. The authors explain the order by society's need for cooperative relations in primary groups, productive and innovative work, and only then gratification of self-oriented needs. — [Schwartz & Bardi 2001 PDF (Royal Holloway)](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/4c815cf2-e6e6-9a01-7090-90d03bbe5645/7/Schwartz_Bardi_2001_Value_Hierarchies_across_Cultures.pdf)

**Dweck's unified theory**
- **Dweck 2017 [NEW, early/theory].** Three basic needs are present from birth: **predictability** ("the need to predict our world"), **competence** and **acceptance** ("the need for acceptance from others"). Compound needs grow out of these. While trying to meet the needs, children form "BEATs" (beliefs, emotions and action tendencies). Needs and BEATs are the invisible core of personality; goals and behaviour are the visible part. Temperament, such as shyness or fearfulness, can strengthen particular needs. The APA summary I opened does not name the compound needs. — [APA Psychological Review spotlight on Dweck 2017](https://www.apa.org/pubs/highlights/spotlight/issue-111.html)

**The need to belong**
- **Baumeister & Leary 1995 [NEW, strong (narrative review)].** The need is for "frequent, nonaversive interactions within an ongoing relational bond" marked by "stability, affective concern, and continuation into the foreseeable future." Contact without a bond, or a bond without contact, is not enough. People form bonds readily and resist ending them. Lacking attachments is linked to poorer health, adjustment and well-being: "Social isolation is practically incompatible with high levels of happiness." The authors expect the need "to some degree in all humans in all cultures," expressed differently. The evidence is largely correlational and observational, and the authors flag alternative explanations. — [Baumeister & Leary 1995 PDF (Hendrix)](https://www.hendrix.edu/uploadedFiles/Academics/Faculty_Resources/2016_FFC/Baumeister%20and%20Leary%20(1995).pdf)

**Status and significance**
- **Anderson, Hildreth & Howland 2015 [NEW, moderate].** Status is defined as respect, admiration and voluntary deference, distinct from power ("people defer to high-status individuals because they want to, but they defer to powerful individuals because they have to"), from wealth and from belonging (status is vertical; belonging is horizontal).
  - Peer-rated and self-reported status predicted subjective well-being with an average standardized coefficient of about .45, even after controlling for acceptance.
  - In an experiment, raising felt status raised well-being without changing felt acceptance.
  - SES has a small average effect on self-esteem (r=.08); it matters mainly when it raises one's standing in the **local** group.
  - The authors conclude "the desire for status is indeed fundamental" but admit universality "is difficult to fully address," and that the evidence is mostly correlational and leans on self-rated "community ladder" items.
  - — [Anderson et al. 2015 PDF](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP387/Desire%20for%20Status%2C%20Human%20Motive%2C%20APA.pdf)
- **Kruglanski et al. 2022 [NEW, early to moderate].** Significance-quest theory: a universal "need to have social worth," usually met by living up to values shared with important others. Loss of significance, or a chance to gain it, activates the quest. The means are culturally shaped and validated by one's reference group. — [Kruglanski et al. 2022, Jagiellonian repository](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/xmlui/handle/item/311546)
- **Alsaadi et al. 2025 [NEW, early].** Of 23 meaning and significance measures reviewed, only one directly measures the quest for significance (a Turkish adult sample). None "fully address the quest for significance as a standalone construct," and the measures show "limited cultural adaptability." The review notes the quest correlates with sacrificial behaviour and extreme political attitudes. — [Alsaadi et al. 2025, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1513720/full)

### Inferences
- A defensible "core" for the app is: autonomy, competence, relatedness/love, respect, and safety/basic security. Meaning, hope and peace are better treated as **outcomes or outlooks** that grow from these needs and from culture and faith than as separate drives. Their evidence as distinct needs is weaker (Sheldon 2001; Kenrick 2010; VR&S 2020).
- Because needs act additively and Maslow's order is soft (Tay & Diener), the app should not assume someone "isn't ready" to care about meaning or respect because their security is shaky.
- Everyone needs every core need. What differs is **which feels most salient now** and **how a person tries to meet it**. Need strength barely moderates benefits (Chen 2015; VR&S 2020).

### Gaps
- Sheldon et al. 2001's findings on security (often said to become salient when deprived) were not visible in the abstract I opened. I could not verify them.
- I could not open Dweck 2017's full text, so her compound needs (often summarized as trust, control, self-esteem/status and self-coherence) are unverified here.
- I could not open the Yu et al. 2018 and Slemp et al. 2018 autonomy meta-analyses directly. They are cited only through VR&S 2020.
- PubMed and PMC pages returned CAPTCHA or empty pages, so some abstracts were reached through repositories instead.

## 2. How do the owner's cravings (freedom, security, hope, success) map onto these models, and which are universal versus variable?

### Takeaway
- **Freedom** is the universal need for autonomy (volition), but freedom as *independence from others* is culturally variable.
- **Security** is the most context-dependent: it depends on country conditions and rises with age.
- **Success** splits in two. Competence/mastery and earned respect are universal and strongly tied to well-being. Wealth, fame and power are the lowest-ranked values worldwide and the least satisfying.
- **Hope** is not a "need" in any major model; its *level* varies enormously by country, faith and employment.
- **Belonging, love and respect** have the most universal links to positive feelings.

### Cited Findings

**FREEDOM → autonomy and self-direction**
- Autonomy ("able to choose how time is spent," "feeling free in life") was one of the top three predictors of **negative feelings** worldwide (22% of explained variance). — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf) (moderate)
- Self-direction ranks **2nd of 10 values** pan-culturally. — [Schwartz & Bardi 2001](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/4c815cf2-e6e6-9a01-7090-90d03bbe5645/7/Schwartz_Bardi_2001_Value_Hierarchies_across_Cultures.pdf) (strong). Black African, Fijian and Filipino samples ranked self-direction unusually low.
- Autonomy, in the sense of self-endorsed motivation, predicted well-being in collectivist South Korea, Turkey and Russia as well as the US. — [Chirkov et al. 2003](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Differentiating-Autonomy-From-Individualism-and-Independence/991031598656802976) (moderate)
- **Fischer & Boer 2011 [NEW, strong].** Meta-analysis of 63 societies and 420,599 people, with distress (General Health Questionnaire), anxiety and burnout as outcomes. A combined individualism/autonomy index predicted well-being more consistently than wealth: "Wealth may influence well-being only via its effect on individualism." Individualism mattered **more in poorer countries**, and its benefit levelled off at the extremes. Caveats: non-representative samples, a composite with low reliability (α=.52), and complex interactions. — [Fischer & Boer 2011 PDF](https://scr.hse.ru/data/2011/11/24/1271296504/fischer%20boer%202011%20psp.pdf)
- **Age.** Openness-to-change values (self-direction, stimulation, hedonism) **fall** with age; stimulation shows the strongest negative age link of all 10 values. The pattern replicated in all 12 European countries and both cohorts. — [Robinson 2012/13, ESS](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/8968/) (moderate; ⚑ industrialized European nations only)
- **Gender.** Men rate self-direction (and power, stimulation, hedonism, achievement) slightly higher than women. Effects are small (median d≈.15), sex explains less variance than age and much less than culture, and culture moderates every difference. — [Schwartz & Rubel 2005](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/sex-differences-in-value-priorities-cross-cultural-and-multimetho/) (strong)

**SECURITY → safety, self-protection, the security value, predictability**
- Safety (feeling safe walking alone, no theft or assault) and basic needs were "contingent on country membership." People tend to secure them first, though not strictly. — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- Security ranks **4th–5th of 10** values pan-culturally: important, but below benevolence, self-direction and universalism. — [Schwartz & Bardi 2001](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/4c815cf2-e6e6-9a01-7090-90d03bbe5645/7/Schwartz_Bardi_2001_Value_Hierarchies_across_Cultures.pdf)
- Conservation values (security, conformity, tradition) **rise with age**; tradition has the strongest positive age link. — [Robinson 2012/13](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/8968/)
- Women rate security slightly higher than men, but "less consistently" across countries. — [Schwartz & Rubel 2005](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/sex-differences-in-value-priorities-cross-cultural-and-multimetho/)
- **Contested status.** Self-determination theory treats security as a compensatory need-substitute that rises when the core needs are frustrated, not as a basic need. — [VR&S 2020](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf). Dweck instead lists **predictability** as one of three inborn needs. — [APA on Dweck 2017](https://www.apa.org/pubs/highlights/spotlight/issue-111.html). Kenrick keeps **self-protection** as a foundational evolved motive. — [Kenrick 2010](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/KenrickGriskeviciusNeubergSchaller2010.pdf)

**HOPE → no direct "need"; it relates to meaning, faith and future prospects**
- **counted2025 [LIB:counted2025, moderate; worldwide].** Global Flourishing Study, 202,898 people in 22 countries. Hope was rated 0–10 on "Despite challenges, I always remain hopeful about the future."
  - Highest: Indonesia 9.17, then Mexico and Argentina. Lowest: Japan 5.95, with Sweden and Hong Kong near the bottom.
  - People attending worship more than weekly scored 8.68, versus 7.82 for those who never attend.
  - Unemployed people scored 7.68, versus 8.27 for the self-employed. Married people scored higher than single people.
  - Older people were slightly more hopeful overall, but not in every country.
  - "A snapshot in time, not a study of cause and effect."
  - — [Global Flourishing Study: Where hope lives](https://globalflourishingstudy.com/paper/where-hope-lives-who-stays-hopeful-around-the-world)
- **Oishi & Diener 2014 [NEW, moderate; worldwide].** Gallup World Poll, 132 countries, more than 140,000 people. Residents of **richer nations reported higher life satisfaction but lower meaning in life**; religiosity largely accounted for the gap. More education, fewer children and individualism went with higher satisfaction but lower meaning. — [APS press release on Oishi & Diener](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/residents-of-poorer-nations-find-greater-meaning-in-life.html)

**SUCCESS → competence/mastery, achievement, respect/status (and, separately, wealth and fame)**
- Competence is consistently in the top four needs in satisfying events. **Money/luxury and popularity/influence** are rated less important. — [Sheldon et al. 2001](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/What-Is-Satisfying-About-Satisfying-Events/991031600454702976)
- Achievement ranks in the middle (4th–6th) and **power ranks last** in all 63 nations' pooled hierarchy. — [Schwartz & Bardi 2001](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/4c815cf2-e6e6-9a01-7090-90d03bbe5645/7/Schwartz_Bardi_2001_Value_Hierarchies_across_Cultures.pdf)
- **Respect** (feeling treated with respect, being proud of something) was the **strongest predictor of positive feelings** worldwide (36% of explained variance). Mastery was one of the six needs. — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- Status, meaning respect from one's local group, predicts well-being beyond SES; status gained through income works only relative to people nearby. — [Anderson et al. 2015](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP387/Desire%20for%20Status%2C%20Human%20Motive%2C%20APA.pdf)
- Pursuing and even reaching extrinsic goals (wealth, image) "has been found to relate to diminished need satisfaction." Such goals work as need substitutes. — [VR&S 2020](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf)
- Self-enhancement values (power, achievement) **decline with age** and are higher in men. — [Robinson 2012/13](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/8968/). Men score higher on power (d≈.32, the largest sex difference) and achievement. — [Schwartz & Rubel 2005](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/sex-differences-in-value-priorities-cross-cultural-and-multimetho/)

**Added by the evidence: BELONGING and LOVE, RESPECT, MEANING, PEACE**
- **Belonging and love.** Benevolence (caring for close others) is the **#1 value in every sample type** across 63 nations. — [Schwartz & Bardi 2001](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/4c815cf2-e6e6-9a01-7090-90d03bbe5645/7/Schwartz_Bardi_2001_Value_Hierarchies_across_Cultures.pdf). Social support and love were the second-strongest predictor of positive feelings worldwide (24%). — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf). The need to belong is a "powerful, fundamental, and pervasive motivation." — [Baumeister & Leary 1995](https://www.hendrix.edu/uploadedFiles/Academics/Faculty_Resources/2016_FFC/Baumeister%20and%20Leary%20(1995).pdf)
- **Self-transcendence** (benevolence and universalism) is higher in midlife and older adults than in young adults, and higher in women. — [Robinson 2012/13](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/8968/)
- **Meaning** is higher in poorer, more religious nations. — [Oishi & Diener 2014](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/residents-of-poorer-nations-find-greater-meaning-in-life.html). Self-actualization/meaning was less salient than the core needs in satisfying events. — [Sheldon 2001](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/What-Is-Satisfying-About-Satisfying-Events/991031600454702976)
- **Peace (low-arousal positive states: calm, serenity).**
  - **Tsai et al. 2007 [NEW, moderate].** Hong Kong Chinese valued low-arousal positive states more, and excitement less, than European Americans (d≈0.26–0.39; N=568 in Study 1). Experimentally, "adjustment" goals (fitting in with others) raised the value placed on calm, and "influence" goals raised the value placed on excitement. These links **did not interact with culture**. In Study 1 European Americans also valued calm more than excitement, a result the authors say needs replication. — [Tsai et al. 2007 PDF](https://culture-emotion-lab.stanford.edu/sites/culture_emotion_lab/files/media/file/tsai_miao_seppala_fung_yeung_2007.pdf)
  - **Tsai et al. 2025 [NEW, strong].** Individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 124 datasets and more than 31,000 people. How people *want* to feel (ideal affect) is distinct from how they *actually* feel, and **culture shapes ideal affect more than actual affect**. European Americans valued high-arousal states more than East Asians, but have come to value low-arousal positive states more over time. SES, gender and age were also linked to ideal affect, but the abstract does not give the direction. — [Tsai et al. 2025, Psychological Bulletin (UCLouvain record)](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280)

**Income and country context**
- Country-level need fulfilment predicted people's life evaluation **beyond their own fulfilment**: "Improving one's own life is not enough; society-wide improvement is also required." Country-level needs did not predict daily feelings. — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Most universal:** belonging and love, respect, autonomy as volition, and competence. Each shows consistent links to well-being across regions, and they sit at the top of value hierarchies.
- **Most variable:** security, in both level met and the importance given to it (rising with age, contingent on country). Hope, in level (Indonesia 9.17 vs Japan 5.95). Freedom understood as independence or stimulation (falling with age, higher in individualist contexts). Peace versus excitement as an ideal (culture shapes the ideal more than the actual feeling).
- From the hope rankings (Indonesia, Mexico and Argentina highest; Japan, Sweden and Hong Kong lowest) and Oishi & Diener's meaning data, national wealth does **not** seem to buy hope or meaning, and faith and community may matter more. This is my inference: the hope page did not test national wealth directly.
- "Success" in the app should be split into separate options: *doing something well* (competence), *being respected* (status), *getting ahead materially* (extrinsic), and *making a difference* (benevolence or meaning). They relate to well-being very differently.
- Age and gender differences in values are real but small relative to culture and to individual variation. Schwartz & Rubel: sex explains less variance than age and much less than culture. The app should never infer a person's cravings from demographics.

### Gaps
- I found no cross-cultural meta-analysis on *hope* as a craving (as opposed to hope levels). Snyder's hope theory work was not opened.
- I found no direct cross-national data on desire for "peace of mind" in the Gallup World Poll. Ideal-affect evidence covers mainly North American and East Asian samples, and the direction of the age effect in Tsai 2025 is not visible in the abstract.
- I did not get per-value effect sizes for age (Robinson's page gives no magnitudes or sample size).
- Only the abstract of Schwartz & Rubel 2005 was visible. The country-level variation (e.g., whether gaps are larger in more egalitarian countries) was not verified.

## 3. Being understood: the need to feel seen and known accurately

### Takeaway
Feeling understood is linked to daily well-being and relationship health. The feeling, however, only modestly tracks whether others *actually* understand us. People want both to feel good about themselves and to be seen accurately. In judgments and real choices, accuracy (self-verification) often wins. When rejection is a risk, people prefer praise; in safe, committed relationships they prefer accurate feedback, even about flaws.

### Cited Findings
- **Reis, Lemay & Finkenauer 2017 [NEW, moderate].** Feeling understood matters for "relationship and personal well-being." Feeling understood is "only modestly related to actually being understood by others." The authors' model includes situational, dispositional and relational causes of feeling understood. Only the abstract was visible. — [Reis et al. 2017 (Utrecht repository)](https://utrecht-main-test.atmire.com/items/1df3db17-70bb-4df9-84c2-6da7cb6cf86a)
- **Lun, Kesebir & Oishi 2008 [NEW, early; likely ⚑].** In a two-week daily diary study, "people reported greater life satisfaction and fewer physical symptoms on days in which they felt more understood by others." The link was **stronger for women and for people high in interdependent self-construal**. Sample size and country were not shown on the page. — [Lun et al. 2008 (London Business School)](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/o/on-feeling-understood-and-feeling-well-the-role-of-interdependence)
- **Kwang & Swann 2010 [NEW, strong with caveats; ⚑ mostly lab studies].** A meta-analytic review of about 38 critical tests of self-verification versus self-enhancement:
  - **Cognitive responses** (judging accuracy, attributions): self-verification r≈.30 vs self-enhancement r≈.18.
  - **Affective responses:** self-enhancement r≈.29 vs self-verification r≈.13. We *feel* better after praise.
  - **Actual feedback choices:** verification r≈.32 vs enhancement .18. **Stated preferences** favoured enhancement (.41 vs .27).
  - **High rejection risk** (dating, global evaluations): enhancement r≈.31 vs verification .08.
  - **Low rejection risk** (established marriages): verification r≈.19 vs enhancement .05. Marital intimacy and divorce findings followed this pattern.
  - Negative evaluations are "unwelcome, even if they contain a kernel of truth" when rejection looms.
  - About 70% of people hold positive self-views, so many apparent "self-enhancement" results may actually be self-verification.
  - — [Kwang & Swann 2010 PDF](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/swann/files/2016/10/kwangSwann2010.pdf)
- **Dweck 2017 [early/theory].** Acceptance is one of three inborn needs; **predictability** is another. — [APA on Dweck 2017](https://www.apa.org/pubs/highlights/spotlight/issue-111.html)
- **Sheldon et al. 2001.** Self-esteem was among the top-ranked needs in satisfying events. — [Sheldon 2001](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/What-Is-Satisfying-About-Satisfying-Events/991031600454702976)
- **Context for the app.** Feeling understood links to respect and to belonging: respect was the top predictor of daily positive feelings worldwide. — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)

### Inferences
- Why people value being known accurately, even about negative traits: accurate self-knowledge makes the social world **predictable** (Dweck's predictability need). In committed, low-risk relationships, accuracy signals that one is truly known and still accepted (Kwang & Swann's marriage findings). The coherence and predictability explanation is the usual account of self-verification, but it was **not stated** in the Kwang & Swann text I opened. Treat it as inference.
- A private, non-comparative app is closer to a "low rejection risk" setting. That is the condition in which people welcome accurate, even unflattering, feedback. Feelings still respond more to praise, so **accurate and warm** framing is the honest middle ground.
- Interdependent people and women may gain more daily well-being from feeling understood (Lun 2008). This is early evidence, but it suggests "being understood" insights may matter more to some users.

### Gaps
- I could not open the perceived-responsiveness literature directly: the PMC pages hit a CAPTCHA. That includes Reis's responsiveness model, studies linking responsiveness to health and longevity, and neural "feeling understood" studies. Gable et al. 2004 (responses to good news) is in the library as gable2004 but was not re-opened.
- No cross-cultural test of self-verification was found within budget.

## 4. How do personality traits relate to which needs dominate and how they are met?

### Takeaway
Personality shapes **how much** need satisfaction people report and **which values they prioritize**, but the effects are small to moderate. Higher neuroticism goes with less satisfaction of all three core needs; extraversion, openness, agreeableness and conscientiousness go with more. Insecure attachment, whether anxious or avoidant, goes with lower need satisfaction, which in turn explains lower life satisfaction. Traits and values are related but distinct, and links weaken in threatening environments. Low Honesty-Humility is, by definition, a desire to display wealth, privilege and status.

### Cited Findings
- **Vukasović Hlupić et al. 2023 [NEW, moderate].** Meta-analysis of 16 studies, 17 samples, 206 effect sizes and more than 10,500 participants. "All three needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) had negative correlations with neuroticism, and positive correlations with extraversion, openness, agreeableness, and conscientiousness." All 15 pooled correlations were significant and "small to moderate." Heterogeneity was large, and there were too few studies for moderator analyses by culture, age or gender. Exact coefficients were not visible on the page. — [Vukasović Hlupić et al. 2023, Current Psychology](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s12144-022-04158-9)
- **Chen, Khir et al. 2026 [NEW, early; non-WEIRD].** 488 Chinese university students. Attachment anxiety and avoidance were each negatively associated with autonomy, competence and relatedness satisfaction (βs −0.25 to −0.51). Need satisfaction fully mediated the link from attachment insecurity to life satisfaction (indirect β −0.33 for anxiety, −0.25 for avoidance; the model explained 49.2% of variance). The design was cross-sectional self-report. — [Chen et al. 2026, IJMHP](https://www.techscience.com/IJMHP/v28n7/68247)
- **Parks-Leduc, Feldman & Bardi 2015 [NEW, strong].** Meta-analysis of 60 studies linking the Big Five to Schwartz values. The relations were "consistent, theoretically meaningful" but "not generally large," so **traits and values are distinct**. More cognitively based traits relate more strongly to values; emotionally based traits relate less strongly. — [Parks-Leduc et al. 2015 (Royal Holloway)](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/personality-traits-and-personal-values-a-meta-analysis/)
- **Fischer & Boer 2015 (online 2014) [NEW, strong; 14 countries].** N=9,935. The strongest links were **Openness with the Conservation value dimension** (security, conformity, tradition) and **Agreeableness with Self-Transcendence** (benevolence, universalism). Most trait–value links, except those for Extraversion, were **weaker in contexts with more financial, ecological or social threat**. Context explained more than 10% of the variance in the correlations. — [Fischer & Boer 2015 (rlp-forschung record)](https://www.rlp-forschung.de/public/publications/106634)
- **HEXACO-PI-R scale descriptions [NEW, descriptive].** Low Honesty-Humility scorers are "motivated by material gain" and "feel a strong sense of self-importance." Low Greed Avoidance scorers "want to enjoy and to display wealth and privilege." Low Modesty scorers see themselves "as superior and as entitled to privileges." High Emotionality scorers are "strongly inclined to avoid physical harm" (Fearfulness), "become preoccupied even by relatively minor problems" (Anxiety) and "want to share their difficulties with those who will provide encouragement and comfort" (Dependence). Low Social Self-Esteem (an Extraversion facet) means "a sense of personal worthlessness." — [HEXACO scale descriptions](https://hexaco.org/scaledescriptions)
- **Need strength vs benefit.** Individual differences in how much a person desires a need barely change how much they benefit from it. — [Chen et al. 2015](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf); [VR&S 2020](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf). VR&S note that people "may have been differently (de)sensitized" to need satisfaction through personality and history.
- **Dweck 2017.** Temperament, such as shyness or fearfulness, strengthens certain needs and shapes the beliefs (BEATs) through which they are pursued. For example, people who see ability as fixed avoid challenges that might expose a weakness. — [APA on Dweck 2017](https://www.apa.org/pubs/highlights/spotlight/issue-111.html)
- **Kruglanski et al. 2022.** The *means* of seeking significance depend on values and culture. — [Kruglanski et al. 2022](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/xmlui/handle/item/311546)

### Inferences
- **Neuroticism / Emotionality.** Expect more salience of **security and predictability**, more reported frustration, and a stronger pull toward comfort-seeking (Dependence). Only the direction for need satisfaction is meta-analytic; the security link is inference from the HEXACO descriptions and from VR&S's "security as substitute."
- **Extraversion.** Expect easier satisfaction of relatedness and more status-seeking through social visibility. The status part is inference.
- **Openness.** Expect stronger valuing of freedom, novelty and self-direction, and weaker valuing of security and tradition. Fischer & Boer report the Openness–Conservation link as the strongest, but the sign was not shown on the page I opened. A negative sign is implied by Schwartz's circular model, in which Conservation is the opposite pole to Openness to Change. Treat the sign as inference.
- **Agreeableness.** Expect stronger valuing of belonging, benevolence and harmony.
- **Low Honesty-Humility.** Expect the "success" craving to take the form of visible wealth, status and privilege. High H-H people likely meet "success" through competence and fairness. This is inference from the scale definitions, not a tested need study.
- **Attachment anxiety.** Likely heightens the craving to be understood and reassured. **Avoidance** likely heightens the craving for autonomy while lowering satisfaction of relatedness. Only the overall negative link with need satisfaction is evidenced (one Chinese sample).
- **The app's frame.** Personality changes *which craving is loudest* and *which route a person takes*, not *whether* the craving matters. The app should never tell someone they "don't need" connection or freedom.

### Gaps
- I could not see exact pooled coefficients (trait × need) from Vukasović Hlupić 2023, or any results for need *frustration* by trait.
- No meta-analysis linking HEXACO Honesty-Humility to need satisfaction or status-seeking was opened. Searches returned only general pages.
- Attachment evidence here rests on one cross-sectional Chinese student study. La Guardia et al. 2000 and other within-person attachment and needs work were not opened.

## 5. What happens when these cravings are frustrated?

### Takeaway
Active **frustration** of a need (being controlled, rejected or made to feel incompetent) is distinct from merely lacking satisfaction. It predicts ill-being, especially depressive symptoms, across cultures. People then tend to compensate: rigid behaviour, contingent self-worth, defiance, chasing substitutes (money, image, security), or seeking significance through extreme routes. Losing status or respect lowers well-being and health.

### Cited Findings
- **Chen et al. 2015 [moderate; 4 countries].** Frustration of each need uniquely predicted **depressive symptoms**. **Relatedness frustration** was strongest (b=.35), then autonomy (b=.26) and competence (b=.17). The paths did not differ across Belgium, China, the USA and Peru, and people low in desire for a need were not protected (no moderation). — [Chen et al. 2015](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf)
- **VR&S 2020 [strong for the distinction].** Frustration "involves an active threat of the psychological needs (rather than a mere absence of need satisfaction)," and "the absence of need satisfaction does not necessarily imply the presence of need frustration." Frustration "predicted incremental variance in maladjustment above and beyond low need satisfaction" (Bartholomew et al. 2011). Consequences include:
  - amotivation, disengagement, stress, depressive symptoms and anxiety
  - compensation by "developing rigid behavior patterns" and contingent self-worth
  - oppositional defiance; "defiant adolescents become increasingly alienated from their personal preferences and interests"
  - need substitutes such as extrinsic aspirations and security-seeking ("need sacrificing")

  — [VR&S 2020](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf)
- **Nateghi & Nemattavousi 2019 [NEW, early; non-WEIRD].** 464 Iranian university students: "needs frustration had direct and negative effect on the psychological well-being," partly through self-esteem and self-regulation. — [Nateghi & Nemattavousi 2019 (ensani.ir)](https://ensani.ir/fa/article/download/416286)
- **Belonging deprivation.** It is linked to higher mortality, mental illness, crime and suicide in the data Baumeister & Leary reviewed. "Unsatisfied needs should lead to pathology (medical, psychological, or behavioral), unlike unsatisfied wants." The evidence is correlational. — [Baumeister & Leary 1995](https://www.hendrix.edu/uploadedFiles/Academics/Faculty_Resources/2016_FFC/Baumeister%20and%20Leary%20(1995).pdf)
- **Status loss.**
  - MBA graduates whose status fell after graduation saw their well-being fall.
  - Lower community-ladder status was linked to depression, anxiety, cardiovascular risk and susceptibility to infection; status explained "an additional 9.5% of the variance in depressive symptoms" beyond demographics.
  - Deferential behaviour was linked to depression, anxiety and shame. "Low status seemed uniquely harmful to health."
  - — [Anderson et al. 2015](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP387/Desire%20for%20Status%2C%20Human%20Motive%2C%20APA.pdf)
- **Unmet respect and autonomy** were the top predictors of **negative feelings** worldwide (25% and 22% of explained variance), along with unmet basic needs (23%). — [Tay & Diener 2011](http://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-101-2-354.pdf)
- **Significance loss** activates the quest for significance. Experiments show loss of significance reduces meaning in life. The quest correlates with sacrificial behaviour and extreme political attitudes. — [Kruglanski et al. 2022](https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/xmlui/handle/item/311546); [Alsaadi et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1513720/full)

### Inferences
- In everyday terms, frustration shows up as feeling **controlled** (autonomy), **left out or misunderstood** (relatedness), **useless or failing** (competence), or **disrespected** (status). Across four countries, being left out or rejected had the largest link to low mood.
- The cravings for wealth, image and extra security can be *symptoms* of frustrated core needs rather than root desires (VR&S). An app could gently reflect "what this might be standing in for" without moralizing. This is inference; it should be offered as a question, not a verdict.

### Gaps
- Most frustration evidence is cross-sectional. Longitudinal and experimental cross-cultural tests were not opened within budget.
- I opened no direct evidence on frustration of hope or of peace specifically.

## What this means for the app (situations, honest insights, what to avoid)

### Takeaway
Use concrete everyday choices that pit cravings against each other (freedom vs security, being liked vs being seen accurately, calm vs excitement, competence vs recognition). Because the core needs are universal, the honest insight is about **which craving was loudest in that moment and how the person tried to meet it**, never "you need X and others don't." Use accurate, warm language. Never diagnose. Watch for repeated frustration signals, especially of belonging.

### Cited Findings (design implications tied to evidence)
- Needs are universal and their benefits are not moderated by how much people say they want them, so the app should describe **salience and routes**, not "needing less." — [Chen et al. 2015](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf); [VR&S 2020](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/2020_VansteenkisteRyanSoenens_BPNSIntro_MOEM.pdf)
- **Autonomy is about volition, not independence.** Doing things for family can be fully autonomous if the person endorses it, so ask *why* someone chose an option, not only *what* they chose. — [Chirkov et al. 2003](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Differentiating-Autonomy-From-Individualism-and-Independence/991031598656802976)
- In a low-rejection-risk setting, people choose and trust accurate feedback, while their feelings respond more to praise. Polite naming of negative traits fits this, if paired with acceptance. — [Kwang & Swann 2010](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/swann/files/2016/10/kwangSwann2010.pdf)
- Frustration, especially relatedness frustration, predicts depressive symptoms across cultures, so recurring answers about rejection or being controlled deserve care, not clever insight. — [Chen et al. 2015](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/2014_Chen-et-al._need-satisfaction.pdf)
- Status works relative to people nearby, and status loss harms health. An app that never compares people with others avoids feeding this craving. — [Anderson et al. 2015](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP387/Desire%20for%20Status%2C%20Human%20Motive%2C%20APA.pdf)
- Culture shapes which feelings people *want* (calm vs excitement) more than the feelings they have. Neither should be framed as the better one. — [Tsai et al. 2025](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/d4fbbc23-48dc-4d47-8232-efe6fe572280); [Tsai et al. 2007](https://culture-emotion-lab.stanford.edu/sites/culture_emotion_lab/files/media/file/tsai_miao_seppala_fung_yeung_2007.pdf)
- Value priorities shift with age (security and tradition up, stimulation down) and differ slightly by gender, but culture and individual variation matter far more. The app should not tune insights by demographics. — [Robinson 2012/13](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/8968/); [Schwartz & Rubel 2005](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/sex-differences-in-value-priorities-cross-cultural-and-multimetho/)

### Inferences

**Everyday situations that reveal each craving.** All of these are concrete options with no scales. Each is my design suggestion, grounded in the constructs above.

| Craving | Example situation (morning or evening) | Options that reveal it |
|---|---|---|
| **Freedom (autonomy)** | "Your evening suddenly frees up." | Plan it fully / leave it open / say yes to whoever asks first / catch up on duties. Follow-up on why: "because I wanted to" / "I'd feel guilty otherwise" / "it was expected" (relative autonomy, per Chirkov). |
| **Security / predictability** | "A plan you counted on changes an hour before." | Re-plan at once / feel relieved / go with it / feel a bit lost and wait. Or: "Payday is a week away; you…" keep a buffer / treat yourself / don't think about it. |
| **Hope** | "Thinking about next month, what comes to mind first?" | Something to look forward to / something to get through / nothing specific / something I'm working toward. |
| **Success: competence** | "You finish a hard task. What feels best?" | That I did it well / that someone noticed / that it's off my list / that it helped someone. This separates competence, recognition, relief and benevolence. |
| **Respect / status** | "Someone else gets credit for your idea in a meeting or family chat." | Say something now / let it go / mention it privately later / feel it sting but stay quiet. |
| **Belonging / love** | "Lunch break with a free hour." | Eat with others / call someone / eat alone and enjoy it / work through it. |
| **Being understood** | "A friend asks 'how are you really?'" | Tell the real story / say "fine" / turn it back to them / tell part of it. Or: "Which would mean more today?" Someone praising you / someone describing you exactly right, flaws included. |
| **Meaning** | "Evening: what made today worth it?" | Someone I helped / something I learned / something I finished / it was just a day to get through. |
| **Peace (calm vs excitement)** | "Your ideal Saturday morning." | Slow and quiet / out and lively / productive / with people at home. |

**What an insight can honestly say**
- After **one answer**, describe the moment: "This morning, keeping things predictable mattered more to you than keeping options open." Do not assign a trait ("You are a security-seeker").
- In **weekly and monthly mirrors**, describe frequencies and contrasts: "On 5 of 7 evenings, the best part of your day involved someone else." "When plans changed, you usually re-planned straight away." Then link gently to a construct: "That often points to a strong pull toward predictability."
- **Name the route, not just the craving.** For example: "You often meet the wish to feel respected by doing things well, not by being noticed." (Competence route vs status route, per Sheldon and Anderson.)
- **Negative traits, politely and accurately.** For example: "When someone else got credit, you tended to stay quiet and let it sting. Many people do this to keep the peace; it can also mean your contribution goes unseen." This is accurate, warm and non-judgmental. Do not reframe every flaw as a hidden strength; that is flattery.
- **Possible substitutes, as a question.** For example: "Lately you've picked the money or safety option a lot. Is something else feeling uncertain right now?" Never assert it (VR&S need-substitute idea).

**What to avoid**
- **Overclaiming.**
  - Don't present a single answer as a trait.
  - Don't claim a craving is unusual or rare: the app doesn't compare people, and all these needs are universal.
  - Don't present theory-level ideas as fact. Kenrick's pyramid, Dweck's needs and significance-quest theory are early or theoretical.
  - Don't say "you don't need people" or "you don't need freedom," even if answers suggest low interest. Need strength doesn't moderate benefit.
- **Flattery.** People's feelings like praise, but their choices favour accuracy (Kwang & Swann). Vague, universally flattering statements would undermine trust. I opened no source on "Barnum" effects this session, so treat this as a design inference.
- **Status framing.** Avoid rankings, percentiles or "top X%" language, and avoid treating wealth, fame or power as markers of success. Power is the lowest-ranked value worldwide, and extrinsic goals relate to lower need satisfaction.
- **Cultural bias.** Don't treat independence as healthier than interdependence, or excitement as better than calm. Autonomy can be met through chosen duty (Chirkov); calm is a valid ideal (Tsai).
- **Harm to vulnerable people.**
  - Repeated answers signalling rejection, being controlled, worthlessness or hopelessness are associated with depressive symptoms (Chen 2015; Anderson 2015). The app should stop offering clever insights and give a gentle, non-diagnostic note pointing to real-world support.
  - Never use clinical labels ("depressed," "anxious attachment," "narcissistic") or say it is therapy.
  - For hope questions, avoid forcing optimism. Hope varies hugely by circumstance (unemployment, country), and a person's low hope may reflect real hardship, not a flaw.

### Gaps
- I found no study testing whether *situational-choice* questions, as opposed to rating scales, validly measure need salience. This would need the app's own validation.
- No evidence was opened on the effects of automated personal insights or self-tracking feedback on vulnerable users. Other researchers may cover this.
- How weekly and monthly patterns in such answers relate to stable traits is untested here. Mirrors should describe patterns, not personality.
